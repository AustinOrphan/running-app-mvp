# Novice runners, run-walk and return to running

## What is established

**Novices carry the most risk, and having no running history is the clearest single marker.** Pooled incidence is 17.8 vs 7.7 injuries per 1000 h in novice vs recreational runners [videbaek-2015-incidence]. Across 36 prospective studies the pooled novice incidence was 14.9%, with a very wide range [fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review]. In an 8-week event cohort, having no previous running experience roughly doubled the hazard (HR 2.6 in men, 2.1 in women) [buist-2010-rri-risk-factors-4-mile]. Injury comes early: 10.9% of runners were injured in a 6-week programme [kluitenberg-2015-nlstart2run] and about 20% within 8-13 weeks [buist-2008-graded-training]. Injury was also the main reason novices stopped running: 48% of the 29.5% who quit within 26 weeks [fokkema-2019-discontinuation-start-to-run]. Avoiding an early injury therefore matters more than reaching a target dose quickly. Risk accrues per hour of exposure, so the engine controls time on feet, not only distance.

**The inputs that should set the start are known, but their sizes are not.** A GRADE-rated review of 29 prospective cohorts looked at short-distance runners and long-distance runners separately [vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review]:

- In short-distance runners, previous injuries not caused by running were the strongest factor (high quality). Higher BMI, higher age, male sex, no running experience and lower volume were moderate-quality factors.
- In long-distance runners, a previous running injury was the strongest factor.

This matches the NLstart2run factors [kluitenberg-2015-nlstart2run]. Another novice cohort found weaker links [nielsen-2013-novice-predictors]. In a 9-week Couch-to-5K cohort, a previous injury raised the odds of a new one (OR 7.56, 95% CI 2.06-27.75) [relph-2023-couch-to-5k-injury]. Risk models built from these factors predict poorly. They can tell the engine which way to adjust, but not by how much.

**Heavier novices need a smaller first week, and they do not choose one on their own.**

- In an exploratory cohort, runners with BMI over 30 who ran 3 km or more in week 1 had a cumulative injury risk 14-16 percentage points higher than the low-BMI, low-distance group [nielsen-2014-first-week-distance-obese].
- An RCT in runners with BMI 30-35 compared 3 × 1 km with 3 × 2 km per week. The shorter start did better: -16.3% by intention to treat (not significant) and -31.2% per protocol [bertelsen-2018-start-to-run-distance-rct].
- Obese novices chose the same first-week distance and duration as normal-weight novices, only at a slower pace [bertelsen-2018-bmi-self-chosen-start].

**Weekly progression rules have no outcome support. The session spike does.**

- A 13-week programme built on the 10% rule had the same injury rate as a standard 8-week programme (20.8% vs 20.3%) [buist-2008-graded-training].
- Reviews find the evidence on distance, duration, frequency, intensity and progression conflicting [fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review][nielsen-2012-training-errors].
- In 5205 runners, the week-to-week ratio showed no association with injury. A single session longer than 1.10× the longest run of the previous 30 days did [frandsen-2025-session-spike].

**Keep the early weeks easy.** In 1696 novices, a higher RPE over the previous week was the clearest time-varying injury signal. Running 3 days a week rather than 2 showed only a non-significant trend toward more risk. Lower volume went with more injury, which is probably confounded, and the authors say not to use it as guidance [kluitenberg-2016-nlstart2run-training-factors].

**Other established points carried over from before:**

- Hip and core work before runs lowered injury in novices (HR 0.66) [leppanen-2024-hip-core-novice].
- A 4-week walking and hopping block before running did not (15.2% vs 16.8%) [bredeweg-2012-preconditioning-novice-rct].
- Detraining is fast at first and then levels off [coyle-1984-detraining-time-course][zheng-2022-detraining-vo2max][mujika-2000-detraining-part1][mujika-2000-detraining-part2].
- Fitness can be maintained on about two sessions a week [hickson-1981-reduced-frequency][spiering-2021-minimal-dose].
- Return from bone stress injury depends on the site (44-127 days) [hoenig-2023-bsi-return-to-sport].

## What is contested

**Run-walk versus continuous running.** No study compares a run-walk start with 3 × 20 or 3 × 30 minutes of continuous running.

- Linton found that first-year runners on a structured programme such as Couch-to-5K were less often injured than those on a self-made plan, but the design was retrospective and cross-sectional [linton-2018-uk-novice-recreational-injury].
- Relph found 21 injuries in 110 runners (19%) and only 27.3% completion on a modified Couch-to-5K [relph-2023-couch-to-5k-injury]. A run-walk ladder is therefore not proven safe.
- The published plans agree on the shape:
  - NHS Couch-to-5K starts at 8 min of running per session, gives the first continuous 20 min in week 5 and reaches 3 × 30 min in week 9 [nhs-2026-couch-to-5k].
  - The original Couch-to-5K has the same shape [clark-2026-couch-to-5k-original].
  - Galloway starts lower still, at 5 s of running per minute [galloway-2026-beginner-run-walk].
  - All three let the runner repeat a week.
- None of these plans start a true novice at 3 × 20 min continuous. They are conventions (grade D).

**3 × 20 versus 3 × 30 minutes.** In a 1977 trial, 15-min and 30-min sessions three times a week had similar injury rates (22% and 24%), while 45 min reached 54%. Five days a week reached 39%, against 12% for three days [pollock-1977-frequency-duration-injury]. Those subjects were young men jogging at 85-90% of maximum heart rate, and 20 min was not tested, so this is direction only.

**Progression and risk factors.** Older disagreements remain:

- The 30%-over-two-weeks signal was not significant [nielsen-2014-weekly-progression].
- Comparing intensity-led and volume-led progression gave no clear answer [ramskov-2018-run-clever].
- Linton reports one counter-intuitive odds ratio [linton-2018-uk-novice-recreational-injury].
- The exact point where detraining levels off is unsettled.

## What is unknown

- Whether run-walk lowers injury compared with short continuous runs, and which run:walk ratio is best.
- How much age, BMI or injury history should change the starting dose. Only the BMI first-week distance has trial data, and even that is underpowered.
- How self-rated physical function, which predicts dropout [fokkema-2019-discontinuation-start-to-run], should change the plan.
- Return-to-run intervals for injuries other than bone stress injury. The available protocols are clinical conventions [sanford-2024-return-to-running][bjsm-2019-bone-stress-return-to-run] or level IV expert opinion [george-2024-tibial-bsi-return-to-run].
- How fast fitness comes back after a layoff, and anything specific to ultras. The tissue side is no better known: tendon stiffness fell within 2 to 3 weeks of complete limb unloading, and a gain built over 3 months was gone after 1 month of detraining, in young non-runners [deboer-2007-ulls-tendon-time-course] [kubo-2012-achilles-training-detraining]; no study gives a retraining rate for runners (see the injury-load topic).

## How the engine should use this

The start rules apply when the runner's typical recent week is under 3 x 20 min, or when there are no logged runs.

1. **Choose the start path from history, not from one number.**
   - A true novice (experience novice with no run of 20 min or more ever logged, or no runs at all and no declared longest run) gets a 9-rung run-walk ladder.
   - A returner who has completed a continuous run of 20 min or more in the last 30 days gets the continuous floor, 3 x N min.
   - A returner without such a run but with a run in the last 90 days is judged by a reference run: the longest run of the last 30 days when that window holds any run, else the J7 fraction (by layoff length) times the longest run of the last 90 days. If the reference is 20 min or more, the continuous floor at N = 20 applies. If it is under 20 min, the runner enters the ladder at the highest rung whose longest running bout is at most 1.10 x the reference.
   - A runner with no run in 90 days: an experienced runner (trained or recreational experience, 2 or more years of running, no soft flag) gets the continuous floor at N = 20. Everyone else starts at ladder rung 1. A declared longest run counts as the 30-day longest run (A3) where the engine supports it.
   - A runner with any injury listed in the profile for the last 12 months (running-related or not) and a typical week under 60 min, or no runs, gets the injury-return ladder. A recent bone stress injury takes the same ladder today; the n1 walk-jog stages and the walk-and-hop gate are design intent, not implemented.

   Sex never changes the dose (G-4).

2. **The ladder (grade D, adapted from the NHS and original Couch-to-5K plans).**
   - 3 non-consecutive days a week, with a 5-min walk before and after each session; the walks are not counted in the week.
   - Rung 1 keeps the engine's 1:2 ratio (1 min run, 2 min walk, 7 repeats, so 7 min of running). This is slightly gentler than the NHS week 1.
   - Later rungs follow the NHS run-interval shape. The first continuous 20 min comes at rung 5, then 22, 25 and 28 min, and 3 x 30 min at rung 9.
   - One rung per plan week. A rung advances only after at least 80% of the week's planned minutes with no pain flag; otherwise it is repeated, and a runner may hold any rung. A mean session RPE of 5/10 or more also holds the rung (direction grade C, the 5/10 value D). Without ratings the ladder advances on completion and pain reports alone.
   - No gentler "rung 0" is offered; it would be a speculative default.
3. **Continuous floor for returners.** Three sessions of N min, where N is the longest continuous run of the last 30 days rounded down to 5 min and kept between 20 and 30. N above 20 needs at least 2 years of running (a5.trainedMinYears), no soft flag and no recent-injury flag; otherwise N = 20. A floor session never exceeds the runner's own completed run, so G-1 stays inside its reference band.
4. **Limits during the start.** Every ladder and floor session obeys G-1 as a hard 1.10 x limit on whole-session distance and duration, walk blocks included. The ladder and floor weeks are exempt from the G-2 soft 10% warning only (a fixed convention programme), never from the 30% over 2 weeks hard ceiling. The injury-return weeks (G-3) are exempt from the weekly ramp in full, with every run held to the hard 1.10 x limit. A tightened profile without a start path gets no exemption. Weekly time on feet in the ladder is about 63, 63, 54, 64.5, 62, 69, 75, 84 and 90 min, so the ceiling is not reached.
5. **BMI over 30** (height and weight in the profile). Hold rung 1 for weeks 1-3 and advance in week 4. This copies the tested arm of one trial whose intention-to-treat result was not significant, so it carries grade C and low confidence.
6. **Soft flags.** The flags are age 45 or over (no upper limit), BMI over 30, any injury in the last 12 months (running-related or not), no running history, and under 2 years of running. Any flag removes the 30-min option. The 2:1 recovery cadence comes from the novice experience class, the recent-injury flag, the low-frequency profile or the recovery-cycle option, not from these flags.
7. **Intensity.** Ladder, floor and injury-return weeks are all easy, with no quality sessions or strides during the ladder and for 2 weeks after it ends.
8. **No preconditioning block.** Do not add a walking-only block before running for injury prevention. Keep the hip and core block.
9. **Label honestly.** Every rung, ratio and floor value is labelled a default. The app does not claim run-walk prevents injury.

## Gaps

- No trial compares a run-walk start with 3 x 20 or 3 x 30 min of continuous running for novices or returners, so the choice of path rests on conventions plus risk-factor direction.
- No evidence gives the size of a dose adjustment for age, prior injury or years of running; only BMI over 30 has a first-week distance (under 3 km), and that RCT's primary ITT test was null and underpowered (n=56).
- No evidence on 20-min sessions specifically: Pollock 1977 tested 15, 30 and 45 min at 85-90% HRmax in young men; nothing at easy pace or in women or older adults.
- The BMI start rule holds rung 1 for 3 weeks; the trial dose was a distance (under 3 km a week), and no paper converts it to run-walk minutes.
- Self-rated physical function (RAND-36) predicts dropout but is not collected and no paper links it to a dose.
- Return-to-run evidence for non-BSI injuries (tendinopathy, patellofemoral pain, muscle strain, post-surgery) is clinical convention only; the engine's recent-injury flag does not record injury type, so one generic ladder is an extrapolation.
- How G-1 should treat run-walk sessions (total session distance vs longest continuous bout) is not addressed by Frandsen 2025, which used GPS session distance without walk breaks reported.
- No evidence on the right G-1 reference when the 30-day window is empty after a long layoff.
- Couch-to-5K completion and injury data come from one small UK cohort (n=110, 27.3% completion); no data on hold-or-repeat ladders vs fixed ladders.
- Sex, menstrual status, footwear and surface as moderators of the start dose were not searched; sex is excluded from dosing by G-4.
- Retraining speed after a layoff and ultra-specific return guidance remain missing (unchanged from the previous synthesis).
