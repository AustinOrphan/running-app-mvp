# Novice runners, run-walk and return to running

## What is established

**Novices are the high-risk group, and the risk is front-loaded.** Pooled injury incidence is 17.8 per 1000 h in novices vs 7.7 per 1000 h in recreational runners, a ratio of about 2.3 [videbaek-2015-incidence]. In supervised starter programs roughly 11% of runners were injured within 6 weeks [kluitenberg-2015-nlstart2run] and about 20% within 8-13 weeks [buist-2008-graded-training]. Risk is per hour of exposure, so the engine should control running time, not only distance.

**Hip and core strength work is the only intervention in this topic with a positive RCT for novices.** Done before runs, it lowered lower-extremity injury (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.45-0.97). Ankle/foot-only exercises did not help [leppanen-2024-hip-core-novice].

**Detraining is fast early and then plateaus.** In trained subjects VO2max fell 7% by day 21 and about 16% by day 56, stabilising above sedentary levels [coyle-1984-detraining-time-course]. The 2022 meta-analysis found larger loss for long than short cessation but no significant additional loss beyond about 30 days [zheng-2022-detraining-vo2max]. Recently acquired gains are lost completely, while long-trained athletes keep part of their fitness; strength decays slowly [mujika-2000-detraining-part1][mujika-2000-detraining-part2].

**Fitness can be maintained on far less training than it took to build.** Two sessions per week at unchanged intensity preserved VO2max for 15 weeks in young adults [hickson-1981-reduced-frequency]. The review synthesis adds 33-66% volume cuts, with intensity the key variable [spiering-2021-minimal-dose].

**Bone stress injury return timelines depend on site.** Pooled return to sport ranged from 44 days (posteromedial tibia) to 127 days (tarsal navicular), with higher complication rates at the femoral neck, navicular, anterior tibia and fifth metatarsal [hoenig-2023-bsi-return-to-sport].

## What is contested

**Progression rules.** The 10% rule did not reduce injuries in an RCT [buist-2008-graded-training]. In a cohort, progression over 30% in 2 weeks showed a distance-related-injury signal that was not significant (HR 1.59, P = .07) and no overall difference [nielsen-2014-weekly-progression]. A systematic review could not identify which training errors cause injury [nielsen-2012-training-errors]. Intensity vs volume progression showed no differential injury pattern, with wide intervals [ramskov-2018-run-clever].

**Individual risk factors.** Higher age, BMI, prior non-sport complaints and no running experience were associated with injury in one cohort [kluitenberg-2015-nlstart2run]. In another, associations were borderline and running experience was not predictive [nielsen-2013-novice-predictors]. Treat these as soft flags.

**Detraining magnitude and timing.** Coyle shows decline still continuing from day 21 to day 56 (n = 7); the meta-analysis says no significant change beyond 30 days, with high heterogeneity and small-study bias for long-term cessation. The plateau shape is consistent; the exact knee is not.

## What is unknown

- No trial shows run-walk reduces injury in novices, and no evidence identifies an optimal run:walk ratio. The only run-walk trial here is a 42-person marathon study showing similar finish time and less muscle discomfort, but no cardiac benefit [hottenrott-2016-run-walk-marathon]. Nothing covers ultra distances.
- Return-to-run evidence after injury is level IV expert opinion with no interval durations [george-2024-tibial-bsi-return-to-run], and only for tibial BSI.
- No paper in this corpus quantifies how fast fitness returns (retraining rate) in runners.
- Strength dose for hip and core work is not in the abstract.

## How the engine should use this

1. Classify every plan start as novice, returning (by layoff length and training history), maintenance, or post-injury, and apply different caps.
2. For novices use time-based progression, three non-consecutive run days, walk intervals early, and a hip/core block before runs. State in the UI that the 10% and 30% numbers are conservative conventions, not proven.
3. Model layoff with a saturating curve (7% at 3 weeks, up to about 16% at 8 weeks, cap about 15-20% for long-trained athletes), scaled up for higher VO2max and under-20s, and drop it to zero retention for runners with only recent gains. After a break, re-estimate paces from fresh data instead of using pre-break values.
4. Offer maintenance mode (2 runs per week, 33-66% less volume, intensity kept, at most about 15 weeks) with a visible caveat that ultra durability is not covered.
5. For logged bone stress injuries, require a clinician-cleared date for high-risk sites and block automatic plan generation until it exists. Use pooled durations only as minimum planning intervals.
6. Where a number has no evidence (walk ratios, restart fractions, strength dose), ship a conservative default labelled grade D and make it configurable.

## Gaps

- No trial tests whether run-walk reduces injury in novices, and no evidence identifies the best run:walk ratio, the length of the run-walk phase, or when to progress to continuous running.
- No ultra-distance (50 km to 100 mile) evidence on walk strategies, maintenance of ultra-specific durability, or minimum dose; the only run-walk trial is a 42-person marathon study.
- Retraining speed is missing: no verified paper here quantifies how fast VO2max, threshold pace or running economy return after a layoff in runners.
- Detraining magnitudes come from 7-subject (Coyle) and non-run-specific studies; Coyle (still falling to day 56) and Zheng (no change beyond 30 days) are in tension, and effects in older runners, women and recreational runners are not quantified.
- Return-to-run guidance beyond tibial bone stress injury (tendinopathy, patellofemoral pain, muscle strain, post-surgery) is not covered, and no interval durations or step sizes exist even for tibial BSI.
- Hip and core strength dose (sets, reps, frequency) is not stated in the Run RCT abstract; the trial was supervised.
- Evidence for the 10% and 30% progression ceilings is weak; no RCT tests a 30%-per-2-weeks cap, and whether time-based progression is safer than distance-based is untested.
- Restart-volume fractions after layoffs and BSI-based exposure caps have no direct evidence and are engineering defaults.
- Moderators such as sex, menstrual status, footwear, terrain and heat on novice injury risk were not searched here; Videbaek's per-hour rates mix heterogeneous injury definitions.
