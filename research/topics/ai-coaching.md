# Algorithmic and data-driven training planning

## What is established

**Individuals do not respond identically, and individual response is hard to measure.** Responses to the same standardized training vary widely [mann-2014-high-low-responders]. A 21-person crossover found that individual responses to endurance and sprint-interval training were uncorrelated, and everyone responded to at least one stimulus [bonafiglia-2016-interval-response-variability]. Measurement error and within-person variation mean that a single test or block cannot reliably classify someone as a "low responder" [hecksteden-2015-individual-response]. Evidence level is B at best, and it is almost all cycling or lab work, not running plans.

**Fitted fatigue models are poorly identified.** The Banister fitness-fatigue model fits training and performance well, but its time constants have very wide bootstrap intervals and correlated parameters [hellard-2006-banister-limitations]. This was shown in 9 elite swimmers, not runners, but it is the only identifiability evidence in the corpus. It argues against per-athlete fitting of CTL/ATL-style constants.

**Wearable fitness estimates are noisy at the individual level.** Exercise-based VO2max estimates carry limits of agreement of about plus or minus 10 ml/kg/min; resting-based estimates are biased upward [molinagarcia-2022-wearable-vo2max] (grade A).

**LLM-generated plans are not reliable prescribers.** Coaching experts rated ChatGPT plans sub-optimal, with quality rising as input detail rose [duking-2024-chatgpt-running-plans]. Eight 2024-25 era models were inconsistent on mileage and pacing and blurred experience levels [montaruli-2026-ai-marathon-programs]. Both studies are small, descriptive and subjective, but they point the same way.

**Race-result and wearable data support individualized performance modelling.** Personal-exponent or two-parameter models fitted to a runner's own data predict race times better than fixed formulas [blythe-2016-runner-performance-prediction, emig-2020-running-big-data]. Case-based and recommender approaches show that predicting finish time and recommending plans from similar runners is feasible [feely-2020-cbr-marathon-plans, berndsen-2019-pace-my-race, smyth-2021-marathon-recsys]. None of these report outcome trials, and the abstracts give few error figures.

## What is contested

**ACWR.** Observational syntheses support an association between ACWR and non-contact injury in team sports, with EWMA preferred [griffin-2020-acwr-systematic-review]. A methodological critique says no study has estimated a causal effect, the ratio is statistically flawed, and there is no evidence for using it to steer training [impellizzeri-2020-acwr-pitfalls]. Both can be true: association without a justified intervention. The engine should not treat any ratio band as a safety gate.

**HRV-guided training.** The meta-analysis shows significant benefit for vagal HRV indices but only small, non-significant benefits for VO2max and performance, with possibly fewer negative responders [manresa-rocamora-2021-hrv-guided-meta]. That supports a cautious, optional use.

**ML injury prediction.** AUCs of 0.68 to 0.72 in 74 elite runners [lovdal-2021-injury-prediction-ml] show some signal but are far from decision-grade.

## What is unknown

- Whether any adaptive or AI-generated plan improves outcomes or injury rates versus a fixed plan in running. No trial exists in this corpus.
- How Firstbeat/Garmin training status, TrainingPeaks PMC, intervals.icu, Runna and Stryd model load. No peer-reviewed documentation was found in this pass; PMC-style tools are only indirectly covered through the Banister literature. Do not copy their numbers as if validated.
- Validated progression caps, ultra-distance (100-mile) prediction accuracy, and sex, age and heat effects on these algorithms. These belong to other topic corpora, and nothing here covers them.

## How the engine should use this

1. **Deterministic core, LLM text only.** Sessions, volumes and paces come from rule code with schema validation. An LLM may explain or reword, never prescribe. Build the Düking-style expert criteria as automated tests.
2. **Individualize from the athlete's own data, with shrinkage.** Fit a personal race-projection model (personal exponent or two-parameter capacity/endurance) from recent best efforts and refit as data arrive. Update fitness and tolerance parameters toward population priors, with confidence-weighted soft adjustments and visible uncertainty [hecksteden-2015-individual-response, blythe-2016-runner-performance-prediction, emig-2020-running-big-data].
3. **No hard responder labels.** After a block with no measurable improvement, change the stimulus before concluding non-response [bonafiglia-2016-interval-response-variability].
4. **Load display is descriptive.** Use fixed population constants if a CTL/ATL view is shown, never fitted ones. Do not claim injury prediction or precise form/taper timing. Any ACWR figure is low-confidence and never a gate [hellard-2006-banister-limitations, impellizzeri-2020-acwr-pitfalls].
5. **Progression limits are explicit expert rules** declared as such (grade D), because the load-injury literature here does not justify numeric caps.
6. **Optional modifiers only.** HRV can downgrade a hard session against a rolling baseline. ML injury scores are at most a gentle heads-up. Device VO2max is a smoothed trend, never a pace source.
7. **Cohort priors.** Similar-runner retrieval can seed volumes for a goal time, bounded by safety caps and never used as a target to exceed [feely-2020-cbr-marathon-plans].

## Gaps

- No peer-reviewed documentation found in this pass for how Firstbeat/Garmin training status, TrainingPeaks PMC, intervals.icu, Runna or Stryd model load; WebSearch/grey literature (vendor white papers) was not searched, so these remain unverified.
- No randomized or prospective trial of adaptive or AI-generated running plans versus fixed plans on performance or injury outcomes.
- No LLM plan evaluation found from 2025-2026 beyond Montaruli 2026 (older model generations); only a targeted Europe PMC search was run.
- No running-specific evidence for ACWR causality in either direction; progression caps cannot be justified numerically from this topic's corpus.
- Ultra-distance (100-mile) race-time prediction and pacing accuracy of personal-exponent and two-parameter models is not covered by the cited abstracts.
- Individual-response evidence comes from lab cycling or general exercise, not running plan blocks; the minimum observation count for adaptation (3) has no empirical basis.
- Effects of age, sex and temperature on these algorithms are not addressed by the papers here.
- Author lists for two added papers (smyth-2021-marathon-recsys, bonafiglia-2016-interval-response-variability) and griffin-2020 fourth author were only partly confirmed against Crossref this session (first three authors confirmed); corrections for these entries should be checked before display.
