# Running injury risk and load progression

## What is established

**Injuries are common and novices carry the most risk per hour.** Pooled incidence is 17.8 injuries per 1000 h in novices and 7.7 in recreational runners [videbaek-2015-incidence]. The one factor that consistently predicts injury across reviews is a previous injury [hulme-2017-risk-protective] [vanderworp-2015-risk-factors]. For bone stress injury (BSI), a previous BSI (pooled OR 4.99) and female sex (OR 2.31) are the strongly supported factors [wright-2015-stress-fracture-meta], and women have more BSI than men even though overall injury rates do not differ by sex (RR 0.99) [hollander-2021-sex-differences].

**The weekly 10% rule is not an injury preventive.** The only RCT found no difference between a 10%-rule graded program (20.8% injured) and a standard program (20.3%) in novices [buist-2008-graded-training]. A 10% and a 24% average weekly increase showed no difference either [damsted-2018-training-load-change], and in the exploratory Nielsen cohort, weekly progression groups did not differ for overall injury; only distance-related injuries hinted at risk above 30% (HR 1.59, 0.96-2.66, P = .07) [nielsen-2014-weekly-progression]. A 2012 review concluded that no specific training error could be identified [nielsen-2012-training-errors].

**A single-session spike is the best current running-specific signal.** In 5205 Garmin-tracked runners, a session more than 10% longer than the longest run of the prior 30 days carried HRR 1.64 (10-30%), 1.52 (30-100%) and 2.28 (>100%), versus the reference of up to a 10% increase [frandsen-2025-session-spike]. Week-to-week ratios showed no association there. Most overuse injuries looked sudden rather than gradual, and few runners reported a preceding problem (6.9% at 7 days, 11.1% at 28 days) [frandsen-2025-sudden-onset].

## What is contested

**ACWR.** Team-sport reviews support an association with non-contact injury [griffin-2020-acwr-systematic-review] and the paradox paper promotes it [gabbett-2016-paradox], but methodological critiques say no causal evidence exists and that the ratio itself creates artefacts and fails around tapers [impellizzeri-2020-acwr-pitfalls] [wang-2020-acwr-lessons]. In the only large running cohort here, a 1-week:3-week ACWR showed a negative dose-response with injury [frandsen-2025-session-spike]. The IOC consensus treats rapid load change as a risk factor but gives no numbers [soligard-2016-ioc-load]. The Lolli coupling editorial has no retrievable abstract and was not added.

**Sex.** One best-evidence review found women at lower overall injury risk [vanderworp-2015-risk-factors]; the larger meta-analysis found no difference [hollander-2021-sex-differences]. Use the latter.

**Protective effect of load.** The paradox model argues that low chronic load raises risk [gabbett-2016-paradox]. That is team-sport, narrative evidence; treat it as a reason not to over-restrict, not as a rule.

## What is unknown

There is no evidence on ultra-distance and 100-mile build-ups: the injury reviews cover up to 42.2 km [hulme-2017-risk-protective], and ultra data are sparse [videbaek-2015-incidence]. Duration, elevation, intensity and heat as spike exposures are untested. Effects of age and non-sex factors on progression limits, optimal deload frequency, and return-to-run ramps after injury are not covered. No RCT tests the session-spike rule, so its causal effect is unknown. Cadence has a BSI association in 54 collegiate runners only [kliethermes-2021-step-rate-bsi].

## How the engine should use this

1. **Make the session rule the primary guard.** Compute the trailing-30-day longest run from actual logs, and check every planned run against it. Default maximum is 1.10x; show a soft warning above that. Do not let the plan generator exceed 2.0x of it without an explicit user override. The risk bands are not monotonic, so do not describe 30-100% as safer than 10-30%.
2. **Keep weekly volume progression soft.** Default about 10% per week with regular lower-volume weeks, and an outer bound of about 30% over two weeks for novices; none of these numbers is validated. Never tell users the 10% rule prevents injury.
3. **Do not build around ACWR.** No ratio targets, no sweet spot, no hard limits. If shown, label it descriptive, require history, and suppress it in taper and recovery weeks.
4. **Use risk-profile inputs for conservatism, not for sex-based volume caps.** Previous injury, previous BSI, menstrual irregularity, and novice status select more conservative settings and referral prompts; their magnitudes are engineering defaults (grade D). Sex affects BSI vigilance copy only.
5. **Do not rely on symptom check-ins alone.** Keep them as a secondary signal.
6. **For ultra distances, apply the same guard but label it unvalidated.** Long runs and back-to-backs in 100-mile builds will often be flagged by distance-only rules; resolve with time-on-feet and user overrides, and say so.

## Caveats

All Frandsen results are observational hazard rates relative to a reference state, not proven effects of capping sessions. Abstract-level verification was used for every entry.

## Gaps

- No evidence on ultra-distance and 100-mile build-ups: the reviewed injury studies are capped at 42.2 km and ultra injury data are sparse. Duration, elevation, pace and heat as spike exposures are also untested.
- No RCT of the session-spike rule; only observational hazard ratios exist, and Frandsen 2025 was verified at abstract level only (full text not read).
- Lolli 2019 (mathematical coupling in ACWR, BJSM 53:921-922, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2017-098110) is an editorial without an abstract, so no finding could be verified; it was not added.
- Quantitative evidence for deload frequency, return-to-run ramps after injury, and age effects on progression limits is missing; all such values in the rules are grade D defaults.
- Bone stress risk beyond sex and prior BSI (low energy availability, RED-S, vitamin D, impact load) is not covered; Hoenig 2022 (Nat Rev Dis Primers, bone stress injuries) and IOC RED-S consensus papers are candidates for a bone-health topic.
- Hollander 2021 and van der Worp 2015 conflict on overall sex differences; no resolution for sex-specific progression limits.
- Frandsen 2025 sudden-onset paper has no PubMed record and no abstract in Europe PMC; verification relied on Crossref.
