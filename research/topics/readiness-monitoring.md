# Readiness, HRV and recovery monitoring

## What is established

**HRV-guided training is safe and roughly equivalent to a planned programme; it is not a performance lever.** Two meta-analyses find small, non-significant effects on performance and VO2max (g about 0.08 to 0.17 in [duking-2021-hrv-guided-meta]; SMD about 0.20 in [manresa-rocamora-2021-hrv-guided-meta]). The consistent mechanism is that guided arms do fewer moderate and high-intensity sessions and still match or slightly beat the fixed plan. In runners, [vesterinen-2016-hrv-runners-rct] saw about 25% fewer hard sessions (13.2 vs 17.7) with a small between-group effect (ES 0.42), and [nuuttila-2017-hrv-block-training] saw the same direction. The most recent runner RCT found no between-group differences between HR, race-pace and HRV prescription at equal load [ranieri-2025-racepace-hr-hrv-rct]. Possible benefits are fewer non-responders [duking-2021-hrv-guided-meta] and better vagal HRV with fewer negative responses [manresa-rocamora-2021-hrv-guided-meta]. A subgroup analysis suggests more benefit in amateurs and women [granerogallegos-2020-hrv-vo2max-meta], but that is a moderator analysis on VO2max only.

**No single objective marker identifies overreaching.** Resting RMSSD rises with productive training and barely changes (small rise) with overreaching; post-exercise HRV and HR recovery rise in both [bellenger-2016-autonomic-hr-meta-analysis]. The ECSS/ACSM consensus says no marker meets the criteria for general use and that diagnosis is by exclusion [meeusen-2013-overtraining-consensus]. Subjective measures track acute and chronic load more sensitively and consistently than objective ones, and the two rarely correlate [saw-2016-subjective-vs-objective-monitoring].

**Consumer-device HRV is noisy.** In one 13-person ECG comparison, nightly HRV error ranged from about 6% (Oura Gen 4) to about 10% (Garmin Fenix 6) and 16% (Polar), while nightly resting HR error was 1.7 to 3.0% [dial-2025-nocturnal-wearables].

## What is contested

- **The baseline method.** The classic rule is a 10-day rolling mean minus 1 SD, or a 2-day decline [kiviniemi-2007-hrv-guided-rct]; Vesterinen used an individual smallest-worthwhile-change band. The meta-analysis authors say fixed versus rolling baselines, index choice and recording position are unresolved [manresa-rocamora-2021-hrv-guided-meta]. Expert guidance favours individual smallest-important-change interpretation in training context [buchheit-2014-hr-monitoring] (narrative, grade D).
- **Whether HRV adds anything beyond zones.** [ranieri-2025-racepace-hr-hrv-rct] says not for time trials at equal load.
- **Sex and level effects.** Thin and subgroup-driven; all early RCTs are male [kiviniemi-2007-hrv-guided-rct] [nuuttila-2017-hrv-block-training].
- **Sleep.** Sleep loss plausibly shifts autonomic balance and impairs sport performance, but effects of 1 to 3 partially short nights are unclear [fullagar-2015-sleep-loss-performance] [walsh-2021-sleep-athlete-consensus]. The injury link rests on an adolescent cross-sectional study [milewski-2014-sleep-injury].

## What is unknown

There is no randomised evidence in ultra-distance or 100-mile runners, in masters runners, in women-only samples, or on wearable-derived HRV driving plan changes over a full season. There is no evidence-based numeric return-to-training protocol after illness (only symptom duration, mean 7.1 days, and the fact that about 80% of respiratory illnesses cost no days [snyders-2022-respiratory-illness-rts]), and consensus documents give no thresholds for load adjustment [schwellnus-2016-load-illness-ioc]. No trial shows that acting on daily subjective scores improves outcomes.

## How the engine should use this

1. **Planned session is the default.** Readiness can only downgrade a hard session to easy, never add load or back-fill missed intensity.
2. **Subjective first.** Collect a short daily wellness check-in (fatigue, soreness, sleep quality, mood, stress, illness) and treat it as the primary signal. HRV and resting HR corroborate.
3. **Require agreement or persistence.** Change the plan only when two signals agree, or one signal persists for at least 2 consecutive days. Never act on one night of consumer HRV.
4. **Make HRV thresholds per-user and per-device.** Use ln-transformed morning/nocturnal values vs the individual rolling baseline; default to the Kiviniemi window until better evidence exists, labelled as a configurable default. Store the device model and raise the noise floor for lower-accuracy devices. Mark missing or noisy days unknown.
5. **Do not diagnose.** Flags are prompts for easier days; sustained unexplained decline sends the user to a clinician and a checklist (energy intake, iron, illness, sleep) [meeusen-2013-overtraining-consensus].
6. **Illness overrides everything.** A logged symptom flag suspends intensity; HRV/RHR changes alone never declare illness. The graded return is an engine safety convention (grade D).
7. **Keep pace/HR zones as the intensity anchor.** HRV is a modifier only.
8. **Copy discipline.** Language stays non-judgemental: "consider an easy day", never "behind" or "missed", consistent with the project's targets-are-a-sketch rule. Disclose that HRV evidence is small-effect and that this is not medical advice.

## Evidence-quality caveat

Meta-analytic conclusions pool roughly eight small trials with heterogeneous protocols; individual RCTs have 8 to 20 per arm. Treat every numeric threshold below grade A as a default to be tuned, and log outcomes so the engine can audit its own rules.

## Gaps

- No RCT of HRV- or wellness-guided training in ultra or 100-mile runners, masters runners, or women-only samples.
- No evidence on whether wearable-derived (nocturnal, vendor-processed) HRV used for daily plan adjustment improves running outcomes or injury rates over a full season.
- Optimal HRV index, recording position, baseline window and threshold (fixed vs rolling, SD vs smallest worthwhile change) remain unresolved; the plan engine defaults are conventions.
- No evidence-based numeric return-to-training protocol after illness (symptom-free days, easy-run duration); only symptom duration data. Neither non-respiratory illness nor post-viral guidance was covered by sources retrieved here.
- No trial shows that acting on daily subjective wellness scores improves performance or reduces injury; Saw 2016 is correlational about training-load response.
- Sleep: no adult runner evidence for a load-adjustment threshold; 1 to 3 nights of partial restriction have unclear effects.
- The Javaloyes HRV RCTs located by the researcher are in cyclists, so they were not included; a runner-specific replication and the Plews HRV monitoring literature (not retrieved here) could be added later.
- Heat and temperature effects on HRV and resting HR readings were not addressed by this topic's evidence.
