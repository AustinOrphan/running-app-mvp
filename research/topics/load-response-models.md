# Load-response and fitness-fatigue models

## Scope and headline

The 17 retained papers cover the Banister impulse-response (fitness-fatigue, FFM) model and its Busso variable dose-response (VDR) extension, EWMA acute/chronic metrics, parameter estimation, and 2019-2026 critiques and ML hybrids. All DOIs resolved in Crossref, titles, journals and years matched, and every finding was checked against the Europe PMC abstract (Marchal's sample sizes came from open-access full text). No entries were dropped. Corrections made: design labels for Busso 2003 and Vermeire 2021, the CI width in Hellard 2006, Bourdon's evidence level (A to D), and unsupported wording in Busso 2023 and Nakaoka 2021. Three papers were added.

## What is established

- **Structure.** Training impulses produce a slowly decaying positive (fitness) and a faster-decaying negative (fatigue) response that combine linearly into predicted performance [morton-1990-running-model]. VDR makes fatigue gain depend on recent load, fits better in-sample, and yields an inverted-U between daily training and performance [busso-2003-variable-dose-response].
- **Fit is not prediction.** In-sample fit is good (R2 about 0.79 in elite swimmers) [hellard-2006-banister-limitations], but forward prediction error was 2.0-2.7% MAPE on swimming sprint times, which the authors judged not satisfactory for individual planning [busso-2023-impulse-response-validity]. VDR was no better than Banister there. Busso and Thomas reached the same conclusion in 2006 [busso-2006-modeling-training-planning].
- **Poor identifiability.** Parameter CIs are 26-42 days wide and parameters are strongly correlated [hellard-2006-banister-limitations]. Bayesian analysis on two skater datasets found the fatigue parameters add no predictive value (p > 0.40) and that informative priors dominate the fitness time-constant posterior [marchal-2025-ffm-statistical-flaws].
- **Load metric matters.** Fit error was similar across TRIMP variants, TSS and sRPE, but the implied timing of peak training benefit moved by up to 19.6 days [vermeire-2021-load-quantification]. Metrics are not interchangeable.
- **ACWR is weak as a safety rule.** There is no causal evidence for ACWR thresholds and the ratio has statistical flaws [impellizzeri-2020-acwr-pitfalls]. In 435 runners, higher ACWR was associated with lower injury probability, and EWMA was the least informative method [nakaoka-2021-acwr-runners]. The favourable EWMA result comes from 59 football players and an injury outcome [murray-2017-ewma-acwr].

## What is contested

- Whether the fatigue term is real or an overfitting artifact [marchal-2025-ffm-statistical-flaws] versus useful for diagnosing overreaching via time-varying estimates (eight swimmers, descriptive) [busso-2025-time-varying-overreaching].
- Whether ML should replace or wrap FFMs. Elastic net and PCR on filtered load features generalised better than VDR in seven skaters [imbach-2022-generalisation]; the authors argue for physiology-informed features inside ML models [imbach-2022-ffm-machine-learning]. Both are tiny samples in non-running sports.
- Whether wearable HRV/HRR can serve as the response variable. Per-athlete errors ranged from 4% to 87% MAPE in 13 cyclists [wang-2025-ffm-mass-sport].

## What is unknown

No verified study validates any FFM, CTL/ATL variant or ML predictor for running performance, marathon-to-ultra distances, 100-mile races, older athletes, sex differences or heat. Evidence comes from swimmers, skaters and cyclists with 6-14 athletes. EWMA time constants for CTL/ATL (commonly 42 and 7 days) are industry convention, not supported by the retrieved evidence.

## How the engine should use this

1. **Plan generation must not depend on the FFM.** Weekly volume, progression, taper and long-run structure should come from other topic rules. The FFM is a monitoring and what-if layer.
2. **Default model** is a fitness-dominant, regularised impulse-response with bounded time constants, with priors rather than free estimation; the fatigue term and VDR switch on only if a forward-chaining CV beats the simpler model [imbach-2022-generalisation, marchal-2025-ffm-statistical-flaws].
3. **Always attach uncertainty.** Show forecast bands, and a conservative 3% race-time band as a placeholder until running data exist. Do not display time constants or label them physiological [hellard-2006-banister-limitations].
4. **One load metric per fit**, stored with the parameter set; refit on change [vermeire-2021-load-quantification]. Prefer an intensity-aware metric (HR TRIMP or sRPE) over volume alone [jobson-2009-cycling-training-data].
5. **Benchmark** every mechanistic predictor against elastic net on EWMA features using time-series CV [imbach-2022-generalisation].
6. **ACWR** is a displayed context value only. No cap or red flag; low values may mark return from a break [nakaoka-2021-acwr-runners, impellizzeri-2020-acwr-pitfalls].
7. **Cap benefit** of added load via the inverted-U [busso-2003-variable-dose-response] and keep separate stores for load, readiness and chronic capacity [jeffries-2021-training-framework].
8. **Data sufficiency.** When performance tests are sparse (typical for recreational runners), fall back to load-only guidance [jobson-2009-cycling-training-data, wang-2025-ffm-mass-sport].

## Gaps

- No verified study validates the Banister/VDR model, CTL/ATL, or any ML predictor for running performance (road, trail or ultra); all evidence is from swimmers, skaters and cyclists with 6-14 athletes.
- No evidence on 100-mile or ultra-distance events, where performance tests are rare and race outcomes are driven by pacing, fuelling and heat as much as load.
- No quantified moderators for age, sex or temperature on the fitness/fatigue response; the framework papers are conceptual only.
- No head-to-head comparison of consumer CTL/ATL (fixed 42/7-day EWMA) against fitted Banister models or ML for predicting performance; the 42/7 defaults are convention.
- No study of hierarchical or population-prior (mixed-effects/Bayesian) estimation in runners, though Avalos 2003 and Marchal 2025 suggest it as a remedy for ill-conditioning; not retrieved here.
- Lack of a validated low-burden performance proxy (for example field time trials, parkrun-type efforts, or HR-pace efficiency) for recreational runners to supply the performance observations the model needs.
- ML literature 2019-2026 retrieved is thin and non-running; ML injury-prediction papers were out of scope and not verified.
- Prediction horizon and error are untested for taper and race-day performance; the 2-3% MAPE figure comes from 50-m swim trials.
