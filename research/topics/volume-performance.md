# Training volume and race performance

## Summary for the plan engine

The first pass verified 15 papers on volume and half-marathon, marathon and ultra performance (abstracts only). A second pass on 2026-10-22 added 12 sources on the weekly long run for 5 km and 10 km goals. Three are reviews or a cohort on injury and training parameters, two are surveys or correlational studies of recreational short-distance runners, one is a 10 km prediction study, and six are published plans or coach statements. The plans and coach statements are conventions (grade D).

### What is well established

**Volume tracks race performance wherever it has been measured.** Doherty found every volume covariate (weekly distance, runs per week, longest run, hours) associated with faster marathon times across 137 cohorts [doherty-2020-marathon-training-meta]. Muniz-Pumares reproduced this in 119,452 runners [muniz-pumares-2025-marathon-tid], and smaller samples agree [fokkema-2020-half-marathon-volume-long-run, yamaguchi-2023-volume-frequency-run-distance, casado-2021-easy-run-volume, gordon-2017-recreational-marathon-characteristics]. For 10 km the direction is the same. In 60 recreational runners, weekly distance (r = -0.605) and training days (r = -0.583) were the strongest correlates of 10 km time, and with other variables they explained 57% of its variance [suwankan-2024-10km-time-prediction]. Targets scale with the goal and the runner's base.

**Breaks in consistency are costly.** A gap of 7 or more days in the 16 weeks before a marathon cost 5-8% of finish time [feely-2022-training-disruptions].

**Single prediction equations are unreliable** [vickers-2016-race-times, keogh-2019-marathon-prediction-equations].

**No study identifies a safe or unsafe length for a single session.** A review of 36 prospective studies (23,047 runners) found the evidence on weekly distance, duration, frequency, intensity and changes in them conflicting [fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review]. In short-distance runners, lower volume and no running experience were moderate-quality risk factors for injury [vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review], so very low volume is not automatically safe. The relevant single-session evidence is still the spike relative to the runner's own 30-day longest run (hazard ratios 1.5-2.3 above 1.10x) [frandsen-2025-session-spike], which is G-1. A fixed long-run cap is therefore a convention, not an injury limit.

### The long run for 5 km and 10 km goals

No study of 5-10 km runners measures long-run length against performance or injury. Suwankan did not include the longest run as a predictor [suwankan-2024-10km-time-prediction], and NURMI reports no long-run variable [knechtle-2023-nurmi-training-by-distance]. In marathoners, at a fixed monthly volume, long-run length added nothing to performance [yamaguchi-2023-volume-frequency-run-distance]. A weekly longest run was associated with running-economy durability over 90 minutes in 26 men [zanini-2026-long-runs-economy-durability], which bears on the half-marathon and longer, not on 5-10 km.

What recreational 10 km runners actually do: in the main competitive period they reported about 26 km, 3.6 h and 3 sessions a week, with standard deviations as large as the means [knechtle-2023-nurmi-training-by-distance]. Most short-race runners therefore sit in the low or medium tier with about 3 run days. At that frequency the long run is inevitably a large share of the week.

The conventions converge on three points. At low and medium volume the long run is about 30-40% of the week. At high volume the ceiling is about 90-100 minutes or 10 miles (16 km) for both distances. Higdon's +5 min steps (about 8%) sit inside G-1's 1.10x band. The engine's 60 min cap for 5 km is below every advanced plan. Because no run of the week may be longer than the long run, that cap also limits a 6-day 5 km week to 360 minutes, which cuts weekly volume, the variable the evidence does support.

### Contested or weakly supported

- **Diminishing returns.** No paper estimates the curve. The associations are monotone and between-runner, so they cannot be extrapolated upward for one athlete.
- **Floors.** Fokkema's half-marathon and marathon thresholds come from categorised self-reports [fokkema-2020-half-marathon-volume-long-run]. They are soft floors. Johnson's 5-10 km minimums are coaching opinion and are not floors.
- **Frequency against volume.** At a fixed volume, extra runs added nothing for marathon time [yamaguchi-2023-volume-frequency-run-distance], while training days correlated with 10 km time in an unadjusted analysis [suwankan-2024-10km-time-prediction]. The two fit together: the engine adds run days as volume rises (F5), not as a separate lever (F2).
- **Share of the week.** Daniels gives 30% of distance; Higdon's intermediate plan implies 35-47%. Measured in time, an easy long run is a slightly larger share than it is in distance, so 0.35 of weekly time sits inside the range.
- **Load ratios.** Each day at a 7:28-day ratio of 1.5 or more carried an injury OR of 1.06 [toresdahl-2023-nyc-marathon-training-injury]. A duration-based ACWR was associated with injury (adjusted HR 1.33), but not after runners injured at baseline were excluded [cloosterman-2026-gps-load-injury]. Frandsen found an inverse ACWR association [frandsen-2025-session-spike]. Ratios are for display only (G-5). The IOC statement is qualitative [soligard-2016-ioc-load].
- **Intensity and ultras.** Fast runners add easy volume [muniz-pumares-2025-marathon-tid, casado-2021-easy-run-volume]. Ultra predictors disagree [tanda-2015-training-anthropometry-100km, knechtle-2010-100km-predictors, knechtle-2009-24h-run-training-volume].

### What is unknown

- Whether a longer long run improves 5-10 km performance independently of weekly volume.
- Any long-run length that is safer or riskier than another.
- The individual dose-response.
- Minimum training to finish a marathon.
- Volume for 100 miles.
- Age and sex modifiers of the volume response.

### How the engine should use this

1. Scale weekly targets to the goal and the current base. For 5-10 km, grow weekly time first and let run days follow volume [suwankan-2024-10km-time-prediction].
2. Set the long run at 0.35 of the week's running time, capped by a tier chosen from the runner's completed typical week (median of the last 4 weeks with a run), never from the planned week. The 5 km caps are 60, 75 and 90 min, and the 10 km caps 75, 90 and 100 min, at under 180, 180-360 and over 360 min/week. Both distances are also capped at 16 km. All of these are grade D defaults and are labelled as defaults. G-1 and G-2 still bind every week, so a runner reaches a cap only from their own history.
3. New runners start from a structured, graded programme in the style of Couch to 5K, which was associated with fewer first-year injuries than a self-devised plan [linton-2018-uk-novice-recreational-injury]. The E6 start paths provide it, and the long run grows from the longest recent continuous run under G-1 rather than from a share target. This design is not claimed to prevent injury: a graded RCT programme did not [buist-2008-graded-training].
4. Keep the 7-day gap flag and graded restarts. Show load ratios without gating on them.
5. Report predictions as intervals from at least two sources.
6. Present the long-run caps as coaching conventions and never as injury protection.

## Gaps

- No study of 5-10 km runners measures long-run length (minutes, distance or share) against race performance; Suwankan and NURMI do not report a longest-run variable.
- No study identifies a safe or unsafe single-session duration; the injury reviews find training-parameter evidence conflicting or low quality.
- Whether a longer long run adds anything for 5-10 km beyond weekly volume and run days is untested; the only within-volume test (Yamaguchi) is in male marathoners.
- The minute values of coach plans are estimates from miles at assumed easy paces; the sources give miles only (except Higdon advanced 5K).
- Daniels' rule was read through a magazine report, not the book; Higdon pages were read through a summariser, and the advanced 5K header (12 mi) contradicts its 90 min schedule.
- NURMI self-reported hours do not reconcile with sessions; per-session length cannot be derived for recreational 10 km runners.
- Women, older runners and slower 5-10 km runners are not analysed separately for long-run length.
- Minimum training to finish a marathon, diminishing returns, 100-mile volume, and age/sex modifiers remain unmeasured (carried over from the first pass).
