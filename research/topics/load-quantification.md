# Training load quantification and wearable data validity

## What is established

**Session RPE is the best-supported universal internal-load measure for this app.** Foster's original validation compared session RPE with an HR-based score in cycling and basketball and found a consistent relationship [foster-2001-session-rpe]. A review of 36 validity and reliability studies found good validity and reliability across sports, sexes and ages [haddad-2017-session-rpe-review]. In seven trained runners, sRPE, Banister TRIMP and running TSS all gave similar model fits to 1,500 m performance (r = 0.60 to 0.70), with no method clearly ahead [wallace-2014-load-methods-runners]. The practical consequence is that sRPE works when HR is missing or unreliable (treadmill, trail, hot, no strap), and none of the metrics is demonstrably better than the others.

**Internal and external load are different constructs.** Internal load is the psychophysiological response (HR, TRIMP, RPE) and external load is what was done (distance, pace, elevation, power) [impellizzeri-2019-internal-external-load] [bourdon-2017-load-consensus]. The data model should keep them apart.

**Wrist optical HR is good on average in steady conditions and poor in variable ones.** The meta-analysis pooled treadmill running at -0.51 bpm bias (95% CI -1.60 to 0.58) [zhang-2020-wrist-ppg-meta], and most devices had rc 0.88 to 0.93 on a treadmill [gillinov-2017-wearable-hr-aerobic]. On a hilly 3.22 km trail run, wrist and other PPG devices agreed poorly (Garmin Fenix 5 MAPE 13%, LOA -32 to 162 bpm), while a chest-strap system had MAPE 2% [navalta-2020-trail-running-hr]. Error rises with activity change [bent-2020-ppg-inaccuracy]. Device-reported energy expenditure was not accurate for any brand [fuller-2020-wearables-systematic-review].

## What is contested

- **Skin tone.** Four of ten studies found reduced wrist-HR accuracy in darker skin, four found none, two were mixed [koerber-2022-skin-tone-wrist-hr]; a separate analysis found no significant skin-tone difference [bent-2020-ppg-inaccuracy], and that finding has published rebuttals not read here. There is no basis for a correction factor.
- **RPE vs physiology.** The pooled RPE-HR correlation is about 0.62, well below the older assumption of 0.80 to 0.90 [chen-2002-borg-rpe-meta]. sRPE and HR load will often disagree, and neither is simply correct.
- **TRIMP version.** The only runner-specific TRIMP evidence located is a small individualized-TRIMP study [manzi-2009-individualized-trimp-marathon] and the sRPE/TRIMP/rTSS comparison [wallace-2014-load-methods-runners]. Neither tests sex-specific constants.

## What is unknown or unverified

- **The Banister sex-specific constants (1.92 men, 1.67 women) could not be verified.** The source is a 1991 book chapter that is not indexed, and no abstract in this corpus states them. The weights should be implemented as a commonly cited default, labelled grade D, with the formula visible in the UI. Edwards (zone weights 1 to 5 of time in 50-100% HRmax bands) and Lucia (zone weights tied to ventilatory thresholds, from a cycling paper whose abstract does not give the multipliers) are likewise not backed by any verified abstract here.
- **Running TSS / hrTSS** has one small study [wallace-2014-load-methods-runners]; there is no validation for hrTSS from consumer devices.
- **GPS distance accuracy in running** has no systematic review in the corpus. The only evidence is a skiing study with one old wrist watch [gloersen-2018-gnss-endurance].
- **sRPE and wearable validity in ultra-distance (up to 100 miles) running** are untested in this corpus. All HR validation here involved runs of under about an hour.
- **Smartwatch LT estimates:** HR error is about 9 to 11 bpm (MAE) and LT pace is overestimated by 13 to 26% (MAPE) [lu-2025-smartwatch-lactate-threshold].
- **Running power:** Stryd shows good repeatability and validity against VO2; other devices have low repeatability; the evidence is 12 male athletes in 2020 [cerezuela-2020-running-power-validity].

## How the engine should use this

1. Make sRPE (minutes x CR-10) the primary internal-load metric and ask for it after every run with fixed wording and anchors. HR load, where trustworthy, is a second, separate number. Never sum or convert between them [foster-2001-session-rpe] [haddad-2017-session-rpe-review].
2. Tag each activity with HR source (chest strap, wrist, none), device make/model and activity type. Compute TRIMP only from chest-strap HR or wrist HR on steady, flat or treadmill running, after sample-level sanity checks. For hills, trails, intervals and non-running activities, fall back to sRPE. The check thresholds are a design choice, not an evidenced number.
3. Treat wrist/sRPE disagreement as information (possible fatigue, heat, illness, or sensor failure) to display, not auto-correct [chen-2002-borg-rpe-meta].
4. Use external load (distance, duration, elevation) for volume and mechanical questions. Use device distance rather than rebuilt pace; smooth speed; treat watch elevation as noisy.
5. Do not use device kcal, watch LT pace or watch LT HR as anchors. Anchor zones on observed max HR, recent hard efforts or a race/time trial, and let users override.
6. Present all load numbers as a sketch for one athlete, not as a validated injury predictor [bourdon-2017-load-consensus].

## Gaps

- Banister 1991 TRIMP formula and the 1.92 / 1.67 sex-specific constants, Edwards zone-weighted TRIMP and Lucia TRIMP multipliers are not verified in any indexed abstract in this corpus (the Lucia 2003 cycling paper, 10.1249/01.mss.0000064999.82036.b4, was found but its abstract omits the multipliers). Needs full-text or book access.
- No validated source for running TSS/hrTSS beyond one 7-runner study; no validation of hrTSS computed from consumer-device data.
- No systematic review of consumer GPS distance, pace or elevation accuracy in running; the only GNSS evidence is a skiing study with one 2014-era watch.
- No evidence on sRPE validity, wrist or chest HR drift/validity, or GPS accuracy in ultra-endurance running (6 to 30+ hours, up to 100 miles).
- Wearable HR validation is mostly laboratory and short; no evidence on cardiac drift, heat effects or cadence lock over long runs, and no data on current (2024 to 2026) watch sensors except a single 2025 LT study.
- No evidence for age- or sex-specific differences in sRPE, TRIMP weighting or wrist-HR accuracy in runners.
- Running-power evidence is 12 male athletes in 2020; no data for Stryd vs native Garmin/Coros power with current firmware or on trail terrain.
- Candidates verified in Europe PMC but not added (cap of three additions): Halson 2014 (10.1007/s40279-014-0253-z, notes dissociation of internal and external load), Foster 2017 (10.1123/ijspp.2016-0388, history of TRIMP and sRPE), Esteve-Lanao 2005 (10.1249/01.mss.0000155393.78744.86, zone time and performance in runners, n=8).
