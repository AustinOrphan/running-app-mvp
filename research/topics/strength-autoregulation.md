# Repetitions in reserve and autoregulation in resistance training

## Summary for the plan engine

This topic holds the literature on stopping a set a counted number of repetitions short of its limit (repetitions in reserve, RIR), on how well lifters count, on choosing loads by that count against fixed percentages, and on estimating a one-repetition maximum from a set. It was written by hand on 2026-10-31 for task RU1 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-sources-methods-evaluation-proposal.md` (the owner's decision of the same date: the logging sheet keeps reps left, and a `research-update` pass grounds the RIR Params), not by the corpus workflow. Each source was located by DOI and read in the source that day. Eighteen records were added (406 to 424) and seven earlier records were re-read and now carry `vetting`. Eleven sources were read in full on Europe PMC or the journal's open-access page (Helms 2016, Helms 2018, Steele 2017, Grgic 2022, Refalo 2022, Richens 2014, Martikainen 2025, Wiedenmann 2026, the 2026 position stand, and the re-read Hickmott 2022 and Nuzzo 2023, whose tables of values were images and were not read); the rest by abstract, because the full text is closed access and was not fetched. Each record says which.

Three points carry the rest.

1. **The count is a usable but imprecise effort target.** Near the limit, trained lifters were within about one repetition: 0.65 repetitions of absolute error at 1 and at 3 left on the bench press at 75% of 1RM [refalo-2023-rir-accuracy-bench], and about 1 at 0-5 left against more than 2 at 7-10 left in sets of 10 [hackett-2017-accuracy-repetitions-failure]. Far from the limit, in longer sets, and before practice, errors were several repetitions: a call of 3 left came before about 6.7 more repetitions in 16-repetition squat sets [zourdos-2021-intraset-rir-accuracy], an error of 2-5 at 4 left on leg press sets of 13-15 repetitions that halved within six sessions in younger and older adults alike [wiedenmann-2026-rir-trainability], and beginners understated what they could do by up to about 8 repetitions [steele-2017-predict-repetitions-failure]. The pooled mean is an underprediction of 0.95 repetitions [halperin-2021-rir-prediction-accuracy]. The direction means a reported count understates the true reserve.
2. **Stopping short of the limit does not cost strength in lifters.** Two meta-analyses of randomised trials found no strength difference between training to the limit and not to it [grgic-2022-failure-vs-nonfailure, vieira-2021-failure-vs-nonfailure], strength did not depend on the estimated reps in reserve in a meta-regression [robinson-2024-proximity-failure-meta], a randomised trial found the same gain at 1 left as at 4 falling to 1 [martikainen-2025-varying-proximity-failure], and the 2026 position stand of the American College of Sports Medicine says the limit is not necessary and names near-limit or 2-3 in reserve as enough effort, while stating that the evidence cannot fix an exact target [currier-2026-acsm-resistance-prescription].
3. **Choosing loads by the count was no worse than fixed percentages in trained men.** Two small trials [helms-2018-rpe-vs-percent-loading, graham-2021-rir-autoregulation-fixed] and a pooled estimate (SMD 0.21, interval including 0; 0.30 for the RIR-based subgroup, p = 0.06, with those groups lifting at a higher average intensity) [hickmott-2022-autoregulation-meta].

None of it is in runners, none tests a threshold of 1 or 4 in reserve for raising a load, and the only runner data concern repetitions at a percentage of 1RM (8 runners, one machine) [richens-2014-reps-at-percent-runners].

## What is contested

- **Whether training status changes the accuracy of the count.** Beginners underpredicted most [steele-2017-predict-repetitions-failure] and experienced squatters rated the maximum as harder [zourdos-2016-rir-rpe-scale], while five papers found no effect of training status or experience [halperin-2021-rir-prediction-accuracy, hackett-2017-accuracy-repetitions-failure, zourdos-2021-intraset-rir-accuracy, remmert-2023-rir-accuracy-trained-untrained, refalo-2023-rir-accuracy-bench]. Accuracy improved with a few sessions of practice in two studies [martikainen-2025-varying-proximity-failure, wiedenmann-2026-rir-trainability]. The engine reads the cautious side: a missing count holds the load.
- **Whether training to the limit helps hypertrophy in trained people.** One meta-analysis found a small advantage for failure in two trials of trained people (ES 0.15) [grgic-2022-failure-vs-nonfailure]; another found a trivial advantage under any definition of failure that lost significance under a change of an assumed correlation, and none for momentary failure [refalo-2022-proximity-hypertrophy]; a third found an advantage that disappeared once volume was equal [vieira-2021-failure-vs-nonfailure]. Hypertrophy is not the goal of the strength block.
- **How large the benefit of count-based loading is.** One trial found a significant advantage over 12 weeks [graham-2021-rir-autoregulation-fixed], another none over 8 weeks [helms-2018-rpe-vs-percent-loading], and the pooled estimate is not significant [hickmott-2022-autoregulation-meta]. In each, the count-based group trained at a higher intensity, so the advantage may be the intensity and not the method.

## What is not known

- Anything about repetitions in reserve in runners: a block target, accuracy, or the effect of a stop short of the limit on running economy or injury. The review of failure against non-failure says its findings may not carry to concurrent training, where it cites studies in which training to failure did worse beside sprint interval training [grgic-2022-failure-vs-nonfailure]; those studies were not read.
- The accuracy of the count on heavy sets of 3-6 repetitions with a squat, hinge or calf raise. The accuracy studies read use machines or the bench press, sets of 10-16 repetitions or more.
- A threshold for raising a load. The 2026 stand does not restate the 2009 criterion, finds the effect of intrasession autoregulation on strength impossible to determine and says there is insufficient evidence to quantify exact RIR targets.
- The relation of repetitions to a percentage of 1RM for the squat and hinge lifts in recreational runners. The pooled tables are mostly of the bench press and leg press in young resistance-trained men; the runner data are one study of 8 men on the leg press, whose own caveat is that their 1RM may have been under-tested [richens-2014-reps-at-percent-runners].

## Read and not admitted

Read and not recorded, with the reason: a systematic review of autoregulation methods (Larsen 2021, 10.7717/peerj.10663; read in full; one searcher, no bias rating, no pooled estimate, and the same two RIR trials as the records above); a randomised crossover of RPE-based against velocity-based prescription in rugby players (Shattock and Tee 2020, 10.1519/jsc.0000000000003530; abstract; compares two autoregulation methods, not one with fixed loading); a study of volume autoregulation by RPE stops in powerlifters (Helms 2018, 10.1519/jsc.0000000000002032; abstract; about volume, not the Params); an acute crossover of four prescription methods (Cowley 2025, 10.1519/jsc.0000000000005026; abstract); a pilot trial of RIR-based against percentage loading in 16 cardiac rehabilitation patients (Gismondi 2024, 10.3390/jcdd12010008; patients under treatment); an umbrella review that the 2026 stand supersedes and shares authors with (McLeod 2024, 10.1016/j.jshs.2023.06.005; abstract); two further accuracy studies that add nothing new to the dependence on distance from failure (Mansfield 2020, 10.1519/jsc.0000000000003779; Ormsbee 2019, 10.1519/jsc.0000000000001901; abstracts). Two conference abstracts of the American College of Sports Medicine meeting (Perlmutter 2017, Haischer 2017) turned up in a search and were not fetched. Not read: the comment on Refalo 2022 and its response (paywalled letters with no abstract); Julio 2012 (10.1016/j.scispo.2012.07.003, the publisher answered with a bot check); the full text of Graham and Cleather 2021 (closed access, no abstract in PubMed or Europe PMC; the publisher-deposited abstract in Crossref was read); preprints (Halperin 2021 on OSF, Robinson 2024 on SportRxiv), which are not admitted.
