# Performance prediction

## Performance prediction: what the engine can rely on

**Verification result.** All 13 researcher entries resolved in Crossref and Europe PMC with matching title, year, journal and first author. None were dropped. Eight needed content corrections: unsupported or over-stated implications were removed or rewritten (Vickers anchor distance, Oficial-Casado VDOT error and age category, Ruiz-Alias CP overestimation, Minetti steep-gradient claim, Ehrstrom sample description, Blythe data description). Three papers were added: [peronnet-1989-running-model], [maunder-2021-durability], [knechtle-2011-24h-ultra]. No peer-reviewed primary source for Daniels VDOT or the original Riegel 1981 paper was retrieved, so claims about those two methods rest on secondary sources only.

### What is reasonably established

1. **A fixed Riegel exponent is acceptable to about the half marathon and optimistic for the marathon.** [vickers-2016-race-times] found Riegel well calibrated up to the half marathon but at least 10 min too fast for half of runners at the marathon. Two prior races lowered marathon MSE from 381 to 208. [blythe-2016-individual-prediction] shows a runner-specific exponent fits better than fixed power laws and that per-runner parameters relate to specialisation and training state. [emig-2020-big-data] and [peronnet-1989-running-model] agree structurally: sustainable power falls roughly with the log of duration, with an athlete-specific slope.
2. **Marathon prediction from other performances carries about 5-8% error even with big data.** Half-to-marathon regression: MAE 5.9% [oficial-casado-2026-valencia-vdot]. CS from training logs: 7.7-8.0% [smyth-2020-cs-training-data]. Adding durability: 6.45% to 5.16% [smyth-2022-durability-decoupling]. For a 4 h marathon this is 12-19 min, so predictions must be presented as ranges.
3. **Durability (fade) is the main missing variable at marathon and beyond.** CS fraction sustained falls with slower ability (93% at 150 min, 79% at 360 min) [smyth-2020-cs-training-data]; high-decoupling runners ran 82% CS versus 88% [smyth-2022-durability-decoupling]; [maunder-2021-durability] makes the conceptual case. Starting too fast is penalised [smyth-2018-pacing-start-end].
4. **Heat slows everyone, more so slower marathoners, and in ultras hurts faster runners proportionally more** [ely-2007-weather-marathon], [parise-2011-161km-pacing-heat].

### What is contested or weak

- **Does Daniels VDOT beat a simple regression?** The only independent comparison found says similar overall accuracy, with ability-dependent divergence not described in the abstract [oficial-casado-2026-valencia-vdot]. Single-event, single-city data.
- **Whether a long-run decoupling measure predicts race fade.** Smyth 2022 measures decoupling during the race. The proxy is a proposal, not a validated result.
- **ML models.** [dash-2024-generalized-prediction] reports 90.4% versus 80% Riegel with only 15 athletes and an undefined metric. Hypothesis-generating only.
- **Trail prediction.** [ehrstrom-2018-short-trail] shows road physiology predicts a 27 km trail race poorly (R = 0.48), but n = 9 and the R = 0.98 model is overfit.

### What is unknown

No validated model in this corpus predicts 50 km to 100-mile trail times from training logs with a stated error. No ITRA/UTMB index methodology was retrieved (grey literature, not looked up). Fatigue-adjusted downhill and uphill speed, night and sleep effects, altitude, age and sex grading beyond marathon, and calibration of prediction intervals are all absent. Minetti's cost curve is fresh-state, treadmill, n = 10 [minetti-2002-gradient-cost].

### How the engine should use this

1. **Model hierarchy.** Fit per-athlete parameters (power-law exponent or endurance index plus a speed index) when at least two efforts at different durations exist; fall back to Riegel with a flag when only one exists. Choose CP only when efforts span about 3-30 min; otherwise power law/Peronnet [ruizalias-2023-long-duration-power].
2. **Always output a range** for any race at or above the marathon, with half-width at least the cited MAE, widening for ultras, trail, missing recent race data and heat.
3. **Marathon and ultra: add durability.** Estimate decoupling from long runs, flag it as an unvalidated proxy, and shade predicted pace down for high-decoupling athletes.
4. **Environment and course.** Apply a WBGT penalty (cap 5-25 C), scaled up for slower runners, and a segment-level heat penalty for ultras. For trail, convert elevation profile to flat-equivalent distance using the Minetti cost curve with a capped downhill benefit and lower stated confidence.
5. **Ultras.** Anchor on marathon PB (r = 0.72 at 100 km; r = -0.58 at 24 h) with weekly km and longest run as covariates [knechtle-2011-100km-predictors], [knechtle-2011-24h-ultra]. Do not code the published 24-hour equation.
6. **Back-test.** Because published ultra accuracy is weak, log each prediction and compare it to the athlete's actual result; recalibrate per athlete.

Numeric thresholds here come from specific datasets (mostly road marathoners). Grades below say how far each can be pushed.

## Gaps

- No peer-reviewed primary source for Daniels VDOT or the original Riegel 1981 paper was retrieved; the only VDOT benchmark is a single Valencia half-to-marathon comparison whose VDOT-specific error is not in the abstract.
- The ITRA performance index and UTMB index methodology (grey literature) were not retrieved or verified, so nothing in this corpus supports mapping them to finish times.
- No validated model predicts 50 km to 100-mile trail times from training logs with a stated error; Dash 2024 is n = 15 with an undefined accuracy metric.
- Long-run decoupling as a proxy for race-day decoupling (Smyth 2022 measures it during the race) is untested.
- Fatigue-adjusted uphill and downhill speed, technical terrain, night and sleep loss, altitude and nutrition effects on ultra pace have no quantitative support here; Minetti 2002 is fresh-state, n = 10, treadmill.
- Heat penalty multiplier for slower runners and for humidity, sun and wind beyond WBGT is not quantified; Ely 2007 compares to course records and states no sample size.
- Sex-specific and age-specific ultra prediction (female runners, masters) and the Vickers model coefficients were not available from abstracts; the Vickers MSE units are not stated.
- Calibration of prediction intervals (what share of results fall inside a stated range) is not reported by any paper in this corpus.
- Maunder 2021: only the first three Crossref authors were seen; the last two author names in the entry (Kilding, Plews) were not confirmed against Crossref.
