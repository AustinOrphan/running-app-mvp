# Durability and cardiovascular drift

## What the evidence establishes

**Fresh-state profiles are optimistic late in a long run.** Threshold speed, VO2max, economy and fractional utilisation all deteriorate during prolonged exercise, and this deterioration differs a lot between people. This is the core of the durability and resilience concept [maunder-2021-durability][jones-2024-physiological-resilience]. The best running-specific numbers are laboratory ones. After 90 min at threshold pace, mean threshold speed fell from 12.8 to 12.1 km/h (about 5.5%) in recreational marathoners [hunter-2025-marathoners-durability-slt]. After 90 min at 90% of LT1 speed, LT1 speed fell by 5.3 to 5.8% [nuuttila-2025-recreational-runners-durability]. The cycling-derived figure of roughly 10% critical-power loss after 2 h of heavy exercise, with a range of under 1% to about 32%, is consistent with this [jones-2024-physiological-resilience]. Heart rate at fixed easy pace rose about 5.5 to 5.9% over the same 90 min [nuuttila-2025-recreational-runners-durability].

**Durability relates to performance, and is partly trainable by association.** Smaller threshold decline went with faster marathons (r=0.68, n=18) [hunter-2025-marathoners-durability-slt]. In 82,303 marathon runners, internal-to-external decoupling magnitude and onset were associated with finishing time, and adding them to critical-speed models cut prediction error from 6.45% to 5.16% [smyth-2022-durability-decoupling]. Runners who do regular 90-min-plus long runs showed less economy decay than performance-matched runners who do not (+3.1% vs +6.0% at 90 min) [zanini-2026-long-runs-economy-durability]. Higher-performing runners decayed less than lower-performing runners [zanini-2024-re-durability-quantification]. The only randomised evidence is a 10-week heavy strength plus plyometric RCT in 28 trained men, which improved economy durability and post-run time to exhaustion [zanini-2025-strength-economy-durability-rct].

**Heat and hydration drive drift.** At 35 C, HR rose 17 to 19% between 15 and 45 min while stroke volume fell and VO2max declined in step, so the HR rise reflects rising relative intensity, not just noise [wingo-2020-drift-vo2max-heat-running]. Drift scales almost linearly with dehydration [montain-1992-dehydration-drift], and fluid intake preserved stroke volume and VO2peak over 2 h in the warm [ganio-2006-fluid-vo2peak-drift]. Both are cycling studies.

## What is contested or weak

- **Does drift track fitness?** Evidence is thin. Raw HR rise did not correlate with performance in marathoners (r=0.01); drift expressed as cardiac cost did, but only weakly (r=0.18), and pacing confounds it [billat-2020-cardiac-cost-marathon]. HR rise was not reported as a correlate of threshold decline [nuuttila-2025-recreational-runners-durability]. Decoupling has the strongest field evidence [smyth-2022-durability-decoupling], but it is cross-sectional, uses estimated HRmax and CS, and has no heat or terrain control. No paper in this set shows that within-athlete changes in free-living drift follow within-athlete changes in aerobic fitness.
- **Sex.** Males decoupled more and earlier [smyth-2022-durability-decoupling], and highly trained women lost less time-trial speed over 3 h (-1% vs -10%) [jaen-carrillo-2026-sex-differences-runners]. The latter study used 22 runners and unmatched distance, so the effect size is not transferable.
- **Mechanisms and measures.** DFA-a1 and respiratory frequency drift repeatably in cycling [rogers-2025-dfa-hr-drift-durability], but the only running link to threshold change is r=0.46 [nuuttila-2025-recreational-runners-durability].
- **Training prescriptions.** Late race-pace or progressive long-run work is a hypothesis in a narrative review [jones-2025-resilience-training].

## What is unknown

Behaviour beyond 3 h in running is largely uncharacterised. In ultra-duration exercise, drift reverses and then changes character [mattsson-2011-late-drift-ultra], so short-duration priors do not extrapolate. There is no quantified temperature correction for drift, no running-specific hydration dose-response, no age or masters data in this set, and no validated way to separate fitness-driven from pacing-, heat- or hydration-driven drift in free-living data [hunter-2025-durability-methods].

## How the engine should use this

1. **Store a durability descriptor per athlete** (onset, magnitude), initialised from population priors and overwritten by the athlete's own comparable sessions. Priors: about 5.5% threshold-speed decline at 90 min, wide spread, larger for slower or less-trained runners.
2. **Compute decoupling per long run** as cardiac cost relative to an early reference segment, tag each run with duration, relative intensity, temperature, fluid and fuelling, and compare only comparable runs [hunter-2025-durability-methods]. Use Smyth bands (1.1, 1.2) only for marathon-length efforts.
3. **Never move paces or zones off a single drift value.** Require several comparable runs, and attribute slowing-driven drift to pacing before fitness [billat-2020-cardiac-cost-marathon].
4. **In heat, steer by pace and RPE**, flag hot or low-fluid runs, and exclude them from durability updates.
5. **Plan structure:** weekly or fortnightly long runs of 90 min or more for half-marathon and longer goals, optional late-run intensity in marathon blocks, and two weekly strength sessions in a block of at least 10 weeks for trained runners.
6. **Ultras:** apply no drift-based fitness inference past roughly 3 to 4 h; use pace, RPE and fuelling.

Every numeric default beyond the cited laboratory means is a conservative engineering choice and is graded D.

## Gaps

- No running study showing that within-athlete changes in free-living HR drift or decoupling track within-athlete changes in aerobic fitness (VO2max, threshold) over a training block.
- No quantified temperature correction for running drift or decoupling; the heat evidence is short (45 min) and from seven men.
- No running-specific hydration dose-response for drift; hydration evidence is from cycling at 30-33 C.
- No evidence on durability and drift in female runners across menstrual cycle or hormonal status, or in masters/older runners; sex data come from one 22-person lab study and one race cohort.
- Durability beyond 3 h of running and in ultras (including 100-mile racing) is unmeasured in this set; the only ultra-duration drift data are cycling-based and show non-monotonic drift.
- No intervention study testing late-run race-pace or progressive long-run blocks on durability in runners; Matomaki 2023 (Front Physiol, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2023.1128111) shows durability improving with both low- and high-intensity cycling training in untrained adults but was not added to the corpus.
- Terrain, grade-adjusted pace and altitude effects on decoupling were not controlled in the large race cohort.
- The relation between decoupling proxies and laboratory threshold decline (DFA-a1 r=0.46 aside) has not been reported.
