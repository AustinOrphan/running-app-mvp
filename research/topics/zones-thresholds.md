# Intensity zones, thresholds and HRmax

## What the evidence establishes

**HRmax prediction.** Age explains most of the variation in group-mean HRmax and the best-supported population equations are 208 - 0.7 x age [tanaka-2001-hrmax-equation], 207 - 0.7 x age (longitudinal) [gellish-2007-longitudinal-hrmax] and 211 - 0.64 x age [nes-2013-hunt-hrmax]. All three agree that 220 - age underestimates HRmax in people older than about 30. Sex, fitness and BMI did not materially change the age relationship in the two large datasets. The equations are population means only: the largest sample reports an SEE of 10.8 bpm [nes-2013-hunt-hrmax], and a graded-test comparison found wide limits of agreement for every equation plus proportional bias (low maxima under-predicted, high maxima over-predicted) [shookster-2020-hrmax-accuracy]. Nes differed significantly from measured HRmax in that sample; Tanaka and Gellish did not, so Tanaka is the default prior. Nes runs 4-7 bpm higher than Tanaka between ages 20 and 60. The engine should treat any predicted HRmax as a low-confidence placeholder with about +/-11 bpm error.

**Zone concepts.** Fixed %HRmax or %HRR places individuals at non-equivalent metabolic intensities [mann-2013-relative-intensity, jamnick-2020-intensity-methods]. The better-supported structure is a three-domain model with LT1/VT1 (moderate to heavy) and critical speed (heavy to severe) as boundaries; Jamnick finds CS/CP is the only common method with good evidence for the heavy-severe boundary. In well-trained runners CS sat about 1.2 km/h above MLSS, and VO2 was stable 0.5 km/h below CS but reached VO2max 0.4 km/h above it [nixon-2021-cs-vs-mlss]. Cycling data show RCP, CP, MLSS and the deoxyhemoglobin breakpoint share the same VO2 but not the same power [keir-2015-exercise-intensity-thresholds], so the upper boundary should be a range, not a point. Faude's lactate review supports a two-breakpoint structure but reports that individual threshold-to-MLSS error is rarely quantified [faude-2009-lactate-thresholds]. The CP/W' (CS/D') concept itself is well established, mostly in cycling [poole-2016-critical-power].

**CS from training data.** Grade-adjusted best efforts between 400 m and 5 km from GPS logs give a linear distance-time relationship, and CS from them predicts marathon performance with about 8% error [smyth-2020-cs-training-data]. This makes automatic CS estimation feasible for the engine.

**DFA a1.** In small laboratory studies a chest-strap DFA a1 of 0.75 matches gas-exchange VT1 on average (HR 154 vs 152 bpm) [rogers-2021-dfa-a1-aerobic-threshold], but individual limits of agreement are about +/-11 bpm and the relationship degrades after fatigue (bias -12 bpm) [vanhooren-2023-dfa-a1-runners]. In a 6 h simulated ultramarathon DFA a1 at a fixed pace fell from 0.71 to 0.32 while HR barely changed [rogers-2021-dfa-a1-ultramarathon-fatigue]. The 0.5 value as upper threshold is tentative: 6 of 21 athletes could not finish 20 min at that speed [gronwald-2024-dfa-a1-prolonged]. Artifact levels below about 3% introduce minimal bias [rogers-2022-dfa-a1-update].

**Perceived-effort proxies.** Borg RPE 10-11 and the equivocal talk-test stage reflect VT1; RPE 13-15 and a negative talk test reflect the RCP [bok-2022-subjective-intensity]. This is review-level evidence and not runner-specific.

## What is contested

Whether threshold-anchored prescription produces better training adaptation than %HRmax is not demonstrated; Mann and Jamnick both say training studies are needed. Whether heart rate reserve beats %HRmax is also unsupported (similar lactate variation shown). Shookster's suggestion that 220 - age is least biased conflicts with the much larger datasets for older adults; the engine should side with the larger data but still not trust any equation individually. Whether DFA a1 0.5 marks VT2 or an unsustainable zone is contested.

## What is unknown

No paper in this corpus gives: runner-specific HRmax error; effects of temperature, altitude, sex hormones or age on thresholds; field-test validation for runners (time trials, Conconi-type, 3-min all-out); D' reconstitution kinetics for interval design; CS validity beyond the marathon; any threshold validity during ultra-distance running.

## How the engine should use this

1. HRmax: use observed maxima when available; otherwise 208 - 0.7 x age flagged low confidence with +/-10.8 bpm.
2. Zone anchors in priority order: lab or field-derived thresholds, GPS-derived CS plus HR/RPE proxies for LT1/VT1, DFA a1 (optional, fresh state, chest strap), then %HRmax or HRR fallback. Always record which source defined each boundary and show boundaries as ranges.
3. Hard sessions: base on CS, budgeting work above CS against D'; keep sustained threshold work at or slightly below CS and never at CS + 0.4 km/h or higher.
4. Marathon pacing: flag first-half pace above about 94% of CS; no equivalent rule exists for 50 km to 100 miles. For ultra goals use RPE and HR anchored to LT1/VT1 and fall back to conservative defaults (grade D).
5. Recompute thresholds from rolling recent data and exclude fatigued or late-in-run samples.

## Gaps

- No runner-specific HRmax error data or HRmax equations validated in endurance or ultra runners; no evidence on how temperature, heat, altitude, dehydration or cardiac drift shift HR-based zones.
- No verified Karvonen/HRR zone cut-offs and no head-to-head evidence that HRR beats %HRmax; HRR percentages for zone boundaries cannot be justified from this corpus.
- Field tests for runners (e.g. 3-min all-out, 30-min time trial, 3/12 min CS tests, Conconi-type) were not located or verified in this pass; only GPS best-effort CS (Smyth 2020) is covered.
- Critical speed and D' validity beyond the marathon is unknown (50 km to 100 miles); D' reconstitution kinetics for interval design and the effect of training status on D' are not covered.
- Threshold behaviour and durability during ultra-distance running (threshold drift with fatigue, glycogen depletion) is only indirectly addressed by one 6 h simulation with n = 7; broader durability literature (e.g. Maunder 2021, found in search but not verified in this pass) is not included.
- No evidence on sex- and age-specific thresholds (menstrual cycle, masters athletes), or on how thresholds are altered by temperature.
- Training-outcome trials comparing threshold/CS-anchored versus %HRmax-anchored prescription are absent; all zone-validity evidence is acute physiology.
- DFA a1 validity is limited to small laboratory studies; no evidence for women-specific, older-athlete, race-condition or wrist/optical-sensor validity; no validation of the 0.5 value as VT2.
- Intensity-distribution evidence (e.g. polarised versus threshold training, Seiler & Kjerland 2006 found in search) belongs to another topic and was not verified here.
- Mapping between HR, pace and RPE zone systems (three-zone vs five-zone conversions) has no verified source in this corpus; mappings must be defined by the engine and labelled as engineering conventions.
