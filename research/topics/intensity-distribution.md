# Training intensity distribution

## What is established

**Most training time is easy.** Every runner-specific source agrees that low intensity dominates. Elite and world-class runners keep at least 80% of total running volume at low intensity across the year [haugen-2022-world-class-runners]. A 175-TID review found more than 60% low intensity in 91% of cases, and above 80% zone 1 with under 12% zone 2 in running and most other endurance sports [sperlich-2023-tid-phases]. Race-pace-anchored zones in seven world-class runners gave about 88/7/4 [kenneally-2021-race-pace-vs-physiological]. A systematic review of runners suggests at least 70% low intensity [campos-2022-tid-runners-review]. The engine can treat "zone 1 dominant" as the best-supported rule in this topic.

**Threshold-dominant is the distribution to avoid as a default.** The only RCT that held the high-intensity dose constant found that cutting zone 2 from about 25% to about 12% of time (with zone 1 raised from 67% to 80%) gave a larger 10 km improvement over 5 months, with only 12 runners [esteve-lanao-2007-tid-runners]. Pooled RCT data favour polarized over threshold [rosenblat-2019-pol-vs-thr], and reviews of runners say the same [kenneally-2018-periodization-tid-review]. Even so, some elite marathoners use substantial threshold work, so this supports a default and not a ban.

**Polarized versus pyramidal is not settled, and probably does not matter much for performance.** The 2024 meta-analysis found polarized better for VO2peak (SMD 0.24) only in blocks under 12 weeks and in highly trained athletes. Time-trial, time-to-exhaustion and threshold-speed outcomes showed no difference [oliveira-2024-pol-meta]. The 2025 individual-participant network meta-analysis found no polarized-versus-pyramidal difference for VO2max or time trial. Its exploratory subgroup suggests competitive athletes respond better to polarized and recreational athletes to pyramidal [rosenblat-2025-tid-ipd-nma]. The early positive RCTs [stoggl-2014-polarized-rct] gave much larger effects than pooled data, and the recreational-runner trial missed its primary endpoint [munoz-2014-polarized-recreational].

## What is contested or weak

- **Phase shifting.** The best direct evidence is one 16-week trial in well-trained men with a 5 km goal, where pyramidal followed by polarized (8+8 weeks, load constant) beat the other orders [filipas-2022-pyr-pol-periodization]. Reviewers recommend the same shift for elite runners [casado-2022-elite-runners-review], and observational data show a higher heavy-to-severe share in competition than preparation [sperlich-2023-tid-phases]. This is plausible but rests on one RCT plus observation.
- **Labels depend on the anchor.** The same 50 weeks of training were pyramidal by race-pace zones and polarized or pyramidal by physiological zones [kenneally-2021-race-pace-vs-physiological]. "Polarized" is not a well-defined property unless the anchor is named.
- **Counting method.** The same training is about 96% zone 1 by time and about 87% by session goal in skiers [sylta-2014-tid-quantification]. Session-count "80/20" and time-based "80/20" are different prescriptions. Session RPE and HR time-in-zone agreed closely in one skier sample [seiler-2006-tid-quantification]. Systematic reviews recommend using more than one method [campos-2022-tid-runners-review].
- **Adherence.** The one positive recreational result came from a post-hoc subset of runners who actually executed the planned distribution [munoz-2014-polarized-recreational].

## What is unknown

There is no TID trial in ultra or 100-mile runners. The only ultra-specific data here are race-day intensity (about 86% of a 65 km race below VT1) and the finding that lab physiology explains only 59% of race-time variance [fornasiero-2018-ultra-intensity]. How TID should scale with weekly volume, with age, and by sex is also unstudied in a way that supports numbers: the pooled data are about 85% male and mostly under 42 years [rosenblat-2025-tid-ipd-nma]. Environmental effects (heat, altitude) on zone boundaries are outside this evidence.

## How the engine should use this

1. Use a three-zone internal model anchored on LT1/VT1 and LT2/VT2 (or HR/pace proxies), and store a planned zone for every session so time-in-zone and session-goal views can both be derived.
2. Default to a pyramidal, zone-1-dominant plan. Target roughly 80% zone 1, at most about 12% zone 2 in base, and about 8% zone 3 (a range, not a point). Treat these as priors with an uncertainty band [sperlich-2023-tid-phases].
3. Choose polarized only when the athlete is competitive and the block is short (under 12 weeks) with a VO2max or 5-10 km aim, or in the specific phase of a short-event plan. Otherwise stay pyramidal. Do not display claims that either is superior.
4. Shift the distribution toward more race-pace and high-intensity time as the A-race approaches, without raising total load at the transition. For marathon and longer events keep the shape pyramidal and move the race-pace work into the zone 2 band [casado-2022-elite-runners-review].
5. For ultra goals, no TID evidence exists. Use conservative defaults (grade D): at least 85% zone 1 time, a small zone 3 share, and race-day pacing below LT1.
6. Report TID with its anchor and its counting method every time, and show executed versus planned TID to the athlete.

Elite volumes (135-220 km per week) do not transfer to recreational athletes; use the proportions, not the absolute volumes.

## Gaps

- No TID intervention or observational study in ultra and 100-mile runners; the ultra rule is an extrapolation from one 65 km race-intensity paper and elite 80%+ low-intensity data.
- No quantitative evidence for how TID should scale with weekly volume; low-volume recreational runners (3-5 runs per week) are essentially unstudied, so the time-budget rule is an engine default.
- Pooled data are about 85% male and mostly under 42 years; no usable evidence on sex differences, masters athletes or menstrual-cycle effects on TID response.
- Heat, altitude, terrain and vertical gain effects on zone boundaries and TID are not covered by this evidence.
- Only one RCT (Filipas 2022, well-trained men, 5 km) supports a within-plan phase shift; no trial for marathon or ultra periodization.
- TID conversion factors between time-in-zone and session-goal come from skiers on a 14-day camp, not runners.
- Most TID outcomes are VO2max and short time trials over 4-16 weeks; no trial evidence on injury risk, adherence or long-term outcomes by TID.
- Searches for 2025-2026 updates beyond Rosenblat 2025 found no further runner-specific TID meta-analysis; the Burnley/Jones critique of polarized training (MSSE 2022) and the Foster response were found but their abstracts were not retrieved and they were not added.
