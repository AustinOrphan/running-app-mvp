# Sex differences and the female athlete

## What is established

**Cycle phase is not a usable prescription input.** The best pooled evidence finds only a trivial early-follicular dip in performance (ES0.5 = -0.06, credible interval spanning zero), low study quality, and an explicit conclusion that general guidelines cannot be formed [mcnulty-2020-menstrual-cycle-performance]. A stricter review limited to hormone-verified studies finds 19 small studies, inconsistent direction and no quantitative synthesis [schlie-2025-menstrual-cycle-high-standards]. Oral contraceptive use shows the same picture: any group-level effect is most likely trivial, and the review does not support general guidance [elliottsale-2020-oral-contraceptives-performance]. The engine should therefore apply no cycle-phase or contraceptive multiplier to load, intensity or pacing.

**Sex differences in ultra performance are physiological background, not a coefficient.** Running economy and fraction of VO2max sustained do not differ by sex; females show a more even pacing strategy, greater fat use and less fatigue after endurance running, offset by lower O2-carrying capacity [besson-2022-sex-differences-endurance-running]. After a 110 km race, males lost more knee-extensor force (-38% vs -29%) and showed more plantar flexor peripheral fatigue, but central fatigue was equal and n = 20 [temesi-2015-female-neuromuscular-fatigue]. Fatigability differences are task specific [hunter-2014-sex-differences-fatigability]. None of this justifies sex-specific zones, recovery lengths or long-run caps.

**Energy availability, bone and iron are the places where the female athlete (and, notably, the male ultra runner) needs risk flagging.** The IOC 2023 consensus frames REDs for both sexes and introduces a risk-stratified clinical tool, but it is clinician-facing and gives no consumer thresholds in its abstract [mountjoy-2023-reds-consensus]. LEAF-Q has 78% sensitivity and 90% specificity in 45 endurance athletes [melin-2014-leaf-q]. Bone stress injury risk accumulates with factors: 12 h/week or more of exercise, BMI below 21, low BMD, menstrual disturbance and dietary restraint took incidence from about 15-20% to 30-50% in 259 young women [barrack-2014-bone-stress-triad]; a systematic review in female trail runners lists previous BSI, disrupted menstruation, triad risk and low intake [stennett-2025-bsi-female-offroad-runners]. In 100-mile runners, 62.5% of women and 44.5% of men had elevated disordered-eating risk, and low BMD was more common in men [hoeg-2022-ultramarathon-triad]. Iron deficiency affects about 15-35% of female athlete cohorts versus 5-11% of male [sim-2019-iron-athlete], and treating non-anaemic iron deficiency raised VO2max (g = 0.61) [burden-2015-iron-treatment-meta].

## What is contested

Direction and size of cycle effects; whether luteal-phase injury excess is real (one football cohort of 26 players, wide intervals, [barlow-2024-injury-menstrual-cycle-footballers]); how much female fatigue resistance matters for recovery; and how well questionnaire screens perform outside their development samples. A conflicting-evidence claim in the researcher's Barlow entry about ovulatory laxity was not verified and was removed.

## What is unknown

No validated sex-specific load metric (ACWR, TRIMP, HR zones, session RPE scaling) was found in this topic. No evidence quantifies how much to reduce progression when REDs, BSI or iron flags are present. Postpartum guidance rests on expert Delphi consensus [deering-2024-postpartum-return-to-running] and a descriptive survey (median 12 weeks to first run, associations with pelvic symptoms) [moore-2021-postpartum-return-to-running]; the Goom 2019 guideline is grey literature and was not verified. Perimenopause, menopause, pregnancy and transgender athletes are not covered by the verified papers.

## How the engine should use this

1. Keep sex and cycle phase out of load, zone and recovery arithmetic. Derive everything from the individual's own data.
2. For HRV or readiness features, use the individual rolling baseline of about one cycle and widen alert bands so 3-9% cyclic variation is not read as stress; show a caveat for female and contraceptive users [dejager-2026-wearable-hrv-menstrual].
3. Offer optional cycle and symptom logging as awareness only, surfacing a personal pattern after 3 or more cycles.
4. Run a non-diagnostic REDs/bone/iron risk screen for all users, with extra prompts for female, high-volume and ultra users; the engine holds progression and recommends a clinician when flags accumulate. Numeric thresholds (2 flags, a progression cap) are design defaults at grade D, and they should be documented as such in the UI and code.
5. Provide a postpartum mode driven by symptom gates, not dates.
6. Never output diagnoses, supplement advice or calorie targets from wearable data.

## Gaps

- No validated sex-specific training load metric (ACWR, TRIMP, session-RPE scaling, HR zone or HRmax equations) was found for running; rules therefore apply no sex adjustment.
- No evidence gives numeric energy-availability thresholds for self-tracking users; the abstract-level sources contain none (clinical EA cut-offs exist in the full consensus but were not verified here).
- No data quantify how much to slow progression when REDs, BSI or iron flags are present; the 2-flag threshold and 50% cap are conservative design defaults, not evidence.
- No ferritin thresholds or dosing rules verified for athletes; Sim 2019 is a narrative review, not a consensus statement, and its abstract has none.
- Goom 2019 postnatal return-to-running guideline is grey literature and could not be verified through Crossref, Europe PMC or PubMed; postpartum rules rest on the 2024 Delphi and a descriptive survey.
- Perimenopause and menopause, pregnancy training, lactation effects on training, and transgender or non-binary athletes are not covered by verified papers.
- Sex differences in heat tolerance and thermoregulation in ultra running were mentioned in Besson 2022 but no quantitative evidence was extracted.
- Cycle-phase evidence is dominated by non-runners (football, lab tests); no hormone-verified running-specific data, and little on elite or master athletes.
- Barlow 2024 Crossref lookup returned no record at fetch time; metadata was verified through Europe PMC only and should be re-checked.
