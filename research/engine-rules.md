# Engine Rules: Evidence-Based Training Plan Engine

Version 1, 2026-10-22. This document integrates 20 topic syntheses into one rule set. It is decision support for one recreational runner. It is not medical advice.

## How to read this document

- **Grades.** A = consistent meta-analyses or consensus statements. B = RCTs or large prospective cohorts. C = small trials, observational data, or a reasonable extrapolation from strong evidence. D = convention or engineering default. A grade D rule has no direct evidence; it was chosen to be conservative and must be labelled as a default in the code and the UI.
- **Units.** The rules state values in km, min and C to keep them readable. The engine stores distance in metres, duration in seconds, plan targets in minutes, and dates as `YYYY-MM-DD` in local days, with weeks starting on Monday (ADR 0003).
- **Conflicts.** Where two topics disagree, the rule says **Conflict** and explains which side wins and why.
- **Terrain.** The terrain-elevation topic was verified in a second pass on 2026-10-22 (17 papers). Its rules are folded into H4, H5, I11, I12 and L5 below; see `research/topics/terrain-elevation.md`.
- **Second pass on five questions.** On 2026-10-22 five targeted reviews (70 papers and practice sources, added to the corpus) revisited the start of a plan, the session spike and weekly ramp, post-race recovery, the longest ultra training run and the 5-10 km long run. Their rules are folded into A5, A9, D4, D7, E6, F5, G-1, G-2, G-3, G-5, H1, H2, H3, H12, J3 and J7 below; see `research/topics/beginners-return.md`, `injury-load.md`, `periodization-recovery.md`, `ultra-training.md` and `volume-performance.md`. Coach plans, programme pages and magazine articles are kept as grade D conventions: they may set a default, never raise a grade.
- **Tissue and load-change papers.** On 2026-10-25 six papers on tendon time courses, bone stress injury and the load-capacity framework were added (376 papers). They are recorded under G-2 and J7, in `research/topics/injury-load.md` and in the evidence gaps below. They are evidence for caution, grade D at most for any rule that uses them, and no number in this document or in `shared/engine/params/` changed with them. Rule G-8, written with the history block, also cites them (grade D, as a whole).
- **Strength prescription.** On 2026-10-25 rules F7 to F9 were written for the strength epic (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-24-strength-phase3b-design.md`, ADR 0014): the structure of a strength session, the progression of each lift and the lighter days. Twenty papers were added in one gap pass on the evidence gaps of that design (396 papers): reviews and trials of progression models, repetitions in reserve, maintenance and stopping, women and masters athletes, calf raises, jumps, estimating a maximum and injury rates in the lifting sports. They are recorded in `research/topics/strength-plyometrics.md`, F7 to F9 below, the numeric table and the evidence gaps. They add no grade above D to any dose: no paper in the corpus compares ways of progressing a load in runners or novices (one synthesis in resistance-trained lifters found effort-based and percentage-based prescription similar), validates the estimate of a maximum in runners, tests a calf, jump or taper dose in runners, or studies runners over about 50 or 100-mile runners. The amendments to E5, F-lib, F4, H5 and H10 are dated where they stand.
- **Methods (section p).** On 2026-10-31 rules P1 to P4 were written for the methods layer (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-31-sources-methods-evaluation-proposal.md`, task M1): the default, a three-run-day preset, a shorter long run that is not offered, and a longer base. Nine records were added (406 papers: seven journal papers and two programme pages read in the source on 2026-10-31) and the topic `research/topics/training-methods.md` was written by hand. Every preset is grade D, a choice and not a better plan, and no `Param`, value, grade or `PARAMS_VERSION` changed in that task. Task M2 (2026-10-31) added the two `Param`s of P2 and P4 (`p2.runDays`, `p4.buildWeeks`, both grade D) and bumped `PARAMS_VERSION`; no value or grade of an existing `Param` changed.

## Global principles

- **G1 Deterministic core.** Sessions, volumes and paces come from rule code with schema validation. An LLM may only write explanatory text. Every plan must pass an automated quality checklist covering progression, recovery weeks, TID, taper and level differentiation before it is published. Grade C/D [duking-2024-chatgpt-running-plans, montaruli-2026-ai-marathon-programs].
- **G2 The FFM does not drive plans.** Fitness-fatigue models are only for monitoring and what-if views. Plan structure comes from the explicit rules below. Grade C [busso-2023-impulse-response-validity, hellard-2006-banister-limitations, marchal-2025-ffm-statistical-flaws].
- **G3 Priors, not optima.** Every target is a prior with an uncertainty band. Individual values move toward the athlete's own data by shrinkage, and only after at least 3 comparable observations (D). Grade C/D [hecksteden-2015-individual-response, mann-2014-high-low-responders].
- **G4 No responder labels.** If a block shows no improvement beyond measurement noise, change the stimulus before drawing any conclusion. Grade C [bonafiglia-2016-interval-response-variability].
- **G5 Copy discipline.** Targets are a sketch. The app never says "behind" or "missed" and never uses streaks or red states. It never claims that any limit prevents injury or that exceeding one causes injury, and it never shows a personal injury probability: all load evidence is observational. Fixed caps (D7, H2) are never presented as injury limits. A single lint (`shared/copy/lint.ts`) checks these words, exclamation marks and injury-probability phrases in every sentence the app writes from a template; text a provider returns is quoted, not linted, and a runner's own words (a goal name) are shown as typed. Grade B [buist-2008-graded-training, damsted-2018-training-load-change, wang-2024-target-trial-load, vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review, fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review].
- **G6 Safety first.** Rules never move toward more load when the evidence is weak. When rules collide, the more conservative one wins.

## (a) Athlete state estimation

- **A1 Separate stores.** Keep three separate stores: load (external: time, distance, gain, power; internal: sRPE, TRIMP), readiness (check-ins, HRV, resting HR, sleep) and performance (races, best efforts, CS). Moderators (age, sex, heat, sleep) are explicit inputs with wide uncertainty. Grade D [jeffries-2021-training-framework, impellizzeri-2019-internal-external-load, bourdon-2017-load-consensus].
- **A2 Chronic and acute load.** Use EWMA with a 7-day acute and a 42-day chronic time constant. These are a user-adjustable convention, shown as descriptive trends. Grade D [murray-2017-ewma-acwr, nakaoka-2021-acwr-runners].
- **A3 Longest recent run.** Keep the rolling 30-day maximum of completed runs, by distance and by duration. For a new user without logs, use the longest recent run they declare. A completed race of marathon distance or longer does not count (G-1). Grade B [frandsen-2025-session-spike].
- **A4 Consistency.**
  - Count gaps of 7 or more days with no running in the last 16 weeks. Grade B [feely-2022-training-disruptions].
  - Record the share of planned weeks completed; above 80% counts as consistent (D) [lepers-2021-sub3-marathon-decades].
  - Record years of regular running [hoffman-2013-ultra-exercise-behavior].
- **A5 Experience class.** Classify the athlete as novice, returning (by layoff length), recreational or trained.
  - Novice means no running history or no running in the prior few months. The exact definition is the engine's own (D); E6 sets the stricter test for the run-walk ladder.
  - Novices carry about 2.3 times the injury rate per hour (17.8 vs 7.7 per 1000 h). Grade A [videbaek-2015-incidence]. No previous running experience roughly doubles the hazard (HR 2.1-2.6). Grade B [buist-2010-rri-risk-factors-4-mile].
  - The class picks the start path in E6; sex never changes the dose (G-4).
  - Experience is not a reason to loosen limits: runners with under 1 year or over 40 years of running had the most injuries. Grade C [abrahamson-2025-self-reported-characteristics, vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review].
  - Logged frequency under 2 runs per week, averaged over the last 4 weeks, gives a runner above the E6 floor the novice ramp handling (time-based steps) and the 2:1 recovery cadence (E3, G-3). The cut-off is a design default. Grade D [abrahamson-2025-self-reported-characteristics].
- **A6 Fitness.**
  - Primary: CS from at least 3 best grade-adjusted efforts between 400 m and 5 km in a 90-day window. The window is D. Grade C [smyth-2020-cs-training-data].
  - With 2 or more races at different distances, fit a personal exponent. Grade C [blythe-2016-individual-prediction, vickers-2016-race-times].
  - Device VO2max is a smoothed trend only. Its limits of agreement are about ±10 ml/kg/min. Grade A [molinagarcia-2022-wearable-vo2max].
  - Do not use device kcal anywhere. Grade A [fuller-2020-wearables-systematic-review].
- **A7 Durability descriptor.**
  - Prior: threshold speed falls about 5.5% after 90 min, with a large spread. Grade C [hunter-2025-marathoners-durability-slt, nuuttila-2025-recreational-runners-durability].
  - Personalise only after 3 comparable long runs. Runs are comparable when duration is within 20%, ambient temperature is within 5 C, and the fuelling/fluid flag is the same (D) [hunter-2025-durability-methods].
  - Exclude runs at 25 C or warmer, and hot runs with no fluid or more than 2% body-mass loss. Grade C/D [wingo-2020-drift-vo2max-heat-running, montain-1992-dehydration-drift, ganio-2006-fluid-vo2peak-drift].
  - Make no drift-based fitness inference beyond 180 min. Grade D [mattsson-2011-late-drift-ultra].
- **A8 Efficiency index.** Track pace at a fixed HR, or HR at a fixed pace, on steady flat runs.
  - A rising index across weeks indicates adaptation.
  - A sustained fall with rising RPE is an overreaching signal. The threshold is athlete-relative; no cut-off is validated. Grade B [roete-2021-functional-overreaching-markers, nuuttila-2021-intensity-vs-volume-monitoring].
- **A9 Risk profile.** Record at onboarding, then update:
  - Recent-injury flag: any injury listed in the profile for the last 12 months, running-related or not, because the profile records no running-related field (A for the factor, which the studies measured for running injuries; the window is D) [hulme-2017-risk-protective, vanderworp-2015-risk-factors].
  - Previous BSI, with OR about 5. Grade A [wright-2015-stress-fracture-meta].
  - REDs flags [mountjoy-2023-reds-consensus].
  - BSI factor count [barrack-2014-bone-stress-triad].
  - Soft start flags: age 45 or over (no upper bound; 65 was only the enrolment limit of the source cohort), BMI above 30, any injury or musculoskeletal complaint listed in the profile for the last 12 months (running-related or not; the same test as the recent-injury flag), no running history, and under 2 years of running. Grade B for the factors, C for the age and BMI thresholds, D for the 12-month window and the 2 years [vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review, kluitenberg-2015-nlstart2run, relph-2023-couch-to-5k-injury, linton-2018-uk-novice-recreational-injury, nielsen-2013-novice-predictors]. Flags only make the start gentler (E6); none excludes anyone.
- **A10 FFM fitting.**
  - Fit only with at least 20 performance observations over 12 weeks (D).
  - Default to a fitness-only model. Enable the fatigue term or VDR only if forward-chaining CV lowers held-out error.
  - Benchmark against elastic net on EWMA features.
  - Never display time constants. Flag fits whose intervals are wider than 25 days.
  - Grade C [imbach-2022-generalisation, marchal-2025-ffm-statistical-flaws, hellard-2006-banister-limitations, busso-2003-variable-dose-response].

## (b) Load metrics per session

- **B1 Primary metric.** sRPE = minutes × CR-10. Ask for it on every run with fixed wording and visible anchors. Prompt about 30 min after the run; the timing is D and the rating stays editable. Grade C [foster-2001-session-rpe, haddad-2017-session-rpe-review, wallace-2014-load-methods-runners].
- **B2 Which metric when.**
  - **Chest-strap HR, or wrist HR on steady flat or treadmill runs that pass sample checks:** compute TRIMP alongside sRPE. Wrist bias is about −0.5 bpm on steady runs. Grade A [zhang-2020-wrist-ppg-meta, gillinov-2017-wearable-hr-aerobic].
  - **Wrist HR on hills, trail, intervals or non-running activity:** reject it for TRIMP and use sRPE. A Fenix 5 had 13% MAPE on a trail run. Grade C [navalta-2020-trail-running-hr, bent-2020-ppg-inaccuracy].
  - **Pace only (no HR, no RPE):** store external load only and derive the planned and executed zone from pace against CS. Internal load stays missing, not imputed. Grade C/D.
  - **RPE only:** use sRPE for both load and TID zone. Grade C [seiler-2006-tid-quantification].
- **B3 Sex-specific TRIMP.**
  - Banister TRIMP = duration × HRR fraction × weight. The exponent b is 1.92 for men and 1.67 for women, and the leading weights are 0.64 and 0.86.
  - These constants are unverified (Banister 1991 is not indexed). Show the formula. When sex is unspecified, use an explicit user setting, defaulting to the male constant. Grade D [wallace-2014-load-methods-runners, manzi-2009-individualized-trimp-marathon].
  - Individualised TRIMP from lactate or field-test data is opt-in. Grade C.
  - **Conflict:** the sex topic found no validated sex-specific load metric. Resolution: the Banister constants are part of the formula's definition, so they stay. They are the only sex term anywhere in load arithmetic.
- **B4 No cross-scale arithmetic.**
  - Never sum or convert sRPE and TRIMP. Use one metric per fit, store its id and version, and refit when it changes. Grade C [vermeire-2021-load-quantification, chen-2002-borg-rpe-meta].
  - Metric choice moved the implied timing of peak benefit by up to 19.6 days in [vermeire-2021-load-quantification].
- **B5 Divergence.** Show a session as divergent when the sRPE and HR-load z-scores, each against the athlete's own history, differ by more than 1.5 SD, with at least 8 prior sessions. Show it; never auto-correct. Grade D [chen-2002-borg-rpe-meta].
- **B6 External load hygiene.**
  - Use the device's own distance totals and smooth speed.
  - Treat elevation as noisy and prefer barometric or map-corrected values.
  - Down-weight pace-based intensity on slow climbs. Grade D [gloersen-2018-gnss-endurance].
  - Running power: Stryd is medium confidence and other devices are low. Never mix devices within a series. Grade C [cerezuela-2020-running-power-validity].
- **B7 Monotony and strain.** Show them descriptively, flagged only against the athlete's own range. Grade C [foster-1998-monotony-strain].
- **B8 Skin tone.** Apply no correction factor. Model quality per device and recommend a chest strap. Grade B [koerber-2022-skin-tone-wrist-hr].

## (c) Zones and thresholds

- **C1 Three-domain model.** LT1/VT1 separates the moderate and heavy domains. CS separates heavy and severe. The upper boundary is a range, because RCP, MLSS and CS fall at different speeds. Never define zones as fixed percentages of HRmax when a better anchor exists. Grade C [jamnick-2020-intensity-methods, nixon-2021-cs-vs-mlss, keir-2015-exercise-intensity-thresholds].
- **C2 Anchor hierarchy.**
  1. Tested thresholds or CS.
  2. Proxies: GPS CS, DFA a1, RPE or talk test.
  3. %HRmax.
  4. %HRR, only with a reliable resting HR; it is not shown to be better than %HRmax.

  Record the source of every boundary. Grade D [mann-2013-relative-intensity].

- **C3 HRmax.**
  - With no observation, use 208 − 0.7 × age, flagged as approximate with ±10.8 bpm. Never use 220 − age. Grade A [tanaka-2001-hrmax-equation, gellish-2007-longitudinal-hrmax, nes-2013-hunt-hrmax].
  - Replace it with an observed maximum: chest strap only, from a verified maximal effort, in a rolling window, never capped to the equation (D) [shookster-2020-hrmax-accuracy].
  - Age an old measurement down by 0.7 bpm per year. Grade C.
- **C4 LT1 when untested.** Use whichever is available:
  - Z1/Z2 boundary at 82.3% of CS. This is a data-derived cut-off from one large cohort. Grade C [muniz-pumares-2025-marathon-tid].
  - HR at DFA a1 = 0.75: chest strap only, fresh state, artefacts at or below 3%, ±11 bpm. Grade C [rogers-2021-dfa-a1-aerobic-threshold, vanhooren-2023-dfa-a1-runners, rogers-2022-dfa-a1-update].
  - Borg 10-11 RPE, or the equivocal talk test. Grade D [bok-2022-subjective-intensity].
- **C5 Continuous threshold work.**
  - Well-trained athletes, bouts of 30 min or less: at or below CS − 0.5 km/h. Grade C [nixon-2021-cs-vs-mlss].
  - Everyone else: at or below CS − 1.0 km/h. This roughly matches MLSS, which sits about 1.2 km/h below CS. Grade D.
  - Never schedule sustained work at DFA a1 = 0.5 speed. Grade C [gronwald-2024-dfa-a1-prolonged].
- **C6 Intervals above CS.** Each rep lasts at most 0.5 × the estimated time to exhaustion at that speed (the D′ budget). Grade D [poole-2016-critical-power].
- **C7 Boundaries as ranges.** Band widths are ±11 bpm (DFA), ±10.8 bpm (predicted HRmax), and otherwise 5 bpm or 5% of pace (D).
- **C8 Updating.**
  - Re-estimate on rolling data.
  - Exclude fatigued, late-in-run and heat-flagged samples [rogers-2021-dfa-a1-ultramarathon-fatigue].
  - Move a boundary only after 3 concordant observations (D).
  - Re-estimate paces from fresh data after any break of 2 weeks or more [coyle-1984-detraining-time-course].
  - Watch-reported LT pace is never an anchor; it overestimates by 12.7-25.8% MAPE. Watch LT HR is advisory. Grade C [lu-2025-smartwatch-lactate-threshold].

## (d) Goal modelling and feasibility

- **D1 Goal record.** Store distance, gain and loss, surface, altitude, continuous or staged, support level, date, start time and expected weather. An ultra is any race longer than 42.2 km. Rules branch on these descriptors, not on distance alone. Grade D [scheer-2020-defining-off-road-running].
- **D2 Goal type.** Each goal is either finish-first or a time goal. Volume floors apply only to time goals. For 100 miles and beyond, the default is finish-first. Grade C [knechtle-2018-ultra-physiology-review, coates-2021-physio-determinants-ultra].
- **D3 Floors for time goals** (soft; reach them only within the limits in section g):
  - Half-marathon: more than 32 km/week and a longest run above 21 km. Grade B [fokkema-2020-half-marathon-volume-long-run].
  - Marathon: at least 40 km/week in the build block and at least one long run of 25 km or more. Grade B, low confidence.
  - Marathon peak long run: 28-32 km, 1-3 times, only for athletes already at about 50 km/week. Grade D [doherty-2020-marathon-training-meta].
  - Typical session length of about 10 km or more before weekly volume becomes the main lever. Grade C [yamaguchi-2023-volume-frequency-run-distance].
  - The weekly floor is compared with the mean weekly distance of the last 4 completed weeks. Grade D (a default window, not from a paper).
  - The floors are targets within the limits of section g, and so within what the planner schedules. The long run the check works toward for a half or a marathon is the lesser of the floor and the planner's long run: the lesser of the class long-run peak and the long-run share (D7) of the larger of the class peak week and the start week, in minutes at the training pace. A time goal whose floor the planner does not schedule gets the note `long-run-floor` (info, D3), so the label never calls a run comfortable that the plan does not build. Grade D.
- **D4 Feasibility check** (D). From the current 30-day longest run L0 to the target long run Lt:
  - weeks needed ≈ ⌈ln(Lt/L0) / ln(1.10)⌉ × 4/3, which allows for cutback weeks, plus the taper.
  - Weekly volume to its floor uses the same formula with the weekly ramp.
  - The training pace used to turn kilometres into minutes is the median of the weekly paces over the last 4 weeks, leaving out a week without a run. Grade D (a default window, not from a paper).
  - The inputs follow the plan's start (E6, J7). L0 is the larger of the 30-day longest run and the first session of the start path (the 3 x N session, in minutes at the training pace). A run-walk ladder or an injury-return ladder adds its remaining weeks, which have no cutback week, and counts its last session (30 min of continuous running) only after them. A plan with no run in the last 30 days has no G-1 reference and opens with the long-run share of its start week (the typical week, or the restart volume after a break). The weekly floor grows from the first week of the start path or the restart volume, not from the minimum step.
  - Ultras: Lt is time on feet, the least of the class value (180 min for 50 km, 240 min for 100 km, 240 min for 100 miles, 270 min beyond), the H2 cap, and the long-run share × the larger of the class peak week and the start week. Feasibility therefore never counts weeks toward a run the planner would not schedule; with the defaults the plan reaches 175 min (50 km) and 216 min (100 km), so the target follows the plan. The class values are defaults at or below observed practice, and no longest-run level has been associated with finishing (161 km, P = 0.07; UTMB Strava, no difference). Grade D [outside-2023-strava-western-states, outside-2023-strava-utmb, outside-2015-orton-koop-100-miles, tan-2017-finishers-161km, taksaudom-2017-66km-predictors, baldea-2009-leadville-training, daniel-2024-217km-sleep-training].
  - Ultras: the weekly path grows from the start week with the weekly ramp toward the week that carries the target long run at the ultra long-run share (Lt / 0.45), capped at the class's peak weekly time. The peak itself is a ceiling the plan may never reach, not a requirement, so it is not the target. The start week is the larger of the typical week and the start-path floor week (E6), which matches what the generator does. The label uses the slower of the two paths, because weekly volume and frequency, not the longest run, are associated with ultra performance and DNF. The label never raises a limit or refuses a goal. Grade C for the principle, D for the peak weekly values [rust-2012-100km-vs-marathoners, belinchon-2021-mountain-race-predictors, tanda-2015-training-anthropometry-100km, hoffman-2011-wser-vt100-factors, sewry-2022-safer-xxi-dnf, soppelsa-2025-trail-injury-performance].
  - The weeks available are the plan's Monday weeks, from the Monday of the current week (week 1, taken as run) through the race week, so the label and the plan count the same weeks as the weeks needed (amended 2026-10-25: the check counted whole weeks from today, one or two fewer, and could read "not reachable" for a plan that fits). A label only; no grade, value or limit changes.
  - Result is one of three labels: "comfortable", "tight" or "not reachable within default limits". The last label offers a later race, a shorter distance or a finish goal.
  - Never refuse the goal, and never exceed the limits silently.
- **D5 Ultra readiness messages.** These are non-blocking and athlete-relative: weekly distance, sessions per week, goal pace against training pace. Grade B [sewry-2022-safer-xxi-dnf]. Two notes have fixed thresholds:
  - Average under 30 km/week over the last 12 weeks. Grade D [craddock-2020-acwr-two-oceans].
  - Fewer than 3 years of regular running before a first ultra. Grade D [hoffman-2013-ultra-exercise-behavior].
  - First-timer status alone is not a risk factor. Grade C [wegelin-2010-odds-finishing-161km].
- **D6 Race heat.** If forecast or climatological race WBGT is 18 C or higher (D), the macrocycle must include an acclimation block (section i).
- **D7 Long run for 5 km and 10 km goals** (D). The long run is about 0.35 of the week's running time, capped by a tier chosen from the runner's completed typical week (the median of the last 4 weeks with a run), never from the planned week:

  | Typical week  | 5 km cap | 10 km cap |
  | ------------- | -------- | --------- |
  | Under 180 min | 60 min   | 75 min    |
  | 180-360 min   | 75 min   | 90 min    |
  | Over 360 min  | 90 min   | 100 min   |
  - Both goals also cap the long run at 16 km (10 miles, the coaches' maximum), so the cap in minutes is the lesser of the tier cap and the minutes 16 km takes at the long-run pace, the pace the run is carried at (the middle of the zone 1 pace band when the zones carry pace, else the average training pace). Grade D [johnson-2018-long-runs-5k-10k, higdon-2024-advanced-10k].
  - No run of the week is longer than the long run, so a week holds at most its run days × the cap (6 × 90 = 540 min for a high-volume 5 km runner). The tier boundaries at 180 and 360 min keep run days × cap at or above the week at every tier top.
  - G-1 and G-2 bind every week; a runner reaches a cap only from their own history. The caps and the share are coaching conventions, not injury limits, and G-1 is the single-session guard (G5).
  - The caps and the share follow published plans and coaches, not studies [higdon-2024-advanced-5k, higdon-2024-intermediate-5k, higdon-2024-advanced-10k, johnson-2018-long-runs-5k-10k, runningmagazine-2023-daniels-long-run-limit]. No study of 5-10 km runners links long-run length to performance or injury [suwankan-2024-10km-time-prediction, knechtle-2023-nurmi-training-by-distance, fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review].
  - There is no minimum long run for 5-10 km goals. Weekly time comes first, and run days follow volume (F5). Grade C [suwankan-2024-10km-time-prediction, knechtle-2023-nurmi-training-by-distance].
  - New runners start on the E6 paths. During the ladder there is no separate long run; afterwards the long run grows from the longest recent continuous run under G-1, not from the share. No protection is claimed [buist-2008-graded-training, linton-2018-uk-novice-recreational-injury, vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review, higdon-2024-novice-5k].
  - **Conflict:** G6 says rules never move toward more load on weak evidence, and the medium and high caps are higher than the earlier flat 60 min (5 km) and 75 min (10 km) on grade D sources. Resolution: the caps are design ceilings, not safety limits. The earlier flat values were also grade D with no evidence, the safety limits (G-1, grade B; G-2, grade C) are unchanged and bind each long run, the tier comes from completed weeks so a plan cannot promote itself, and the 16 km cap holds the coaches' maximum. The flat cap had also limited weekly volume, which is the variable with supporting evidence.

## (e) Macrocycle

- **E1 Model.** Traditional sequence: base, build, specific, taper, with a hard-day/easy-day week. Grade B [casado-2022-elite-runners-review, gonzalezrave-2022-reverse-periodization].
  - Reverse periodization is offered only on request, with no benefit claimed.
  - Block periodization is opt-in and experimental, for trained athletes only. Grade C [molmen-2019-block-periodization-meta-analysis].
- **E2 Phase lengths.** Build the plan backwards from race day:
  1. Taper (section k).
  2. Specific phase of 6-8 weeks. The 8-week figure comes from the 5-10 km RCT design; every other length is D [filipas-2022-pyr-pol-periodization].
  3. Build.
  4. Base takes the remainder.

  Peak volume for an ultra falls about 3 weeks before the race. Grade D, n = 1 [byrne-2024-elite-24h-training]. Do not place a large overload block directly before the taper. Grade C [aubry-2014-overreaching-before-taper].

- **E3 Recovery weeks.**
  - Default 3:1. Use 2:1 for novices, post-injury athletes, athletes with the recent-injury flag, and while a fatigue flag is active.
  - Recovery-week volume drops 20-30%, keeping some intensity and frequency. Grade D [kiely-2018-periodization-critique, foster-1998-monotony-strain].
  - **Conflict:** the periodization topic assigns 2:1 to masters. The age topic says do not cut load by age alone, because reduced training mediates age-related decline. Both are grade D. Resolution: age does not select 2:1 by itself. A masters athlete gets 2:1 when the perceived-recovery gate (j6) or another flag fires [tanaka-2008-masters-endurance, borges-2018-masters-hiit-recovery].
- **E4 TID by phase.** All values are % of running time; anchor and counting method are always shown.

  | Phase    | 5 km-10 km                                                                                     | Half/marathon                              | Ultra                         |

  Hard rules: Z1 never below 70%; Z2 time never greater than Z1 time.

  Grades:
  - Z1 floor: B [haugen-2022-world-class-runners, sperlich-2023-tid-phases, esteve-lanao-2007-tid-runners, campos-2022-tid-runners-review].
  - Threshold cap: B [rosenblat-2019-pol-vs-thr].

- **E5 Fixed blocks.**
  - Strength: a 10-14 week heavy-plus-plyometric block in base and early build (A) [eihara-2022-heavy-vs-plyo, denadai-2017-explosive-heavy-economy, balsalobre-2016-running-economy-highly-trained, zanini-2025-strength-economy-durability-rct], then 1 maintenance session per week (D) [spiering-2021-minimal-dose, bickel-2011-maintenance-dosing-retention]. The session structure, the doses and the progression of the loads are F7 to F8, and the lighter weeks and the taper F9.
  - **Strength block (amended 2026-10-25).** The block is a property of the plan's weeks and not of a plan: a re-plan reads the latest run of consecutive weeks (adjacent Mondays) that the stored plan flags as block weeks (`strength-block`). A run that ends on the Monday before the current week is a block under way, and the block continues for the weeks it has left. An isolated flag earlier in the plan is not counted, and a run that ended earlier is a block that is over, which starts no other. The minimum of `e5.strengthBlockMinWeeks` applies only where the stored plan carries no block flag, a block that has not started. `macroPlan` makes this count (shipped 2026-10-25, task E14-3 of the strength epic): the block weeks that remain are `e5.strengthBlockWeeks` less the length of that run, and no more than the non-specific weeks left, so a plan that is re-planned every week holds `e5.strengthBlockWeeks` block weeks in all and not a new block at each re-plan. Before that change the block was counted from the first week of every plan, a plan re-planned every week met the first week of a block every week while `e5.strengthBlockMinWeeks` or more non-specific weeks remained, and the block lasted the non-specific weeks at the start less 7. A goal with fewer than `e5.strengthBlockMinWeeks` non-specific weeks ahead and no block under way gets maintenance only. For an ultra goal the block weeks and the sessions a week are the `h10` Params (H10). A regenerate builds a plan with no stored weeks and starts a new block; a runner with logged block sessions in each of the last `f7.blockCreditWeeks` weeks skips the ramp-in of the new block (a design default, F7). Grade D for the credit.
  - Gut training: 2-4 weeks inside the specific phase, ending before the race (B).
  - Heat acclimation: 7-14 days ending close to the race (A).
- **E6 Start of a plan** (the start level is under 3 × 20 min, or there are no logged runs). The path comes from history, not from one number. Sex never changes the dose (G-4). Every number below is a labelled default unless a grade says otherwise, and no study compares run-walk starts with continuous starts.
  1. **True novice:** experience novice with no run of 20 min or more ever logged, or no runs at all and no declared longest run. The run-walk ladder has 9 rungs on 3 non-consecutive days a week, with a 5-min walk before and after each session (the walks are not counted in the week):

     | Rung | Running per session (run / walk minutes)                 | Running minutes |
     | ---- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
     | R1   | 1 run / 2 walk × 7                                       | 7               |
     | R2   | 1.5 run / 2 walk × 6                                     | 9               |
     | R3   | (1.5 run, 1.5 walk, 3 run, 3 walk) × 2                   | 9               |
     | R4   | 3 run, 1.5 walk, 5 run, 2.5 walk, 3 run, 1.5 walk, 5 run | 16              |
     | R5   | three sessions: 5-3w-5-3w-5; 8-5w-8; 20 continuous       | 15, 16, 20      |
     | R6   | three sessions: 5-3w-8-3w-5; 10-3w-10; 22 continuous     | 18, 20, 22      |
     | R7   | 25 continuous                                            | 25              |
     | R8   | 28 continuous                                            | 28              |
     | R9   | 30 continuous (3 × 30 min, 90 min a week)                | 30              |

     Weekly time on feet is about 63, 63, 54, 64.5, 62, 69, 75, 84 and 90 min. Grade D: the shape adapts the NHS and the original Couch-to-5K plans, with the engine's 1:2 ratio kept at rung 1 (slightly gentler than the NHS week 1) and the gentler 22 min at rung 6 [nhs-2026-couch-to-5k, clark-2026-couch-to-5k-original, galloway-2026-beginner-run-walk, linton-2018-uk-novice-recreational-injury].

  2. **Returner with a continuous run of 20 min or more in the last 30 days:** 3 sessions of N min, where N is the longest continuous run of the last 30 days (logged, or declared when there are no logs, A3) rounded down to 5 min and kept between 20 and 30. N above 20 needs one of two routes; otherwise N = 20.
     - **Years.** At least 2 years of running (the A5 experience cut-off), no soft flag (age 45 or over, BMI above 30, any injury in the last 12 months), no tightened profile (an earlier bone stress injury, two BSI factors) and no recent-injury flag.
     - **Block (G-8).** Only at the standard and upper load positions: at least 1 year of running (`a5.noviceMaxYears`) and a history block of at least 3 × 30 = 90 min a week (`restartFloor.sessions` × `restartFloor.sessionMaxMin`) that ended within `g8.creditWindowWeeks` (12) weeks, whichever credit window ships. Every soft flag other than age still closes it (BMI above 30, any injury in the last 12 months, no running history), as do the tightened profiles and every profile G-8 holds at steady. Age 45 or over does not close this route: the flag warns, upper is read as standard, and the lift stays open; the years route keeps its age rule, so a runner of 45 or over can take the lift by a demonstrated block and not by calendar years. At steady the route does not exist. The route replaces a count of calendar years (a difference of calendar years: a start year before the current one, met from a few days to 24 months of running, whenever in the year the check is made) by a measured load, and the lift does not grow: N is still the longest continuous run of the last 30 days. Grade D.

     A session never exceeds the runner's own completed run, so G-1 stays inside its reference band. This is the only start rule that moves toward more load, and it is bounded by the runner's own run. Grade D [frandsen-2025-session-spike, pollock-1977-frequency-duration-injury, linton-2018-uk-novice-recreational-injury, abrahamson-2025-self-reported-characteristics]. In a 1977 trial 15-min and 30-min sessions three times a week had similar injury rates and 45 min did much worse, but the subjects were young men jogging hard and 20 min was not tested. Against the block route: it overturns the "2 years" detail of the engine design's first owner decision and sits against the corpus rule that nothing supports looser limits for experienced runners (G-1); it keys on a demonstrated load, not on experience, but still moves one class of runners toward more load, and the floor week is exempt from the G-2 ceiling up to its minutes, so a longer N makes the first week stand further above the recent weeks.

  3. **Returner without such a run but with a run in the last 90 days:** the reference is the longest run of the last 30 days when that window holds any run (the run G-1 reads), else the J7 fraction (by layoff length) times the longest run of the last 90 days. If the reference is 20 min or more, path 2 applies at N = 20. If it is under 20 min, the runner enters the ladder at the highest rung whose longest running bout is at most 1.10× the reference, so a short run in the last 30 days outweighs a longer older one. Grade D.
  4. **No run in the last 90 days:** an experienced runner (trained or recreational experience, 2 or more years of running, no soft flag) takes path 2 at N = 20, because an experienced returner after a long break is not a novice. Everyone else starts at ladder rung 1 (about 60 min a week). Grade D.
  5. **Recent-injury flag** (any injury listed in the profile for the last 12 months, running-related or not, because the profile records no running-related field; A9) with a start level under 60 min, or no runs: the injury-return ladder, which G-3 describes. A recent bone stress injury takes the same ladder today. The walk-jog stages of n1 for bone stress injuries are design intent, not implemented: no code reads them (as for the race simulation in H2). Recent-injury runners never get the 30-min lift.
  - **Start level.** The start path and the opening week of a new plan read the start level: the higher of the typical week (the median of the last 4 weeks with a run) and the recent level (the lesser of the running minutes of the last 2 complete Monday weeks, both holding a run; the current partial week never counts). A step up held for two weeks sets the level and one big week alone does not, so weeks of 30, 30, 90 and 79 min (median about 55) open a plan at 79 min, not at the 3 × 20 floor below what the runner already runs. The feasibility label and the low-frequency test of A5 read the same level. G-1 and G-2 are unchanged: the opening week still obeys the ramp against the completed weeks (the highest of the 2 preceding loading weeks), and after a break (J7) or inside a post-race window (H12) the restart volume caps the recent level. Grade D, a default with no evidence.
  - **Advance.** One rung per plan week, and only after at least 80% of the week's planned minutes with no pain flag (the J5 hold threshold); otherwise the rung repeats, and a runner may hold any rung. With ratings, a mean session RPE of 5/10 or more also holds the rung: the direction is grade C [kluitenberg-2016-nlstart2run-training-factors], the 5/10 value is D. Without ratings the ladder advances on completion and pain reports alone, as other adjustments need ratings. A rung that G-1 held short of its own length repeats, so the ladder ends only after its last rung was run at full length; an activity linked to a planned session counts for at most that session's minutes, so the recorded walks do not stand for running. A ladder, injury-return or floor session is exempt from the 15-min run-day minimum (the floor session from the 20-min session minimum too) and is placed at the G-1 bound down to 5 min, with the week saying what it works toward; the bound of a floor after a post-race window (H12) includes the last step run, at the length it is placed. Under a progression hold (REDs flags, focal bone pain) the path is still chosen, but the ladder keeps its rung and the floor repeats. A tightened profile takes the path like any other runner and is never lifted above 3 × 20. No gentler "rung 0" is offered. Grade D [relph-2023-couch-to-5k-injury, fokkema-2019-discontinuation-start-to-run, nhs-2026-couch-to-5k].
  - **BMI above 30** (height and weight in the profile): hold rung 1 for weeks 1-3 and advance in week 4. This copies the tested arm of one trial (3 km in week 1, then at most +10% a week); its intention-to-treat result was not significant, and heavier novices chose the same first-week dose as lighter ones. Grade C, low confidence [nielsen-2014-first-week-distance-obese, bertelsen-2018-start-to-run-distance-rct, bertelsen-2018-bmi-self-chosen-start].
  - **Easy only.** Ladder, floor and injury-return weeks have no quality sessions or strides, nor for 2 weeks after the ladder or the injury-return ladder ends. Grade C for the direction [kluitenberg-2016-nlstart2run-training-factors].
  - **Limits.** Every ladder and floor session obeys G-1 as a hard 1.10× limit on whole-session distance and duration, walk blocks included; a session is the lesser of the rung and that bound. The ladder and floor weeks are exempt from the G-2 soft 10% warning only (a fixed convention programme), never from the 30% over 2 weeks hard ceiling, which the ladder does not approach. The injury-return weeks (G-3) are exempt from the weekly ramp in full, because the levels set the load, and every run in them is held to the hard 1.10× limit. A tightened profile without a start path (prior bone stress injury, two or more BSI factors) gets no exemption. The first week of a path is the baseline the ramp measures from, so the ceiling holds from the second week on. A re-plan made inside the floor week keeps the floor for that week (G-2). In a generated plan the planned ladder sessions are no reference for the sessions after them: G-1 reads completed runs, so a plan made before the first run shows the programme and each re-plan holds the next session to the runs that were done. Grade D [frandsen-2025-session-spike, buist-2008-graded-training, fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review].
  - **No preconditioning block.** No walking or hopping block is offered before running as injury prevention. Grade B [bredeweg-2012-preconditioning-novice-rct].

**Spacing and placement**

- **F1 Spacing after hard work.** Allow at least 48 h after high-intensity work and 24-48 h after threshold work before the next hard session. No consecutive hard days by default. Grade C [stanley-2013-parasympathetic-reactivation, casado-2022-elite-runners-review].
- **F2 Quality sessions.** 1-2 per week, sized from a minutes budget. Zone 2 and zone 3 together take about 20% of weekly time, never more than 25%. Grade D [sylta-2014-tid-quantification, casado-2022-elite-runners-review].
  - Do not raise performance at a fixed volume by adding extra runs. Grade C [yamaguchi-2023-volume-frequency-run-distance].
- **F3 Long-run placement.** The long run does not sit between two quality days (D).
- **F4 Strength placement.** Strength sessions are at least 3 h away from key runs, preferably on easy days, and never directly before the long run. Grade A, low confidence [schumann-2022-concurrent-compatibility, wilson-2012-concurrent-interference].
  - **How the planner meets it (amended 2026-10-25).** A strength session is never placed on the day of a hard run, a long run or a back-to-back day, and never on the day before the first long-run day of the week. On an easy or strides day it is ordered after the run, so a run and a lift on one day are the normal case, and the workout says that the session is at least `f4.strengthSeparationHours` hours from the run because the plan holds no clock time. A lift never displaces a run: when no day fits, the week holds fewer lifts and never fewer runs. The day after the long run and the day before a quality session are not avoided (owner decision of 2026-10-24: F4 asks for neither and the corpus has no paper that does). The evidence concerns strength outcomes and not running performance: explosive strength fell more when strength and endurance shared a session than when they were 3 h apart, with no age or frequency effect [schumann-2022-concurrent-compatibility], and interference with running grew with endurance frequency and duration [wilson-2012-concurrent-interference]. The room a session needs on a day is its budget, `f7.blockSessionMin` in a block week and `f7.maintenanceSessionMin` otherwise, which replaced the placement default `fplace.strengthSessionMin` of 30 minutes (retired 2026-10-25, task E14-3). The time a day offers a session is the runner's availability for that day; the 30-day reference of the spike guard (G-1) limits a run and does not size the gym time. Every limit still bounds a session on a day that a rest window, a limited window or a post-race return step rules, and in a week a window touched.
- **F5 Frequency.**
  - Novices, and anyone in the E6 start weeks: at most 3 non-consecutive run days per week; 2 is allowed by choice. Grade D: three days a week had far fewer injuries than five in a 1977 trial (12% vs 39%, hard jogging in young men), but the 3-versus-2 contrast in novices was not significant [pollock-1977-frequency-duration-injury, kluitenberg-2016-nlstart2run-training-factors, kluitenberg-2015-nlstart2run].
  - Runners aged 40 and over: no more than 6 run days by default, overridable. Grade D [mckean-2006-masters-injury].
- **F6 One lever at a time.** In any block, progress either intensity or volume, not both. Grade B [ramskov-2018-run-clever].

**Strength prescription (F7 to F9)**

A strength session is a workout of named lifts, not a label. Its prescription is derived on every read from F7 to F9, the runner's equipment and the sets the runner logged (ADR 0014), and none of it is stored on a planned session. E5 says when the block runs and F4 where a session sits in the week. The sessions never change a run, a week's running minutes or a stored session. Every dose below is a default (grade D) unless a grade says otherwise, and the evidence gaps for them are listed at the end of this document. The rules apply to the runner whatever the sex (G-4, A5).

- **F7 Strength session structure and exercise selection.** Grade A for the method, a heavy-plus-plyometric block of `e5.strengthBlockWeeks` weeks; D for every dose below.
  - **Method.** Heavy lower-body lifting, at `flib.heavyMinPct1rm` % of the one-repetition maximum or more, is the primary stimulus after a technique base. Grade A [llanoslagos-2024-economy-speeds, eihara-2022-heavy-vs-plyo, denadai-2017-explosive-heavy-economy, berryman-2018-strength-performance-meta]. Plyometrics join it in the same block: combined methods had the largest pooled effect, at low certainty [llanoslagos-2024-economy-speeds, balsalobre-2016-running-economy-highly-trained, zanini-2025-strength-economy-durability-rct], and jump training alone improved running economy and 2-5 km time trials in a pooled analysis of 21 controlled studies of endurance runners [ramirezcampillo-2021-jump-training-runners]. Submaximal and isometric work did not improve economy [llanoslagos-2024-economy-speeds]. No trial identifies the best number of sets and repetitions. One trial of maximal strength with a running-economy outcome used half-squats of 4 sets of 4RM, three times a week for 8 weeks, in 8 lifting runners (17 were randomised) [storen-2008-maximal-strength-economy]; the other templates in the corpus, below, differ in exercises, sessions and weeks. Grade A for the method, D for the dose.
  - **Templates.** A session's template follows from its week. The plan holds `flib.strengthPerWeek` sessions a week in the block and `e5.strengthMaintenancePerWeek` after it. Grade D for every number in this list; the structure follows the trial templates of 2-4 lower-body exercises plus jumps, 2-3 times a week for 8-12 weeks [balsalobre-2016-running-economy-highly-trained], and of maximal strength plus plyometrics twice a week for 10 weeks [zanini-2025-strength-economy-durability-rct].
    - **Ramp-in**, the first weeks of the block. Its length is `flib.heavyRampInWeeks` weeks by default; the ends of its range are for a runner who has lifted consistently and lifts now (the short end) and for a new lifter or a runner of `f5.mastersMinAge` or older (the long end, a design choice with low-confidence support [piacentini-2013-masters-runners, eihara-2024-middle-aged-pt-rt, schumann-2022-concurrent-compatibility]). Repetitions `f7.rampInReps`, sets `f7.rampInSets` (the first value in the first week, the second after), `f7.easyRir` or more repetitions in reserve, the first rung of the jump ladder, and no barbell lift (G6: the technique base comes first).
    - **Heavy**, the rest of the block. `flib.heavyExercises` lifts of `flib.heavyReps` repetitions, the lead lift at the upper end of `flib.heavySets` sets and the others at the lower end, `f7.heavyRir` repetitions in reserve and the jump ladder.
    - **Lighter week**, a running recovery week (E3) inside the block after its ramp-in: the same lifts at the same loads with `f7.maintenanceSets` sets and `f7.reducedPlyoShare` of the jump dose. A recovery week inside the ramp-in stays a ramp-in (the ramp-in repetitions and `f7.easyRir` or more in reserve, and no barbell lift) with the same cut of sets, the same load and the same share of the jump dose (amended 2026-10-25).
    - **Maintenance**, after the block until the taper: the lifts of the block at the kept load, `f7.maintenanceSets` sets, and `f7.maintenancePlyoContacts` contacts.
    - **Taper**, the taper weeks: `f7.taperLifts` lifts (the lead lift and the calf raise) at `f7.maintenanceSets` sets, the kept load and `f7.easyRir` or more repetitions in reserve; no jumps, and none in the race week or after the race.
    - **Return**, the weeks of an injury return, a ladder or a restart floor (E6, G-3): the ramp-in structure without jumps and without a barbell lift. No evidence either way; G6 decides.
    - The time budget of a session is `f7.blockSessionMin` minutes in a block week and `f7.maintenanceSessionMin` otherwise; a workout that takes less is fine. A new exercise always starts as a ramp-in session, whatever the week, because it is new to the body even for a trained runner (the repeated-bout reasoning of [mchugh-2003-repeated-bout]; a design default). After a regenerate, which builds a plan with no stored weeks and so starts a new block, a runner with logged block sessions in each of the last `f7.blockCreditWeeks` weeks starts the new block at the heavy template and skips its ramp-in, because the loads continue from the logs (G6); a design default.
  - **Exercise selection.** A fixed catalogue with five slots (knee, hip, calf, jumps, trunk), each with an ordered ladder, best supported first. The first exercise of a ladder that the saved equipment allows leads; bodyweight is always possible, so every slot has an answer. The barbell is offered after the ramp-in to any runner whose saved equipment includes it (owner decision of 2026-10-24); no paper in the corpus gives safety data for recreational runners starting barbell lifts, which is why the ramp-in comes first, no maximal attempt is ever asked for and the first session of an exercise leaves `f7.easyRir` or more repetitions in reserve (G6). Grade D for the selection.
    - Squat and hinge lifts carry the heavy method. The hinge and the leg curl load the hamstrings and hip extensors, which the masters topic asks for from `f5.mastersMinAge` (calf, Achilles and hamstring injuries were more common in masters runners, in a retrospective self-report [mckean-2006-masters-injury]; grade C, low confidence).
    - Heavy calf raises are in every block: the change in calf-raise 1RM correlated with the change in oxygen cost (r = -0.477) in one trial of 20 runners, the only calf-specific datum with a running outcome in the corpus [eihara-2024-middle-aged-pt-rt]; the two other calf papers below have a muscle-volume outcome and an acute modelled estimate. Grade C, low confidence. No paper gives sets, repetitions, load or tempo for the calf, so it takes the heavy range and effort of the other lifts (D). Straight-knee and bent-knee raises alternate between the two weekly sessions as a design choice that the one trial comparing the positions does not support: in 14 untrained adults, 12 weeks of standing raises grew the gastrocnemius more and the soleus about as much as seated raises, and the outcome was muscle volume, not running [kinoshita-2023-standing-seated-calf-raise]. An exhausting weighted calf-raise protocol did not change the modelled stress on the tibia in a run that followed in 14 recreational runners, an acute estimate and not an injury outcome [rice-2020-calf-raise-tibial-stress].
    - Knee-extensor and plantar-flexor work is the emphasis for goals of about 100 km and longer (H10), a grade D extrapolation: performance at 101 km was explained by VO2max, maximal isometric strength and body fat in 16 finishers [pastor-2022-trail-determinants-distances], and a 12-week programme lowered the energy cost of running in 25 male ultra-marathoners [giovanelli-2017-ultra-sep]. Ultra goals read the `h10` Params, whose values equal the E5 and F-lib ones. Evidence for 100-mile runners is thin and the doses are labelled extrapolated.
    - No nordic or supramaximal eccentric exercise is in the catalogue: H5 gives no dose, and one bout of unaccustomed eccentric work causes the damage that the repeated-bout effect protects against [mchugh-2003-repeated-bout].
  - **Slow lowering (H5).** Each lift is lowered over `f7.lowerSeconds` seconds. It is the eccentric emphasis that H5 asks for inside the block; the rationale is the fall of knee-extensor force after a long race [temesi-2015-female-neuromuscular-fatigue, millet-2011-ultramarathon-neuromuscular], and there is no dose evidence for it. No loaded eccentric above the lifted load is prescribed. Grade D.
  - **Jump ladder.** Four low-amplitude movements on the spot, one rung per `f7.plyoRungWeeks` weeks, starting at `f7.plyoStartContacts` contacts and adding `f7.plyoStepContacts` contacts every `f7.plyoStepEveryWeeks` weeks up to `flib.plyoContactsMax` contacts (A: the highest dose observed in trials and not a tested limit [llanoslagos-2024-economy-speeds, balsalobre-2016-running-economy-highly-trained]). A step of contacts or a move to the next rung is taken only after a completed running week, a finished week in which the runner ran at least `j5.completionHoldShare` of the run minutes planned for it (the test that J5 applies to the weekly volume); otherwise the dose holds. The dose, taken to the cap first, is multiplied by `f7.reducedPlyoShare` when the goal pace is faster than `flib.plyoEmphasisPaceKmh` km/h, because the plyometric effect was found at that pace or slower [llanoslagos-2024-economy-speeds], and for a bone stress history or a recent injury (G6), which also stop the ladder at its second rung; a lighter week halves it too. Depth jumps, drops and bounds are not prescribed: the corpus has no dose or bone-stress data for them, and depth drops from 60 and 90 cm and depth jumps from 90 cm peaked at 4.30 to 5.39 times body weight in landing force, against 3.34 for a vertical jump, in 14 collegiate and national-level track and field men [wallace-2010-plyometric-grf]. Short sprints stay with the strides sessions of F-lib. No trial gives a starting dose, a progression, a surface or bone-stress data for ground contacts: the start, the step, the cadence, the rung length and the halving are grade D.
  - **Trunk and hip finisher.** Two movements of `f7.trunkSets` sets, each of `f7.trunkReps` repetitions or a hold of `f7.trunkHoldSec` seconds; one movement in a taper session. A hip and core programme before runs lowered lower-extremity injury in novices (HR 0.66) in a supervised trial [leppanen-2024-hip-core-novice]: grade B for that programme in novices, D for this dose, and the finisher is not that programme, which stays out of scope (F-lib).
  - **Bodyweight path.** Without equipment, or with equipment that cannot make a heavy load, movements use `f7.bodyweightReps` repetitions, taken to `f7.heavyRir` repetitions in reserve, and progress by repetitions and then by the next harder movement (F8). The heavy range cannot apply without added load, and the plan does not call this path heavy. Plyometrics alone improved economy at slow paces [llanoslagos-2024-economy-speeds] and matched resistance training in a trial without a control group [eihara-2024-middle-aged-pt-rt]. Grade D for the range.
  - **Expected benefit.** Strength work may lower the oxygen cost of running by about `f7.expectedBenefitPct` % after a full block, which is small, takes the whole block and says nothing about race time. Grade A, moderate confidence: -3.9% in a pooled analysis of concurrent strength training [denadai-2017-explosive-heavy-economy], 2-8% in the 20 of 24 controlled studies that measured economy [blagrove-2018-strength-determinants], and small to moderate effects for work at 80% 1RM or more [llanoslagos-2024-economy-speeds]. Single studies report up to 8%, and the pooled upper-bound figure (SMD -1.42, five trials) is not a planning figure [balsalobre-2016-running-economy-highly-trained]. No method moved VO2max, velocity at VO2max or the threshold [llanoslagos-2024-performance-methods, blagrove-2018-strength-determinants], so no VO2max, threshold or pace change is ever projected from strength work. Evidence in women is small and mixed: in 19 well-trained women 11 weeks of heavy lower-body training raised 1RM by 40% and changed neither running economy nor a 40-minute run [vikmoen-2016-female-heavy-strength-running], while in 19 female duathletes a 5-minute run after 1.5 hours of running improved by 4.7% against no change [vikmoen-2017-female-duathletes-strength]; a trial of 17 runners included 8 women [storen-2008-maximal-strength-economy]. The strongest support is for heavy loads; jumps help at slower paces; and the evidence for 100-mile runners is thin.
  - **Injury.** Strength training is a supportive measure, and the app states no running-specific reduction in injuries and no figure (grade A, low confidence). The sports-general meta-analyses found RR 0.315 and 0.338 [lauersen-2014-exercise-injury-prevention, lauersen-2018-strength-dose-dependent], and the one large running RCT, in which 720 first-time marathoners were randomised to a 10-minute self-directed programme three times a week for 12 weeks or to observation, found no difference in overuse injuries that stopped the marathon (RR 0.97) [toresdahl-2020-nyc-marathon-rct]. For novices a graded mileage progression is the injury control and strength is an addition (B, moderate) [toresdahl-2020-nyc-marathon-rct]. Static stretching is not prescribed as injury prevention (A, moderate) [lauersen-2014-exercise-injury-prevention]. Injury rates in competitive weightlifting and powerlifting, 2.4-3.3 and 1.0-4.4 per 1000 hours of training, are in the range of other non-contact sports and low next to contact sports [aasa-2016-weightlifter-powerlifter-injuries, keogh-2016-weight-training-injuries]; those reviews are of competitive lifters, mostly of low quality, so they are not safety data for a recreational runner (D).
  - **Durability.** Maximal strength plus plyometrics twice a week for 10 weeks improved running economy at 90 minutes of a run (-2.1% against +0.6%) and time to exhaustion after it (+35% against -8%) in 28 well-trained men (B, moderate) [zanini-2025-strength-economy-durability-rct]; untested in novices and older runners, and in women only by the small trials above. Heavy strength and plyometric training also appear to help resilience, in a review that calls its data scant (D) [jones-2025-resilience-training].
  - **Masters.** The same prescription for masters runners (about 40-50 years), with the long ramp-in; age did not moderate the strength outcomes of concurrent training in a meta-analysis. Grade C, low confidence [piacentini-2013-masters-runners, eihara-2024-middle-aged-pt-rt, schumann-2022-concurrent-compatibility]. The evidence is two small trials, one of master marathon runners aged about 43-45 [piacentini-2013-masters-runners] and one of middle-aged recreational runners [eihara-2024-middle-aged-pt-rt], and 9 master endurance athletes with a mean age of 51.5 years, whose knee-extensor torque rose 17.8% in 3 weeks, with the efficiency outcome measured on a bicycle and not in running [louis-2011-master-athletes-strength-cycling]; no runner over about 50 and no woman is in the running trials. Lean mass can be partly kept with age, and strength training is recommended (D) [hawkins-2003-vo2max-decline]. No age-based cut to the dose.
  - **Gaps.** S1 to S12 of the evidence-gap list below. A paper that verifies for a gap changes only the sentence that cites it.
- **F8 Strength load progression.** Grade D throughout. No paper compares ways of progressing a load in runners or novices (one synthesis in resistance-trained lifters found effort-based and percentage-based prescription similar, below), none validates an equation from repetitions to a maximum in runners, and the trial templates are fixed ones. Every rule reads the runner's own logged sets, so a reason can be stated for every figure (ADR 0005, ADR 0010), and a language model never chooses a load. The direction of the evidence: a consensus position stand for healthy adults recommends raising the load by 2-10% when the lifter can perform the current workload for one to two repetitions over the desired number [acsm-2009-progression-models-resistance]; an effort-based prescription and a percentage-based one produced similar strength gains in resistance-trained lifters (SMD 0.21, 95% CI for the mean difference -0.32 to 4.46 kg) [hickmott-2022-autoregulation-meta]; and strength gains did not depend on the repetitions left over a wide range [robinson-2024-proximity-failure-meta]. Toward less load by default (G6): an increase needs every set at the top of the range with repetitions in reserve and a recorded count, any set short of the range lowers the next load, and a missing count holds it.
  - **Effort target.** Repetitions in reserve: `f7.heavyRir` in the block and `f7.easyRir` or more in a ramp-in, a taper, a return and the first session of any exercise. The scale links effort to bar speed in squatters [zourdos-2016-rir-rpe-scale], but the count is imprecise: lifters underpredicted the repetitions left by about one repetition on average (0.95, 95% CI 0.17-1.73), with a spread of 1.45 repetitions between people, and less so closer to the limit of a set and with fewer repetitions [halperin-2021-rir-prediction-accuracy]. A sketch, not a measure.
  - **Rules.** The first that applies wins. `last` is the latest `normal` session of the exercise (a lighter day, a lighter week, a taper session or a return session is another mode, F9), `base` its working load (the load used in most sets, the lower on a tie), `lo` and `hi` the ends of the repetition range of the template, and a step the smallest change the equipment can make. The load judged is the load lifted, not the load suggested.
    1. F8.1: no logged session of the exercise. The first load (below), in the ramp-in structure.
    2. F8.2: the latest session of any mode was `f8.layoffDays` days ago or more. The last base load times `f8.layoffLoadFraction`, rounded down to a step, with `lo` repetitions and `f7.easyRir` or more in reserve.
    3. F8.3: any logged set below `lo`. The base less `f8.reducePct` %, rounded down to a step, and at least one step. The reduction is the whole steps of the equipment that fit in it, and every rounding down to a step in F8 and F9 is counted in exact grams and not on the display grid (amended 2026-10-25).
    4. F8.4: every set at `hi` or more, every count of repetitions in reserve recorded, and at least `f8.bigStepRir` on every set. Two steps, if each step is usable: within `f8.maxStepPct` % of the base, or the smallest step the equipment makes (amended 2026-10-25, below).
    5. F8.5: as F8.4 with at least `f8.progressMinRir` on every set. One step, if the step is usable (as in F8.4).
    6. F8.6: otherwise (repetitions in the range but not all at `hi`, a count not recorded, or no usable step). The base; the target of each set is one repetition above the last count, up to `hi`.
    7. F8.7: no usable step in `f8.ceilingSessions` sessions in a row at the top of the range. The first load of the exercise's harder movement, if the equipment allows it.
  - **Step cap and ceiling (amended 2026-10-25).** The cap on a step, `f8.maxStepPct`, is above the 10% end of the position stand's 2-10% because loads move in the steps the equipment can make, and the step from one dumbbell weight to the next can be a large share of the load; it limits a step and is never a target. The smallest step the equipment makes is always usable, so the cap is the larger of `f8.maxStepPct` % of the base and that step: a ladder or a stack has one step, and a list of weights has its smallest gap between neighbours. Without it a light load on equipment whose step is more than `f8.maxStepPct` % of that load (60 lb on a leg press stack in 10 lb steps) could never move, and a lift with no harder movement (F8.7) has no other way on. A larger step than the smallest (50 lb to 60 lb in a set that also has 5 lb steps) is still not offered. No suggestion passes the upper end of the range of `flib.heavyMinPct1rm` % of the estimated maximum, and when the next step would, the load holds. Grade D.
  - **The first load of an exercise.** In order: an anchor the runner entered, `f8.startPctOf1rm` % of its estimated maximum, rounded down to a step; or a fraction of the body weight on the Profile from the table `f8.startFractionBodyweight`, applied to the load basis of the implement and rounded down to a step, with the lightest load the equipment makes where the product is lighter; a bodyweight movement starts with no added load, and with no body weight on the Profile the lightest load is used. The history of a related exercise is not converted. No paper gives a starting load. The fractions are light on purpose, for a lifter who is new to the exercise: a start that is too light is corrected in a few sessions by F8.4 and F8.5, and one that is too heavy shows as a set below `lo` and is lowered by F8.3. Tuning them is a change through the research update process, after a replay on logged histories. Grade D.
  - **The estimated maximum.** The estimate is `load x (1 + (repetitions + repetitions in reserve) / divisor)` with the divisor `f8.e1rmDivisor`, taking 0 in reserve when no count was recorded (the lowest estimate, G6). It is an estimate, never a test: the app does not ask for a maximal attempt, and the estimate is used only for a first load, for the ceiling above and for a labelled figure beside a load; it is never on Today. No paper validates the equation in runners. The repetitions possible at a percentage of the maximum fall along a curve and vary between people and exercises, from 269 studies [nuzzo-2023-reps-at-percent-1rm], and a linear equation from a set was advised only up to 10 repetitions, for the leg press and chest press [reynolds-2006-1rm-prediction]. A set therefore gives no estimate when its repetitions to the limit, the repetitions done plus the repetitions left (0 left when no count was recorded), are more than `f8.e1rmMaxReps` (amended 2026-10-25; a default taken from that advice). Block sets stay inside it. A ramp-in set of 8 repetitions with 3 in reserve reaches 11 and gives none: an anchor that is such a set gives none, so the first load comes from the body-weight table. The ceiling reads the latest estimate available, from the most recent normal session that gave one (any earlier session, not only the latest) and before any did from an anchor, so a session whose sets all give none does not lift it; only a lift with no estimate at all, such as a body-weight start whose every set so far is over the limit, has no ceiling (the step cap does apply). Grade D.
  - **After a gap.** The return load, `f8.layoffLoadFraction` of the last base, is a caution about technique and tissue after a break and not an estimate of lost strength: strength gained in 16 weeks was largely retained through 32 weeks of detraining in a trial of 70 adults with a detraining arm [bickel-2011-maintenance-dosing-retention], and force production declines slowly [mujika-2000-detraining-part2]. G6 decides the direction. Grade D.
- **F9 Lighter days, recovery weeks, the taper and the return after a gap.** Grade D throughout. No trial tests resistance training in a taper, and the evidence on maintenance comes from non-athletes and trained men, not from runners.
  - **A low-readiness day.** When Today's readiness read is not normal (J3), or a pain report at or above `pain.flagScore` is recent, or an illness is in its easy-only phase (n-3), each lift has `f9.lowReadinessSetsCut` set fewer (at least 2 remain) and a load `f9.lowReadinessLoadPct` % lower, rounded down to a step; the usual load is shown beside it and the runner can use it. These are the tests that J3 already applies to runs: no second test and no new threshold. Readiness never adds load (J3, G6). A day of the illness rest phase withdraws the session, as it does today. No paper in the corpus tests a lighter strength session on such a day.
  - **A lighter week and maintenance.** Volume is cut and the load kept, the pattern that kept strength in general populations for up to 32 weeks with 1 session a week and 1 set per exercise [spiering-2021-minimal-dose]. In a trial of 70 adults, a third or a ninth of the training dose kept the muscle gained in the young, and strength was largely kept through 32 weeks of detraining in both ages [bickel-2011-maintenance-dosing-retention]; in resistance-trained men one set, one to three times a week, still raised 1RM strength [androulakis-2019-minimum-effective-dose]. None of these studies runners in a racing phase, and older adults needed a higher dose than the young to keep muscle size, so the session count and the `f7.maintenanceSets` sets are defaults. Using the pattern for a recovery week is a design choice.
  - **The taper.** One light session a week until the race week, none in the race week or after the race (the plan places none there today). Rule K3 keeps intensity and frequency in a taper and cuts volume for endurance training [bosquet-2007-taper-meta-analysis]: an analogy, because no paper tests resistance training in a taper. Stopping evidence is small and mixed: in 8 middle-distance runners the 5.75% fall in the energy cost of running over an 8-week programme was still a 6.31% fall after 4 weeks without explosive strength training [berryman-2020-strength-cessation-runners], while 14 elite cyclists lost squat jump height, maximal aerobic power and Wingate power relative to a control group after 8 weeks without strength work [ronnestad-2016-strength-cessation-cyclists]. A taper session is a default, not a requirement.
  - **Other modes.** A session in a mode other than `normal` neither raises nor lowers the base load: the next suggestion is made from the latest `normal` session, so a run of lighter sessions cannot drift a load down by rounding. A gap is counted from the latest session of any mode (F8.2). A session the runner moves, skips or does not finish changes no load. The one prompt is a question about a lower weekly count after `f9.countPromptWeeks` finished weeks with fewer strength sessions than planned, and the answer is the runner's (ADR 0007). Lifting data never changes a run.

## (g) Progression and safety limits

- **G-1 Session spike guard** (the primary guard). Compare every planned run with the 30-day longest completed run, separately for distance, duration, gain and descent:

  | Ratio to 30-day longest run | Observed hazard   | Engine behaviour                                              |
  | --------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
  | 1.10× or less               | reference         | normal                                                        |
  | Above 1.10× to 2.00×        | HRR about 1.5-1.6 | warn                                                          |
  | Above 2.00×                 | HRR about 2.3     | the generator never schedules it without an explicit override |

  Do not describe the 30-100% band as safer than the 10-30% band. The bands are observed associations, not causal limits [wang-2024-target-trial-load].
  - Check against completed runs, not planned runs.
  - **Races in the reference.** A completed race of marathon distance or longer (42.195 km or more) does not count toward the 30-day reference, in the check and in the generator alike; shorter races count as runs. A race without a distance counts as a run when it lasted 4 h or less (longer than any marathon the engine expects), else it does not. One predicate decides what counts, for the reference and the gain ramp, as for the athlete state and the weekly totals: the duplicate status alone (`possible` and `confirmed` are out; a duplicate the owner dismissed is a real run). Otherwise a 100-mile finish would allow training runs up to 1.10 times the race, and after a 24 h race the guard would permit a 26 h run for 30 days. Grade D [hoffman-2017-161km-recovery-determinants, millet-2011-ultramarathon-neuromuscular].
  - No study supports another threshold or another window; the calculation method changes which sessions are flagged (16-33% of sessions at the same cutoffs) [cloosterman-2024-acwr-methods, fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review].
  - The same limits apply to every experience class; nothing supports loosening them for experienced runners. Grade C [abrahamson-2025-self-reported-characteristics, viljoen-2022-trail-risk-factors].
  - During the E6 start paths and under G-3 the 1.10× limit is hard, and the generator plans each session at or below it.
  - When the longest run is short, the minimum step is 0.5 km on distance and 3 min on duration (D), so a return built on 5-minute runs can rise.
  - G-1, not a fixed long-run cap, is the single-session guard. Reviews of prospective studies find the evidence on session and weekly distance, duration and their changes conflicting or low quality, and no safe or unsafe session length has been identified. Fixed caps (D7, H2) are defaults, never injury limits. Grade B [fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review, vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review].
  - Grade B on distance [frandsen-2025-session-spike]; D on duration, because the only duration cohort measured an acute:chronic ratio and its small association (HR 1.33) disappeared once runners injured at baseline were excluded, which is indirect support only [cloosterman-2026-gps-load-injury]; D on gain and descent (H4).

- **G-2 Weekly ramp.**
  - Default is a soft 10% per week; exceeding it gives a warning. Grade D: a convention that matches published plans (5-9% average build) [knopp-2024-marathon-plans]. The only RCT of a 10%-rule programme found no effect, and it did not isolate a 10% threshold [buist-2008-graded-training]. The evidence on progression is conflicting [fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review, nielsen-2012-training-errors]. The value is kept because it matches practice and errs conservative. At the standard and upper load positions the soft step of a week whose baseline is under the rebuild ceiling is the position's step, and 10% again from the ceiling up (G-8); the hard ceiling below is never raised.
  - Hard ceiling: 30% growth over any 2-week window. Grade C [buist-2008-graded-training, nielsen-2014-weekly-progression, nielsen-2013-gps-progression, damsted-2019-weekly-distance-change, damsted-2018-training-load-change, fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review]. Weak signals sit at 20-30%, from one time point of one 261-runner cohort and from 60 novices; neither a tighter nor a looser ceiling is supported.
  - A week is compared with the highest of the 2 preceding loading weeks; recovery, taper and race weeks are skipped (D) [cloosterman-2024-acwr-methods].
  - No combined pace-and-volume threshold; F6 governs intensity [ramskov-2022-volume-pace-interaction].
  - Novices progress by time, with a minimum step of +1 min of running per week (D) [buist-2008-graded-training].
  - The E6 ladder and floor weeks are exempt from the soft 10% warning, not from the hard ceiling. The post-race return steps (H12) are exempt from G-2 altogether, as a return to an established base (their fractions of the pre-taper week rise faster than 30% in 2 weeks), but not from G-1; so is the first loading week after a window, up to its J7 restart volume. Grade D.
  - **Load-change framework (2026-10-25; evidence only, no value changes).** The evidence frames injury as the load of a session set against the capacity of one structure, with capacity at the start of the session, load, loss of capacity during it and exceeding it as its parts [bertelsen-2017-rri-etiology-framework]. Weekly minutes measure load only; no paper in the corpus measures a runner's tissue capacity or dates it after a partial reduction in running. A review of bone stress injury says a workload error can show 3-4 weeks later, that tolerance of load changes varies between individuals and between skeletal sites, and that no single progression rule is likely to suit every runner [warden-2021-bsi-optimal-workload]; those statements are the review's summaries of other sources, level D. None of this moves the soft 10% (grade D) or the 30% two-week ceiling (grade C). Grade D.
  - **Conflict:** the periodization topic sets 30% as a per-week ceiling; the injury and beginner topics set 30% over 2 weeks. Resolution: the stricter 2-week window wins.
  - **A week already run above the ramp is taken as it is, and the next week holds.** The ramp limits planned increases; minutes already run are a fact that no plan can change. A loading week of the current plan whose logged minutes pass its target takes them as its target (flag `overshoot`, with `planned-<n>` for the minutes it was planned at), adds no more running that week, and does not fail the checklist's progression item for the minutes already run; the item still fails a week whose planned sessions take it above the limit. The weeks after it are drawn from the planned minutes, not from the minutes run: the lesser of the ramp from the planned week and the ramp from the week as run (G6, G-6), so at steady one week above the ramp lifts nothing after it and nothing is made up. At standard and upper the trailing mean of the minutes run over the last four eligible weeks lifts the baseline of the next loading week when it is higher than the planned one, within the two bounds of G-8 (the newest of those weeks run at its target, the lift no higher than the two-week ceiling over the planned baseline), and the position's step then applies to it; the minutes run above the ramp are still never made up. A recovery, taper, post-race, run-walk ladder or injury-return week keeps its target, because the recovery cadence, the taper and the steps are read from it; the checklist does not fail it for logged minutes either. Grade D, a design default: no study tests what to plan after a week above the ramp, so the rule only applies the G-2 limits and G-6 to the week as run [buist-2008-graded-training, fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review, nielsen-2014-weekly-progression].
  - **The floor week stays the floor for its own week.** The 3 × N restart floor week is exempt from the 2-week ceiling up to its minutes (E6). A re-plan made inside that week keeps it on the floor even when history logged since the plan was made (a week of runs imported late) has lifted the start level to 3 × 20 or more and ended the start path. Otherwise the week would be drawn again from the ceiling of the weeks before the layoff (for example 28 min × 1.3 = 36 min), below the floor it opened on, and the weeks after it would continue from that lower week, though the runner has since run more than the plan expected. The weeks after the floor week follow the plan they continue (the planned minutes of the floor week, above) and stay inside the G-2 and G-3 limits measured from the weeks actually run: the ceiling for a week is taken from those weeks, not from the reference of the layoff. A progression hold (J5) does not repeat the floor of a runner whose start level is past it. Grade D, a design default: no study tests how long a start week lasts, so the rule only applies the E6 exemption to the week it was granted for [buist-2008-graded-training, fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review, nielsen-2014-weekly-progression].
  - **The ceiling never plans the week in progress below the minutes run in it.** The 2-week ceiling measures a week against the weeks two and three before it. A plan made in the middle of the week after a week above that ceiling (runs imported late, a layoff just ended) found its first week capped at, for example, 30 min × 1.3 = 39 min with 79 min already logged in it and 94 the week before; the week was taken as run, but it was planned at 39, so the weeks after it continued from 39 (42, 31, 46, 50) although the weeks actually run describe a runner at 80 to 90 min. The ceiling limits what a plan adds to a week, not minutes already run, so in the week a plan opens in the cap on the planned minutes is the ceiling or the minutes logged so far in that week, whichever is higher; the start level (E6) or the planned minutes of the plan being replaced still set the week, and the 10% soft step of the weeks after it still holds against the weeks actually run. A week that holds more than it was planned at is taken as run (above), so the weeks after it continue from the planned week (for example 62 min, then 68, 74) inside the G-2 and G-3 limits, not from the ceiling of the weeks before the layoff. A runner who has not outrun the restart floor is unchanged: with fewer minutes logged than the ceiling, the cap is the ceiling, and the floor week still opens on its floor minutes (E6). A re-plan made inside a floor week keeps the floor week when the minutes run have lifted this cap to the floor itself. Grade D, a design default: no study tests how to plan a week that is partly run, so the rule only keeps the ceiling from planning below minutes that are a fact [buist-2008-graded-training, fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review, nielsen-2014-weekly-progression].
- **G-3 Tightened profiles.**
  - Recent-injury flag (any injury listed in the profile for the last 12 months, running-related or not, because the profile records no running-related field; A9): the 1.10× session limit becomes a hard limit, the ramp is 5-7%, and a recovery week falls every 3rd week. Grade A for the factor, D for the values [hulme-2017-risk-protective, vanderworp-2015-risk-factors, vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review, relph-2023-couch-to-5k-injury].
  - Recent-injury flag with a start level under 60 min (E6), or no runs: the injury-return ladder.
    - Gate (design intent, not implemented; no code asks for or checks it): pain-free 30-min brisk walk and pain-free single-leg hops, declared by the runner.
    - Levels: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 min of continuous easy running, each done twice, with at least 1 rest day between runs and 2-3 runs a week.
    - Entry: the highest level not above the longest pain-free run of the last 30 days.
    - Advance only when both runs of the level were run at its own length (within 1 min) and symptom-free during the run, after it and the next day (check-in pain 0 and activity pain 0); otherwise rest a day and drop one level. A level the bound holds short is planned at the bound and repeats until it is run at its length, so the programme ends only after level 6 was run at 30 min.
    - The 1.10× limit is hard, so each run is the lesser of the level and that bound (+0.5 km or 1.10×); the levels are targets, and progress is slower than the source protocol.
    - The injury-return weeks are exempt from the weekly ramp (G-2) in full, because the levels set the load; the hard 1.10× limit on each run holds a week to about 10% above the one before. Grade D.
    - Bone stress injuries: a recent bone stress injury takes this ladder today. The walk-jog stages of n1 are design intent, not implemented; no code reads them (as for the race simulation in H2).
    - Grade D: the levels are clinical conventions and applying soft-tissue protocols to a generic injury flag is an extrapolation [sanford-2024-return-to-running, bjsm-2019-bone-stress-return-to-run, relph-2023-couch-to-5k-injury, vanpoppel-2021-overuse-risk-factors-review].
  - Low frequency: completed runs averaging under 2 per week over the last 4 weeks, for a runner above the E6 floor, give the novice ramp handling (time-based steps) and a 2:1 recovery cadence (A5). Grade D, a design default [abrahamson-2025-self-reported-characteristics].
  - Prior BSI: ramp at or below 5% plus a clinician prompt. Grade A for the factor, D for the values [wright-2015-stress-fracture-meta].
  - Two or more BSI factors: the ramp drops to 50% of the default. The factors are 12 h/week or more of training, BMI below 21 (women only), menstrual disturbance, dietary restraint, prior BSI, and low BMD. Grade B for the factors, D for the cap [barrack-2014-bone-stress-triad, stennett-2025-bsi-female-offroad-runners].
- **G-4 Sex-specific limits.** No sex-specific volume or ramp limits. Sex changes only the injury-specific caution copy: bone stress and energy availability for women, Achilles for men. Grade A [hollander-2021-sex-differences].
- **G-5 ACWR is never a gate.** No acute-to-chronic ratio band (any window, rolling or EWMA) blocks, caps or justifies progression.
  - **A personal ceiling in minutes is not a ratio** (ADR 0013). A number of minutes of weekly running taken from the runner's own steady weeks divides no recent load by an earlier one, defines no band of values and flags no week, so G-5 does not forbid it. It may set how far a faster weekly step runs while a plan rebuilds toward a level the runner held recently, and nothing else: it blocks no week, is never called a safe or an unsafe level, and G-1, the 30% two-week ceiling of G-2 and the tightened profiles of G-3 still bound every week. Any figure that sets recent load against earlier load to decide, cap or justify a week is a ratio and stays forbidden, whatever it is called. Grade D: no study tests such a ceiling; rule G-8 sets it, with its Params.
  - **A descriptive figure in a report is not a band** (ADR 0016). A figure printed to describe a plan gates, caps and justifies nothing, so it is not the ratio band this rule forbids, as ADR 0013 found for a personal ceiling in minutes; ADR 0016 applies this to the plan report, which prints no acute-to-chronic ratio and sets no target.
  - If shown, it uses EWMA, is labelled descriptive, needs 4 weeks of history first (D), and is hidden in taper and recovery weeks.
  - A ratio below 0.70 may be shown as information about a return from a break.
  - Grade B/C [nakaoka-2021-acwr-runners, impellizzeri-2020-acwr-pitfalls, wang-2020-acwr-lessons, griffin-2020-acwr-systematic-review, cloosterman-2026-gps-load-injury]. In a second GPS cohort the small duration-ratio association (HR 1.33) disappeared when runners injured at baseline were excluded.
  - **Conflict:** the volume and ultra topics propose a soft cap at 7:28 ≥ 1.5 [toresdahl-2023-nyc-marathon-training-injury, craddock-2020-acwr-two-oceans]. Resolution: display only. The per-day OR was 1.06, a large runner cohort showed an inverse association [frandsen-2025-session-spike], the duration-based ratio lost its association once runners injured at baseline were excluded [cloosterman-2026-gps-load-injury], and G-1 already bounds the spikes.
- **G-6 Never make up lost volume.**
  - Next week's target = min(planned target, actual last week × (1 + ramp)). At steady a week run above its plan lifts nothing: the planned target is the lesser number (G-2). At standard and upper the mean of the minutes run over the last four eligible weeks can lift the baseline of a later loading week, within the two bounds of G-8, and a hold or reduce decision still caps the week it covers by this formula. Below the rebuild ceiling of a plan at the standard or upper position, the ramp of that formula is the zone's step, as in the plan itself (G-8).
  - Skipped or downgraded sessions are not rescheduled. Grade B [vesterinen-2016-hrv-runners-rct, duking-2021-hrv-guided-meta]; the formula itself is D.
- **G-7 Symptom check-ins are secondary.** Only 6.9-11.1% of overuse injuries had a reported problem beforehand, so check-ins never replace load guards. Grade C [frandsen-2025-sudden-onset].
- **G-8 History-informed rebuild** (ADR 0013). When the runner asks for it, the plan can rebuild toward a weekly level the runner held in the last 12 weeks faster than the soft step of G-2, and can start on 3 sessions of 30 min from the same evidence. Status: the history block, the load setting as a plan option, the second route to the 3 × 30 start, the rebuild zone and the response to weeks run above plan are built. Grade D as a whole: no study tests any part of it, and every Param of the rule (group `g8`) is a labelled default. The values marked provisional wait for the replay on real history (proposal section 7, question 3).
  - **Load setting.** The plan option `loadAppetite` has three positions: steady, standard and upper band. Steady is the default, and an absent option reads as steady: the engine without this rule, byte for byte. Standard and upper are held at steady, with the reason stated, while any of these holds: a recent injury or the injury report of a window (G-3, J9), an earlier bone stress injury or two BSI factors (G-3), two or more REDs flags or focal bone pain (J5, n-4, n-1), a rest or limited-running window that has begun and not yet ended (J9), and a plan built inside the window after a past race (H12). The setting resumes when the reason ends. A low-frequency runner (A5) is not held (amended 2026-10-25: the list also ended with "and a low-frequency runner (A5)". The flag keeps the 2:1 recovery cadence and the time-based step. The zone, the lift and the response each ask for a block or eligible weeks of their own, so the hold added little). At age 45 or over, upper is read as standard and the soft flag warns; the age flag does not close the lift below. The setting changes the weekly step below the rebuild ceiling, the lift to 3 × 30 and, through the response to weeks run above plan, the baseline a loading week is stepped from (amended 2026-10-25: this sentence said "only the weekly step"). It never touches G-1, the 30% hard ceiling over 2 weeks, the tightened profiles, the progression hold, the post-race and disruption windows or the recovery cadence. Grade D.
  - **History block.** A block is `g8.blockWeeks` (4) consecutive complete weeks that each hold at least `a5.lowFrequencyRunsPerWeek` (2) runs; a race is not a run for this count, and race minutes are not in a week's running minutes. It is scored by the mean of its weeks without the `g8.blockWeeksDropped` (1) highest, so one big week cannot set it (the idea of the E6 start level, where one big week alone does not). Only a block whose last week began no more than `g8.creditTaperEndWeeks` weeks before the current Monday counts. A block within `g8.creditWindowWeeks` (12) weeks counts in full. With `g8.creditTaperEndWeeks` at 16, an older block counts by a factor that falls in a straight line from 1 at 12 weeks to `g8.creditTaperFloor` (0.5) at 16 weeks: 0.875, 0.75 and 0.625 at 13, 14 and 15 weeks; a block older than that counts for nothing. The block with the highest score after the factor wins, the newer one on a tie; with none there is no history, and the plan is the plan without this rule. The athlete state keeps the winner (`blockMin`, the Monday of its last week, `weeksAgo`, `taper`) and the best score among the blocks inside the 12 weeks (`fullCreditMin`), which the start reads. It is computed from the weeks of the state and the activities of the snapshot, with no query of its own (ADR 0009); the 16-week option aggregates 20 weeks of the same activities. The two windows are built behind `g8.creditTaperEndWeeks` (12 or 16) and the replay on real history chooses between them (proposal section 7, question 7); 12 stands until it does. The block is a calendar window of fixed weeks that divides no recent load by an earlier one (G-5). Grade D.
  - **Start of 3 × 30 from the block (E6 path 2, second route).** Standard and upper only: `fullCreditMin` of at least 3 × 30 = 90 min, a year of running, no soft flag other than age and none of the profiles above. N stays the longest run of the last 30 days and G-1 still bounds every session. Grade D.
  - **Rebuild ceiling and zone.** `R` = credit × taper × H, with H the score of the winning block (for a plan that is being continued, the block of its first Monday until a week has reached R, as the next paragraph says), `taper` its factor, and credit 0.70 at standard (`g8.creditStandard`) and 0.85 at upper (`g8.creditUpper`), both provisional. A loading week whose baseline (the highest of the 2 preceding loading weeks) is below `R` is in the zone and takes the position's step in place of the soft 10%: 12% at standard (`g8.stepStandard`, provisional) and at upper the weekly rate at which 2 weeks meet the hard ceiling, sqrt(1 + `g2.rampCeilingTwoWeeks`) − 1 = 14.0%, derived so that it cannot pass the ceiling. The week is planned at max(baseline × 1.10, min(baseline × (1 + step), `R`)), so the zone never plans a week above `R` except by the usual 10%. Above `R` the step is 10% again: the weekly addition is largest far below the earlier level and falls to the usual one near it. The zone leaves out start-path weeks (floor, ladder, injury return), recovery and taper weeks, the weeks of a post-race or disruption window and its return, the tightened profiles and a progression hold (amended 2026-10-25: the list also ended with "and a low-frequency runner"). The generator, the week placement, the guards, the checklist and the weekly decision read one zone: a week under `R` is checked against the zone's limit before the soft warning, the checklist's progression item says how many weeks used the zone (and adds no item), and the cap on the week after a hold or reduce grows by the zone's step below `R`. A hold or reduce week plans no growth of its own, so the zone resumes from the week as placed. A plan whose race is near ends the zone at the plan's peak week, and the taper is unchanged. 0.85 is the share of VO2max kept at day 56 of complete cessation (1 − 0.16) [coyle-1984-detraining-time-course]; 0.70 is close to the share of patellar tendon stiffness left at day 23 of complete unloading (about 71%: a fall of 29.3% in 9 young non-runners, one leg suspended) [deboer-2007-ulls-tendon-time-course] and near the 75% tier of the J7 table (a layoff of 15 to 28 days), which is a different quantity (amended 2026-10-25: the text read "about the fall" and "the cautious end of the J7 table", but the fall is 29.3% and the table's lowest tier is 50-60%). Both are cessation values, so both are conservative for a runner who kept running a little, and neither is a measurement in runners. The 12% is a preference (taste, no claim that it is better). Grade D.
  - **The plan's own block (amended 2026-10-25).** For a plan that is being continued, the credit window is measured at the plan's first Monday, the earliest week of the stored plan, and not at today, until a complete week since that Monday has run at or above R of that block. A continued plan is the one the response below reads: the stored plan holds the current week and the break is shorter than `j7.tier1Days`. The block and its taper factor are those found at the first Monday. Once a complete week since the first Monday has run at or above R of the plan's own block, the rebuild has reached its ceiling and the block of the athlete state at today applies as before: the weeks run since are the runner's new level. A new plan, a plan that restarts after a break, a plan that opens in the current week and a plan that found no block on its first Monday read the state at today, as before. The 365 days of activities (`a5.historyWindowDays`) hold the weeks the block finder reads before the first Monday only for a plan younger than about 33 weeks (37 at the 12-week window); an older continued plan has no block in reach and falls back to the block of the state. The block is found by the one block finder, given the first Monday as its reference, from the weeks the snapshot already holds (ADR 0009). Reason: without it the block leaves the credit window about six weeks into a rebuild, the weeks run since form a new, lower block, and R falls to a share of that; on the synthetic runner of appendix A of the proposal, run exactly as planned, the re-plans then planned 5% (standard) and 8% (upper) fewer minutes over 32 weeks than the first plan showed, and the week of 285 min came three weeks later at upper (measured 2026-10-25). With the rule the re-plans equal the first plan week for week at both windows. Grade D, a design default: no study tests how long a rebuild should keep its ceiling.
  - **Response to weeks run above plan.** At standard and upper only, the baseline of a loading week is the higher of the planned baseline (as G-2 draws it) and the mean of the minutes actually run in the last `g8.responseWeeks` (4) eligible weeks, within the two bounds stated after this paragraph, and the position's step then applies to it. It applies to a plan the runner is following, that is a re-plan whose stored plan holds the current week after a break shorter than `j7.tier1Days`; a new plan, or one that restarts after a break, starts from the weeks as run (E6, J7) and has no planned minutes to be run above. Eligible weeks are the complete weeks before the current one, newest first, skipping every week that holds a race and every week inside a rest window (a reported window, with its easy days after it, or an injury report); the window reaches back until it holds four, and a state with fewer than four has no response. Recovery weeks count as run. The mean reads the weeks of the athlete state (ADR 0009), so it is the same for every week a plan draws and the chain of planned weeks overtakes it within a few weeks. The response lifts a baseline and never lowers one, follows what the runner did and lags it (one big week moves a four-week mean by a quarter of its excess), and is an absolute number of minutes, never a ratio (G-5). The soft step and the cap of a week are measured from the same baseline, so the generator, the guards and the checklist agree. The week a plan opens on is not stepped from a baseline: its cap is the one the weeks as run give it, so after weeks run under the plan, missed training still lowers it (G-2, G-6). It is off at steady, where a week run above its target still lifts nothing (G-2); off wherever the position is held at steady; off in the weeks the zone leaves out. Above `R` it still lifts the baseline, and the usual 10% then applies to the lifted baseline (amended 2026-10-25: the text said it is off above `R`; the generator, the guards and the checklist read one baseline there, `responseBaseline`). A hold or reduce decision (J5, G-6) caps the week it covers afterwards and so wins, and the hard ceiling and G-1 are applied after it and unchanged. Grade D. At standard and upper it amends the G-2 and G-6 statements that a week run above its plan lifts nothing after it (ADR 0013, decision 5).
  - **Two bounds on the response (amended 2026-10-25).** (1) The response applies only when the newest eligible week, the first week of the window of the mean, was run at or above the minutes the stored plan held for it. A week taken as run past its target counts at the minutes it was planned at (`plannedMinOf`), and a newest week that the stored plan did not hold gives no response. A week run under its target is missed training, which lowers the plan (G-2, G-6) and is never a reason to lift it; the hold or reduce decision that follows such a week keeps its effect. (2) The lifted baseline never passes the planned baseline stepped by the ceiling of two weeks (`g2.rampCeilingTwoWeeks`, with the novice minimum step, through the function the limits use): min(mean, planned × (1 + 30%)) when the mean is higher than the planned baseline. Reason: without them a dip after high weeks lifted a plan that had dropped back to the mean (on the synthetic runner, weeks of 150, 150, 60 and 60 minutes lifted a week of 67 to 101, with the newest week run under its target), and one week of 500 minutes after 120 lifted a baseline of 70 to 185, 2.6 times the plan, and the week after it to 203. The lifts after weeks run above plan stay up to the bound: the proposal's example of 126.75 against a planned 121 is inside it. The bound is a ceiling the engine already holds over two weeks, so it adds no Param and no grade. Grade D.
  - **What stays.** G-1 (1.10× warn, 2.00× override, 30 days, hard in the start paths), the 30% ceiling over 2 weeks and the soft 10% outside the zone (G-2), the tightened profiles (G-3), G-5 (no ratio gates), G-6, the J5 hold and reduce signals, the J7 table and `afterBreak`, the post-race and disruption windows (H12, J9), the recovery cadence and cut (E3) and the 15-item checklist. No session length is credited from the history. Not part of the rule: a longer or history-lifted G-1 reference, an acute:chronic or EWMA ceiling, and an age or experience credit (ADR 0013).
  - **Evidence.** All of it is indirect.
    - Fitness after a reduction. VO2max fell 7% by day 21 and settled 16% below the trained value by day 56 in seven trained subjects who stopped training [coyle-1984-detraining-time-course]. A meta-analysis of 21 studies found a larger fall after long than after short cessation (standardised effects −1.42 and −0.62; heterogeneity 76% in the long group, small-study bias), no significant further change beyond 30 days and a weaker effect when some exercise continued [zheng-2022-detraining-vo2max]. The reviews treat insufficient training of under 4 weeks and of over 4 weeks as separate cases [mujika-2000-detraining-part1, mujika-2000-detraining-part2]; after the long case the gains of long-trained athletes are partly kept and recently acquired gains are lost completely [mujika-2000-detraining-part2] (narrative reviews, level D), so a runner with a year of running is closer to the second group, which argues for a smaller credit. VO2max was held for 15 weeks at 2 or 4 sessions a week with intensity and duration unchanged in 12 young adults [hickson-1981-reduced-frequency], and a review of mostly non-athletes finds endurance performance kept at 2 sessions a week or a third to two thirds less volume when intensity is kept [spiering-2021-minimal-dose], so, by that reading, sparse short easy runs are not a maintenance dose.
    - Tissue. Under 23 days of complete unloading of one leg in 9 young men, patellar tendon stiffness fell 9.8% by day 14 and 29.3% by day 23 [deboer-2007-ulls-tendon-time-course]; an Achilles stiffness gain of 50.3% from 3 months of isometric training in 9 men was back at its pre-training level after 1 month of detraining [kubo-2012-achilles-training-detraining]. Tendon stiffness adapts mainly to loading magnitude across 37 interventions of 8 weeks or more [bohm-2015-tendon-adaptation-meta]. Reviews frame bone stress injury as microdamage against its removal, a workload error that can show 3-4 weeks later, with tolerance that varies between people and sites [warden-2021-bsi-optimal-workload, warden-2014-bsi-long-distance-runners], and injury as the load of a session set against the capacity of one structure [bertelsen-2017-rri-etiology-framework]. None is a runner who kept running, and none gives a rate for a rebuild; they are the reason the credit is on the weekly total (what fitness carries) and not on the session (what tissue carries).
    - Weekly steps. 874 novices showed no injury difference between weekly progressions under 10%, 10 to 30% and over 30%, with a non-significant signal above 30% [nielsen-2014-weekly-progression]; a review of four studies found none between a 10% and a 24% average weekly increase [damsted-2018-training-load-change]; in 261 runners more were injured after a 20 to 60% weekly increase than under 20% at day 21 and not at days 56 or 98 [damsted-2019-weekly-distance-change]; in 60 novices the runners who stayed healthy averaged +22.1% a week [nielsen-2013-gps-progression]. The one randomised trial of a 10%-rule programme found no effect [buist-2008-graded-training], and a systematic review of 36 studies of 23,047 runners found the evidence on weekly distance and its changes conflicting [fredette-2022-training-parameters-injury-review]. All of it is observational and mostly in novices, and none follows a return after a break. The upper step is the weekly rate at which 2 weeks meet the grade C ceiling and carries its evidence.
    - Sessions and disruptions. The session spike cohort did not separate a return after reduced running from other runs [frandsen-2025-session-spike], which is why G-1 is not loosened. A disruption was a complete cessation within the 16 weeks before a marathon, and 7 days or more cost 5-8% of finish time against the runner's own undisrupted marathon [feely-2022-training-disruptions]; 16 weeks is the span of the longer credit window.
  - **Against.** Everything new here is grade D, and the corpus principle (G6) is that a change toward more load needs stronger evidence than a change toward less. A runner who reports that the plan holds back more than they can carry is also the case in which observational associations mislead: runners who tolerate more are the ones who run more, and fewer than 2 runs a week marks higher injury in the largest cohort [abrahamson-2025-self-reported-characteristics], so sparse running is not protective. The tissue papers suggest that capacity can fall faster than fitness and is built slowly, and nothing dates a runner's tissue after a partial reduction. The zone relies on fitness-side evidence for a quantity that tissue also carries. The 3 × 30 route overturns the "2 years" detail of the engine design's first owner decision and sits against the corpus rule that nothing supports looser limits for experienced runners (G-1). The response is a second place where a plan moves toward more load without an explicit choice each time, and one 150-minute week among three of 100 minutes lifts the next baseline from 100 to 112.5 and, at standard, the next week to 126. The answers are defaults (steady), a clamp (`R`), the hard ceiling and G-1 left as they are, and the order of the work (the lift first), not evidence.

## (h) Ultra-specific rules

Almost nothing in this section has trial evidence. Unless a rule cites evidence, it is a labelled engine default.

- **H1 Progression priority.** For goals of 100 km or more, grow weekly volume before adding pace work or lengthening the long run. For 50 km, keep speed work. Weekly volume, frequency and climbing are the training variables consistently associated with ultra performance across seven cross-sectional cohorts; the longest single run is not. Grade C [tanda-2015-training-anthropometry-100km, coates-2021-physio-determinants-ultra, rust-2012-100km-vs-marathoners, belinchon-2021-mountain-race-predictors, daniel-2024-217km-sleep-training, hoffman-2011-wser-vt100-factors].
  - The experienced-ultrarunner median of about 64 km/week is a descriptive check only, never a target. Grade C [hoffman-2013-ultra-exercise-behavior].
  - Ultra training evidence comes largely from men; no sex-specific values are applied. Grade C [kelly-2023-sex-specific-ultra-review, oloughlin-2019-wellington-sex-predictors].
- **H2 Time on feet.**
  - G-1 applies to both distance and duration, so the long run grows by time within the 1.10× session guard even where coaching convention moves faster. Grade D on duration; a duration-based load ratio gives indirect support only [frandsen-2025-session-spike, cloosterman-2026-gps-load-injury]. It is untested on single sessions and in ultra runners [viljoen-2022-trail-risk-factors].
  - Longest single training run is capped at the lesser of 6 h or 60% of expected race duration. Grade D. No study sets these numbers, and no study links any long-run length to injury [viljoen-2022-trail-risk-factors]. The cap sits inside the range of named-coach conventions (3-5 h to 6-7 h) and below finishers' race-inclusive longest runs, so it is a conservative default [outside-2023-strava-utmb, outside-2023-strava-western-states, outside-2015-orton-koop-100-miles]. The 60% fraction binds only for races expected under 10 h.
  - Race simulation (design intent, not implemented): one rehearsal of up to 8 h, within G-1, no later than 3 weeks before race day and never in the taper, which an entered tune-up race could serve; after a tune-up of 100 km or more H12 applies. No code reads the 8 h value, so the plan and the app do not describe the simulation as available. Grade D [outside-2023-strava-western-states, outside-2023-strava-utmb, outside-2015-orton-koop-100-miles].
  - No longest-run length is presented as required to finish. The null results are underpowered, so this is a copy guard. Grade D [tan-2017-finishers-161km, taksaudom-2017-66km-predictors, outside-2023-strava-utmb].
- **H3 Back-to-backs** (D). No study measures back-to-back long days or links them to finishing or injury; same-day double sessions, an injury factor in one review, are a different structure [viljoen-2022-trail-risk-factors].
  - At most one pair every 2 weeks, in build or specific phases only, never in the taper.
  - Day 2 is no longer than day 1 (default 0.6 of day 1 in the micro-cycle).
  - The pair's combined duration is no more than 1.10× the largest 2-day total in the last 30 days. This extends G-1 by analogy [frandsen-2025-session-spike].
  - The pair counts as one hard block; the next day is rest or easy.
- **H4 Vertical gain and descent.**
  - Per-session gain and per-session descent follow G-1 against their own 30-day maxima, with a minimum step of 100 m. Grade D.
  - Weekly gain follows the 10% soft ramp. Grade D.
  - In the specific phase, long runs work toward the race's gain per km. Grade D.
  - Prescribe climbing effort by RPE, HR or grade-adjusted effort, never by vertical speed: at similar VO2max, maximal ascent speed was about 32% higher at 25% slope than at 10%, while relative VO2 followed power. Grade C [hingrand-2023-power-vs-ascent-speed].
  - Hiking default: power-hike on grades of about 28% and steeper; between about 12.6% and 28% the faster gait depends on climbing rate (walking was cheaper above about 12.6% at 800 m/h). Practise hiking on long runs for trail ultras. Grade C [finiel-2026-uphill-walk-run, giovanelli-2016-vk-energetics, ortiz-2017-30deg-walk-run].
  - About 550 m/h is a reference for sustained ultra climbing only, not a target or a race-average figure. Grade C [jeker-2020-vertical-speed-ultra].
  - A session whose planned gain reaches 150 m per hour of running carries a climbing line: climb by effort, not by speed. The 150 m/h is an engine default; no study sets it, and it lies well below the 550 m/h reference above. Grade D [hingrand-2023-power-vs-ascent-speed, jeker-2020-vertical-speed-ultra].
- **H5 Downhill conditioning.**
  - Start graded downhill running at least 8 weeks before a hilly race, using the descent limits in H4. Grade D.
  - The first moderate downhill exposure comes at least 3 weeks before any heavy downhill session (repeated-bout protection); protection beyond 3 weeks is untested. Grade C [khassetarash-2023-rbe-downhill, mchugh-2003-repeated-bout, bontemps-2020-downhill-review].
  - Treat an unaccustomed session near the research damaging protocol (about -12% slope for 40 min) as a high-damage load; introduce descents progressively. Grade D [bontemps-2020-downhill-review].
  - Downhill running builds knee-extensor strength (9-24%) but not aerobic capacity; keep aerobic work on level or uphill terrain. Grade C [toyomura-2018-downhill-training].
  - Use eccentric-biased strength inside the strength block. Rationale: knee-extensor force fell 29-38% after 110 km [temesi-2015-female-neuromuscular-fatigue]. There is no dose evidence for the block itself (D).
  - **Eccentric emphasis (amended 2026-10-25).** Inside the strength block the emphasis is the slow lowering of every lift, `f7.lowerSeconds` seconds (F7). No loaded eccentric above the lifted load and no nordic or supramaximal exercise is prescribed: the corpus holds no dose for either, and unaccustomed eccentric work causes the damage that the repeated-bout effect protects against [mchugh-2003-repeated-bout]. Grade D.
- **H6 Night.** For races expected to cross darkness, schedule 1-2 headlamp rehearsal runs in the specific phase. No sleep-deprivation training: there is no evidence of benefit and it adds clear risk. Grade D.
- **H7 Fuelling practice.** Long runs rehearse the race fuel plan.
  - Floor: 30-50 g/h carbohydrate, 150-400 kcal/h, and 5-10 g/h protein, from varied foods. Grade A [tiller-2019-issn-ultra-nutrition].
  - Higher tiers: 60 g/h, then 90 g/h (multiple transportable carbohydrates), each unlocked only after the previous tier was tolerated in training. Grade C [jeukendrup-2014-cho-intake, king-2022-high-cho-gut-training].
  - 90-120 g/h is an advanced opt-in, labelled low certainty. Grade C [urdampilleta-2020-cho-120, norte-2026-graded-cho-cp].
  - Gut training: 2-4 weeks of sessions of at least 1 h at the target rate, with symptoms logged on a 0-4 scale. Stop progressing when any symptom scores 2 or more on two sessions in a row (the ladder below is stricter: it holds after one). Grade B for the block, D for the stop rule [costa-2017-gut-training, miall-2018-gut-challenge, martinez-2023-gut-training-sr].
  - **Ladder.** The target is a rung of one ladder: the floor, 60 g/h, then 70, 80 and 90 g/h in steps of 10 g/h, then 100, 110 and 120 g/h. The rungs from 70 to 90 g/h open only after the runner opts in to 90 g/h; the rungs above 90 g/h need a second opt-in, which opens only after a run at 90 g/h was tolerated. A diagnosed gut condition locks the ladder at the floor, removes the gut-training block and shows a clinician disclaimer. Every rung above the floor is labelled low certainty, and a target is a rehearsal, not a promise. The ladder changes the fuel line only, never a session. Grade C for the tiers [jeukendrup-2014-cho-intake, king-2022-high-cho-gut-training, urdampilleta-2020-cho-120, norte-2026-graded-cho-cp]; D for the 10 g/h steps, the opt-in gates and the middle rungs (70 and 80 g/h), which are defaults [costa-2017-gut-training]; the lock is D [costa-2017-eigs-sr].
  - **Counting.** A logged run counts toward the ladder when it lasts at least 120 minutes, or at least 60 minutes in a gut-training week (an unlinked run counts when its date falls in such a week). Its carbohydrate rate, taken over elapsed time because eating happens at stops, must reach 0.9 of the rung being tried, and above 60 g/h at least half of the carbohydrate must come from products with more than one transportable carbohydrate. A skipped log, a run that is too short, a run below the target rate and a run with too little multiple-source carbohydrate move nothing, and the log says which. Grade A for the 2 h threshold, which the review puts at 2 h at about 60% VO2max (the app does not check intensity) [costa-2017-eigs-sr]. Multiple transportable carbohydrates above about 60 g/h are Jeukendrup's advice; the half is a D default, and the 0.9 is a D default with no source [jeukendrup-2014-cho-intake].
  - **Score and movement.** A run's score is the highest of its gut and nausea scores on the 0-4 scale. A counted run scored below 2 is tolerated. The target steps up one rung after one tolerated run, never on a hot day. A run scored 2 or more holds the target at its rung, and the next tolerated run on a day that is not hot lifts the hold. Two such runs in a row, or one scored 4, return the target to the last tolerated rung; a run that does not count is not part of the row. A tolerated rung does not decay after a layoff. **Conflict:** the stop rule above lets progress continue until two bad runs; the ladder holds after one. Resolution: G6, the more conservative rule wins, and the stop rule governs the fall-back. Grade D for every count and score [martinez-2023-gut-training-sr, stuempfle-2015-gi-distress-wser].
  - **Heat.** Whether a run was hot is judged by the weather the run had, on every read, and not by a flag fixed when the log was saved: a WBGT of 18 C or more, or an air temperature of 25 C or more when no WBGT exists; the runner's own yes or no to "it was hot" overrides the weather either way. A hot day never steps the target up, and the target for a hot forecast stays at the last tolerated rung. A run scored 2 or more on a hot day still counts toward the hold and the fall-back. A tolerated run on a hot day changes nothing: it does not step the target up, does not mark the rung tolerated, does not lift a hold and does not end a row of runs scored 2 or more. A hot day also brings the nausea plan forward, as does a nausea score of 2 or more on a counted run: simpler fuel and a slower pace. Grade D, from the direction only: heat worsens gut strain and nausea peaked in the hottest segment of a 161 km race [costa-2017-eigs-sr, stuempfle-2015-gi-distress-wser]. The fuel threshold (WBGT 18 C) is lower than the heat-steering threshold of I9 (WBGT 25 C, an air temperature alone never counts): holding a fuel step on a warm day and dropping a heart-rate ceiling are different questions.
  - **Gut-training week.** The block is 3 weeks by default (2-4) and takes the last specific weeks, ending before the taper. In each of them, up to 3 runs of 60 minutes or more carry the fuel-practice flag with their minutes, days and zones unchanged. The block is removed for a gut condition or when the runner opts out. Grade B for the block, D for the count of runs [costa-2017-gut-training, miall-2018-gut-challenge, martinez-2023-gut-training-sr].
  - **Copy guard.** Wherever fuel appears (the target line, the long-run card, the watch description and the fuel lab) the text carries the H8 fluid wording (drink to thirst, never forced) and the EAH safety card, the clinician disclaimer for a gut condition, and the low-certainty label above the floor. Gut training is described as something that may improve tolerance, with no promise of a faster time and no claim that it protects the gut lining. The only fluid figure is the H8 reference range or the runner's own logged amount. Every number that restates a parameter is interpolated from it. Grade A for the gut-training claim [martinez-2023-gut-training-sr, king-2022-high-cho-gut-training].
  - Never claim gut-barrier protection. No in-race carbohydrate scaling by body mass or sex.
  - **Conflict:** the ISSN range is 30-50 g/h; physiology-based guidance says up to 90 g/h. Resolution: the tested floor is the default, and higher rates are gated by tolerance, because GI distress is the leading DNF cause (35.6% of 161 km non-finishers) [stuempfle-2015-gi-distress-wser].
- **H8 Fluid and sodium.**
  - Drink to thirst, with 450-750 mL/h as a reference range. Never produce fixed volumes beyond thirst. The ACSM 2% limit does not apply. Grade B [hewbutler-2015-eah-consensus, hoffman-2015-sodium-supplementation-eah].
  - Sodium above 575 mg/L is for comfort only, never framed as EAH prevention. Grade C.
  - Weight loss up to 3% is fine; weight gain is a red flag. Grade B [hoffman-2013-eah-hydration-161km].
- **H9 Daily diet.** Carbohydrate 5-8 g/kg/day, periodised. Protein 1.6 g/kg/day, up to 2.5. Grade A [tiller-2019-issn-ultra-nutrition, podlogar-2022-cho-new-horizons].
- **H10 Strength.** A 12-week block at 2 sessions per week with slow-speed plyometric emphasis, plus knee-extensor and plantar-flexor work. Grade C [giovanelli-2017-ultra-sep, pastor-2022-trail-determinants-distances].
  - **Read by the engine (amended 2026-10-25).** For an ultra goal the engine reads `h10.strengthBlockWeeks` and `h10.strengthSessionsPerWeek` in place of the E5 and F-lib Params (shipped 2026-10-25, task E14-3 of the strength epic), so the grade shown beside the block is the ultra one (C). The values equal the E5 and F-lib ones, so no plan changes. A strength session of an ultra goal cites H10 beside F7. The structure of the sessions is F7. Evidence: a 12-week home-based programme lowered the energy cost of running by 3.2-6.4% in 25 male ultra-marathoners, with randomisation not stated [giovanelli-2017-ultra-sep]; performance at 101 km was explained by VO2max, maximal isometric strength and body fat in 16 finishers [pastor-2022-trail-determinants-distances]; and two sessions a week for 10 weeks improved economy durability in 28 well-trained men (B) [zanini-2025-strength-economy-durability-rct]. No trial studies 100-mile or trail runners in training, so the doses are extrapolated and labelled so.
- **H11 Race pacing.** Keep HR at or below VT1 for about 85% of race time and flag any planned effort above VT2. Grade C [fornasiero-2018-ultra-intensity].
- **H12 Post-race recovery.** The window is counted in days from the race (day 0), not in plan weeks; days left in the race week belong to the hold. Every value is a default unless graded otherwise.

Grade B, from one small trial of recreational runners [martinez-navarro-2021-marathon-week-rct, bernat-adell-2021-marathon-biomarkers, takayama-2017-marathon-one-week].
  - Ultra holds and all step fractions are grade D [irunfar-2014-uhan-ultra-recovery-rules, skurka-2017-roche-100-recovery, cts-2025-koop-ultra-recovery].   - Walking and light cross-training are allowed during the hold. Grade C [martinez-navarro-2021-118km-inflammation-activity].
  - Run caps, all in zone 1: the first step has at most 3 runs on non-consecutive days, each at most 40 min, with no gain target; the second step has runs of at most 60 min; later steps have a longest run of at most 90 min. Grade B for the marathon first step, D otherwise [cts-2025-koop-ultra-recovery, irunfar-2016-powell-post-100-return].
  - No intervals, threshold, race pace, strides, plyometrics or downhill sessions until the last step ends; a new plan's first quality session waits for that date. Grade C [hoffman-2017-161km-recovery-determinants, petersen-2007-marathon-muscle-mechanics, garciavaliente-2026-trail-damage-review].
  - Modifier: the hold is multiplied by 1.5 (rounded up), once, when the race descended at least 30 m per km (loss divided by distance; skipped when the loss is unknown), the runner is 40 or older (F5), or the runner has no earlier completed race of that class in the history. Grade C for the direction, D for the values [garciavaliente-2026-trail-damage-review, easthope-2010-trail55km-masters, skurka-2017-roche-100-recovery].
  - Check-in gate, delay only: a stage boundary advances only when the last 2 check-ins before it are not poor (J2 fixed scale, or worse than the runner's own pre-race median when 14 or more check-ins exist in the 28 days before the race) and report no pain or illness; otherwise the boundary moves one day at a time. The gate is computed from the check-ins each time the plan is built. It never shortens or advances a stage. Without check-ins the default schedule runs, and a check-in older than 3 days before a boundary neither delays nor releases it, so a poor rating followed by silence holds a step for at most 3 days. After 14 days of extension, the J8 checklist is shown. Pain applies n1 and n2, illness n3. Grade B for the direction, D for the counts [hoffman-2017-161km-recovery-determinants, saw-2016-subjective-vs-objective-monitoring, gaudino-2019-recovery-stress-ultra].
  - HRV and resting heart rate can delay a stage (J3, J4), never advance one; HRV often stays below baseline for more than a week after 100 miles. Grade C [paech-2021-100mile-hrv, martinez-navarro-2019-118km-cardiac-hrv, tiller-2025-ultramarathon-muscle-damage].
  - The window applies after the plan's own goal race (projected) and at the start of a new plan when a completed race of marathon distance or longer lies within the last 35 days (plus the modifier) of today, so that plan cannot place quality before the window ends. After the last step, J7 picks the restart tier from the hold length and G-6 applies. The steps are exempt from G-2, like the restart floor, not from G-1 (the race itself is excluded from the reference).
  - Plan weeks: the steps start on the day the hold ends, even inside the race week, so a marathon's first run falls on day 2 whatever the weekday of the race. The race-week days inside the hold are rest days with the hold copy; the race-week days after it carry the first step's runs (easy, within the run caps, on non-consecutive days, counted with the first step's runs of the later weeks); the race day itself carries no run. The Monday weeks after the race week carry the rest of the steps. A week's target is the sum over the steps it touches of the step's fraction × the pre-taper reference week × (days of the week in that step ÷ 7), held to what the run caps allow and to the normal band of G-1: no run of a week is longer than 1.10× the longest run of the 30 days before it, read over the completed runs and the earlier weeks as drawn, so a window that leaves no recent run in the reference regrows its runs at 1.10× a week instead of the step's cap; with no run at all in the reference the week's runs are of one length. A plan built inside a window opens with the Monday weeks that have most of their days (4 or more) before the window ends; the normal plan starts from the week after them. The first step counts its 3 runs across the weeks it spans, on every other open day from its first day (the trial ran days 2, 4 and 6).
  - **Conflict:** coach conventions for 100 miles range from short runs in week 1 [cts-2025-koop-ultra-recovery, irunfar-2016-powell-post-100-return, irunfar-2023-torrence-post-ultra-downtime] to a week of rest [skurka-2017-roche-100-recovery] or 10-16 days off [irunfar-2014-uhan-ultra-recovery-rules]. Resolution: G6, the longer hold wins; all are grade D.
  - **Conflict:** E3 says age alone does not cut load. Resolution: E3 concerns chronic training load; the masters modifier lengthens an acute post-race window by a few days and rests on direct post-race data [easthope-2010-trail55km-masters].
  - The earlier rule (races of 100 km or more: 7 days without running, then at least 7 days of reduced running) rested on one 24 h elite case [byrne-2024-elite-24h-training] and is replaced by this table.

## (i) Environment

- **I1 Weather index.** WBGT from the Liljegren model, using hourly forecast temperature, humidity, pressure, wind and solar radiation. Implement it as a pure function in `shared/`.
  - Fall back to air temperature (optimum 10-17.5 C) and label which index was used.
  - Add no separate humidity or pressure penalty.
  - Grade C [liljegren-2008-wbgt-model, mantzios-2022-weather-endurance, elhelou-2012-environment-marathon].
- **I2 Optimum band.** WBGT 7.5-15 C carries no penalty. Grade C.
- **I3 Hot-side race penalty.**
  - Elite slope: 0.35% per C above 15 C (range 0.3-0.4). Grade C [mantzios-2022-weather-endurance].
  - Marathon cross-check, relative to WBGT 5-10 C: +0.8% at 10-15, +1.6% at 15-20, +2.8% at 20-25. Grade C [ely-2007-weather-marathon].
  - **Conflict:** the prediction topic derives about 0.18% per C. Resolution: in the 20-25 C band the two sources agree (2.6% vs 2.8%), so use the slope, and use the table to check marathon outputs.
  - Do not extrapolate above WBGT 25 C; show a warning instead.
- **I4 Ability multiplier.** 1.0 at a 2:30 marathon-equivalent, rising linearly to 2.0 at 4:30 or slower. Calibrate from the athlete's own logs. Grade D [ely-2007-weather-marathon, knechtle-2019-boston-weather].
  - The prediction topic's 1.5 placeholder for runners over 4 h falls inside this ramp. The ramp is the more conservative choice for pacing.
  - No sex term.
- **I5 Pacing shape in heat.** Mid-pack and slower runners get a uniformly slower target. Fast (top-quartile) runners get a conservative first half. Grade C [ely-2008-pacing-temperature].
- **I6 Ultras.**
  - Apply the penalty only to hours in daytime heat, and relax it at night. The 161 km reference is about 8% slower in a hot year. Grade C [parise-2011-161km-pacing-heat].
  - Label every heat adjustment for events over 6 h as low confidence. Grade D [bouscaren-2019-marathon-vs-ultra-heat].
- **I7 Training in heat.**
  - Above the optimum band, prescribe by RPE or an HR cap; pace is secondary.
  - Show a hydration reminder and reduce intensity on the first hot days of a season. Grade D [periard-2021-exercise-heat-stress, racinais-2015-heat-consensus].
  - Use the forecast to suggest a cooler time of day for a run (D). Strength, mobility and rest get no heat note.
  - Never hold a fixed HR zone in heat: HR rose 17-19% at 35 C while capacity fell [wingo-2020-drift-vo2max-heat-running].
- **I8 Heat acclimation.**
  - Trigger: race WBGT 18 C or higher (D).
  - Block: 7-14 days of daily heat exposure, minimum 5, longer if time allows.
  - Decay: 2.5% of the adaptation per day, reaching zero credit at about 40 days (the 40 days is an extrapolation).
  - Top up inside the taper; re-acclimation is 8-12 times faster than decay.
  - Acclimation may reduce the penalty by at most 50% (D).
  - Grade A [racinais-2015-heat-consensus, tyler-2016-heat-adaptation-meta, daanen-2018-acclimation-decay, periard-2015-heat-acclimation-adaptations].
- **I9 Warnings.** Warn on WBGT above 25 C, high solar load, a large rise from one day to the next, and high specific humidity. WBGT is a screening index, not a safety guarantee. Grade C [thorsson-2021-pet-vs-wbgt].
- **I10 Cold.** No numeric cold penalty; show advisory copy only. Grade D [castellani-2015-cold-stress].
- **I11 Gradient and GAP.**
  - Convert terrain to flat-equivalent effort with the Minetti cost curve, as a ratio to level cost (3.40 J/kg/m): 5.6 at +45%, 0.51 at −20%, 1.15 at −45%. The full polynomial comes from the paper's text; the abstract gives anchors only. Grade C [minetti-2002-gradient-cost, vernillo-2017-graded-running-review].
  - Cap downhill credit at the −20% minimum and treat the result as an upper bound on speed. Do not turn the downhill metabolic saving into a full pace credit; personalise the cap from the athlete's own descents. Grade C [minetti-2002-gradient-cost, genitrini-2022-downhill-pacing].
  - Late in long mountain events, descent cost rises about 13% while level and uphill cost do not; the late-race term defaults to +13% on descents in events over 6 h and is flagged low confidence. Grade C [vernillo-2015-fatigue-energy-cost].
- **I12 Altitude.** On courses above about 800 m, expect acute VO2max to fall about 6.3% per 1,000 m (range 4.6-7.5%) and adjust effort expectations and predictions approximately; acclimatisation is not modelled. Grade C [wehrlin-2006-altitude-linear].

## (j) Adaptation (weekly re-plan plus daily readiness)

- **J1 Inputs.** Completed versus planned minutes, executed versus planned TID (flag easy runs that leave Z1 by more than 5 pp, D [munoz-2014-polarized-recreational]), load, efficiency index, the daily check-in, HRV, resting HR, sleep, pain, illness, and heat.
- **J2 Subjective first.** A daily check-in (fatigue, soreness, sleep, mood, stress, illness) is the primary readiness input. HRV and resting HR only corroborate it. Grade A [saw-2016-subjective-vs-objective-monitoring].
- **J3 Daily readiness changes downgrade only.**
  - A flag can turn a hard session into an easy one or a rest day. It never adds load. Grade A [duking-2021-hrv-guided-meta, manresa-rocamora-2021-hrv-guided-meta].
  - A flag counts only when it persists for 2 days, or when 1 flagged day comes with a poor subjective score. Grade D.
  - Zones remain the intensity anchor. Grade C [ranieri-2025-racepace-hr-hrv-rct].
  - After a race (H12), readiness can also delay a stage of the return; it never shortens or advances one. A normal rating is not evidence of muscle recovery: after 161 km, soreness and fatigue cleared by day 5 while 400 m speed was still 12% slower. Grade B [hoffman-2017-161km-recovery-determinants, saw-2016-subjective-vs-objective-monitoring].
- **J4 HRV.**
  - Use ln RMSSD, morning or nocturnal, against a rolling 10-day mean − 1 SD, or flag a 2-day decline. Require at least 10 valid readings. Grade C [kiviniemi-2007-hrv-guided-rct].
  - The change must also exceed the device noise floor: 10% for Garmin, Polar and Whoop class devices, 6% for Oura Gen 4 class, using 7-night averages. Grade C [dial-2025-nocturnal-wearables].
  - For users with menstrual cycles, use a baseline of about 35 days and widen the band by 9 pp. Grade B/D [dejager-2026-wearable-hrv-menstrual].
  - **Conflict:** the AI-coaching topic proposed a 7-day window. Resolution: Kiviniemi's 10-day window, because it is the RCT protocol.
  - HRV alone never triggers or clears a recovery week. Grade A [bellenger-2016-autonomic-hr-meta-analysis].
- **J5 Weekly decision.**
  - **Progress** when the week was completed, no flag is active, there is no pain, and the efficiency index is stable or rising.
  - **Hold** (keep the volume, trim the next hard session) on:
    - one sustained signal;
    - completing less than 80% of planned minutes (D);
    - a sleep warning: 7-night average under 7 h or under the athlete's stated need. Grade C [walsh-2021-sleep-athlete-consensus];
    - 2 or more REDs flags;
    - the first days of heat.
  - **Reduce** (insert a recovery week) when 2 independent signals (subjective decline, efficiency decline, a sleep-efficiency drop of about 2 pp) persist for about 7 days. Grade B, low confidence [roete-2021-functional-overreaching-markers, murphy-2024-overreaching-sleep]. Illness and pain also reduce (section n).
- **J6 Masters perceived-recovery gate.** If soreness, fatigue or motivation is still worse than the athlete's own median 48 h after a hard session, defer or convert the next quality session.
  - The recovery multiplier is 1.0 by default and may rise to 1.25 only from the athlete's own data. Grade C/D [borges-2018-masters-hiit-recovery].
- **J7 Breaks.**
  - Restart volume as a fraction of the pre-break week:

    | Layoff       | Restart volume                                                             |
    | ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | Under 7 days | 100%                                                                       |
    | 7-14 days    | 90%                                                                        |
    | 15-28 days   | 75%                                                                        |
    | Over 28 days | 50-60%, with E6 setting the first week when the result is below 3 × 20 min |

    Then the normal limits apply. Grade D [mujika-2000-detraining-part1, coyle-1984-detraining-time-course, zheng-2022-detraining-vo2max].

  - When the restart falls below 3 × 20 min, E6 sets the first week. When the 30-day window holds no run, no first-week session exceeds the J7 fraction times the longest run of the last 90 days; under 20 min this becomes ladder entry, and with no run in 90 days E6 path 4 applies. G-1 has no reference after 30 days off, so this fallback keeps the J7 fraction at session level. Grade D [frandsen-2025-session-spike, coyle-1984-detraining-time-course, zheng-2022-detraining-vo2max].
  - After a post-race window (H12), the layoff is the hold length (including the modifier). The first week is the lesser of the J7 fraction times the pre-taper reference week and the last step's actual minutes × (1 + ramp) (G-6); for 100 miles that is 0.825 of the reference, not 0.90. That first week is exempt from G-2 up to that volume, like the restart floor week, and a baseline for the weeks after it; G-1 still binds it, so a long window that leaves only short runs in the 30-day reference holds the long run down until it regrows at 1.10× per session. The reference counts each step run at the length it is placed, so a restart floor of 3 × N after a short last step is cut to that band (never under 5 min) rather than left out.

  - **Conflict:** the volume topic says 50-70% after any gap of 7 days or more. Resolution: VO2max falls only about 7% by 21 days, so halving after 7-14 days is not justified. The 50-70% figure matches the tiers above 14 days.
  - A gap of 7 days or more in the 16 weeks before a race also widens and slows the projection by 5-8%. Grade B [feely-2022-training-disruptions].
  - Model detraining as saturating: 7% by day 21, 16% by day 56, capped at 15-20%.
  - **Tissue capacity after a break (2026-10-25; evidence only, the table above is unchanged).** In 9 young men, patellar tendon stiffness fell 9.8% by day 14 and 29.3% by day 23 of complete unloading of one leg [deboer-2007-ulls-tendon-time-course]. In 9 young men, an Achilles stiffness gain of 50.3% from 3 months of isometric training was back at its pre-training level after 1 month of detraining [kubo-2012-achilles-training-detraining]. A meta-analysis of 27 loading studies of 8 weeks or more finds tendon stiffness adapting mainly to loading magnitude [bohm-2015-tendon-adaptation-meta]. Two reviews describe bone damage and its repair as a process of weeks to months [warden-2014-bsi-long-distance-runners, warden-2021-bsi-optimal-workload]. These are complete-unloading studies and expert reviews in young non-runners, with no running outcome. They suggest that tissue capacity can fall faster than the fitness losses above and is built slowly, which gives no support to crediting a past block to session length. The J7 fractions are not derived from them (the day-23 tendon figure, stiffness about 71% of baseline, is close to the 15-28 day tier, but the two are different quantities). They date no loss for a runner who keeps running a little, and **no paper gives a retraining rate for runners returning to a prior peak**: the tiers stay a grade D convention until running evidence on retraining speed exists. Grade D.
  - Maintenance mode: 2 runs per week, volume cut 33-66%, intensity kept, for up to 15 weeks. Grade C [spiering-2021-minimal-dose, hickson-1981-reduced-frequency].
- **J8 Fatigue states.**
  - Three states: normal, possible accumulated fatigue, persistent suppression.
  - At 14 days of unexplained decline: suggest a deload and show a clinician checklist (energy intake, iron, illness, sleep).
  - At 28 days: a stronger medical-review prompt.
  - Never diagnose OTS or NFOR.
  - Grade D [meeusen-2013-overtraining-consensus, weakley-2022-ots-systematic-review].
  - **Conflict:** the topics gave 14 days and 4 weeks. Resolution: both are used, as two escalation steps.
- **J9 Disruption windows** (new in phase 3a; grade D as a whole: the parts are conventions built on n-3, J7 and G-3, and no study sets a window length, a wait after a niggle or a return ramp for a recreational runner).
  - A window is a period the runner reports: ill (above or below the neck), a niggle, short on time or travelling. It has a first day, which may lie in the past, a last day and a running mode: no running, or short easy runs capped at a stated number of minutes (the F5 easy-run floor or more, at most 4 h). Illness is always no running. The plan is built around the window, not into it: minutes that fall inside it are lost, never added back to another day or week (G-6). Grade D.
  - **Illness** follows n-3 exactly: rest through the window and the first symptom-free day, then 3 easy days before any intensity. Above and below the neck use the same gate; the copy for a chest, fever or aches adds a clinician line. The counts come from n-3, not from this rule. Grade D [snyders-2022-respiratory-illness-rts, schwellnus-2016-load-illness-ioc].
  - **A niggle** has no symptom-free gate, because the runner reports the last day of the window. Inside it running is held, or capped and easy only; after it comes easy running only for `j9.niggleEasyDays` days (3, the count n-3 uses for illness), then quality returns. Grade D [sanford-2024-return-to-running, snyders-2022-respiratory-illness-rts].
  - **Short on time and travel** hold running or cap the longest run of each day at the stated minutes, and quality stays allowed where a run fits. No easy tail follows. Grade D.
  - **Return.** A window that holds running for 7 days or more restarts at the J7 fraction of the reference week (the week before the window), for the days lost counted to the first day running is allowed again; the symptom-free day after an illness is one of them. The fraction holds the return week, prorated by its open days, and the first full week after it: one low week alone would let the next week return to 110% of the week before the window through G-2. Windows that overlap or touch count as one layoff, and the restart is never applied twice to one break. Grade D [mujika-2000-detraining-part1, coyle-1984-detraining-time-course, zheng-2022-detraining-vo2max, frandsen-2025-session-spike].
  - **Injury report.** A niggle with pain at or above `pain.flagScore` or a gait change (n-2) applies the tightened recent-injury profile (G-3): the weekly ramp and the hard 1.10x session limit, from the first day of the window until the return plus the length of the injury-return ladder (6 weeks, one per level). The recovery cadence stays as it is. Grade D [hulme-2017-risk-protective, relph-2023-couch-to-5k-injury, sanford-2024-return-to-running].
  - **Weekly decision.** A week that a window touches is a hold with a disruption signal, and the days a window covers do not count as unrun, so one illness never cuts volume three times (J5). Grade D.
  - **Limits.** A window lasts at most `j9.maxWindowDays` days, the same as the third J7 tier, so a longer break is a break under J7 and the start paths. It may start at most `j9.maxBackdateDays` days before today, so a runner who opens the app late gets a plan that stops asking about those days. It ends no later than the goal race. Grade D [feely-2022-training-disruptions, mujika-2000-detraining-part1].

  - **Conflict:** the topic gives 14 days as the default for 10 km to half-marathon and 7-10 days for 5-10 km. Resolution: 10 km takes 10 days, the overlap point.

- **K2 Volume.**
  - Mean taper volume is 0.40-0.59 of the pre-taper reference week. Grade A.
  - Weekly multipliers: 3-week taper 0.75 / 0.55 / 0.35; 2-week taper 0.65 / 0.40. Grade D.
  - Taper weeks must decrease strictly, except that 7-10 day tapers may be step tapers. Grade B [thomas-2005-taper-model-simulation].
  - Heavier builds use the longer, deeper end of the range. Grade C.
- **K3 Intensity and frequency.**
  - At least 1 quality session per week at pre-taper pace, with fewer reps. Grade A [mujika-2010-intense-training-taper].
  - Run days fall by no more than 20%. Grade B [mujika-2002-taper-frequency-middle-distance].
- **K4 Overreaching at taper start.** Use the deeper end of the volume range, flag the higher illness risk, and do not extend the taper. Grade C [aubry-2014-overreaching-before-taper].
- **K5 Taper check-in.** A weekly check-in on illness, niggles and sleep. A concern leads to rest. An HRV drop needs corroboration before any alert. Grade C/D [hug-2014-taper-marathon-hrv, timpka-2020-health-complaints-heat-taper].
- **K6 Race week.**
  - For races of 90 min or more: 8-10 g/kg/day of carbohydrate in the final 24-48 h, with little running. Grade C [bussau-2002-carb-loading-one-day].
  - Heat top-up per I8.
  - Ultra fuel plan per H7, plus the EAH safety card (n).
  - Marathon pacing warning when the first half is faster than 94% of CS. Grade C [smyth-2020-cs-training-data, smyth-2018-pacing-start-end].
- **K7 Expected benefit.** Describe it as about 0.5-3%, as a sketch. The 2.6% marathon cohort median is an association, not a promise. Grade D.

## (l) Performance prediction with uncertainty

- **L1 Model hierarchy.**
  - Personal exponent or a two-parameter model when 2 or more races at different distances exist. Grade C [blythe-2016-individual-prediction, emig-2020-big-data, peronnet-1989-running-model].
  - Riegel with exponent 1.06 only for targets up to 21.1 km. Grade C [vickers-2016-race-times].
  - CP or CS fits only from efforts spanning 3-30 min; validated extrapolation reaches 60 min. Grade C [ruizalias-2023-long-duration-power].
  - Refit on a rolling 8-12 week window. Grade D [emig-2020-big-data].
- **L2 Marathon.**
  - From a single anchor of 21.1 km or shorter: add a 3% placeholder slowdown. Grade D.
  - The interval half-width is at least 6% of the predicted time. Reported errors are 5.9%, 7.7-8.0%, and 5.2% with durability added. Grade C [oficial-casado-2026-valencia-vdot, smyth-2022-durability-decoupling].
  - Target pace prior: 93% of CS at 150 min, falling to 79% at 360 min. Low-decoupling runners about 0.88 of CS, high-decoupling about 0.82. Grade C.
  - Decoupling bands: low below 1.1, moderate 1.1-1.2, high 1.2 or more; onset at 1.025. Grade C.
- **L3 Covariates.** Sex, age, BMI, weekly distance, tempo share, course and weather. Use at least 2 sources for any prediction. Grade A/C [keogh-2019-marathon-prediction-equations].
- **L4 Ultras.**
  - Anchor on the marathon PB and prior ultra results, plus weekly km, training pace and longest run. Grade D [knechtle-2011-100km-predictors, knechtle-2011-24h-ultra, knechtle-2010-100km-predictors].
  - Show a wide range; never hard-code a published equation.
- **L5 Trail.** Do not transfer road CS directly to trail targets: the classic road model explains trail performance less well. Widen the interval by 50% (the amount is D) and let it grow with gain per km. Grade A for widening, D for the amount [dewaal-2021-trail-physiology-sr, ehrstrom-2018-short-trail].
- **L6 Minimum band.** Every model-based forecast carries at least ±3% until at least 3 scored races exist (L7). Grade C [busso-2023-impulse-response-validity].
- **L7 Back-testing.** The app compares its predictions with what happened and does not change them. Grade D.
  - Weekly logging: one prediction per goal per local week, the latest of the week, up to the day before the race. Nothing is logged on or after race day, once a result exists, or without a distance or a prediction.
  - Scoring: a recorded result scores each earlier prediction for a comparable course, meaning a distance within 2% of the raced distance with climb counted as flat distance (L5). The signed error is the result minus the prediction, as a share of the prediction; negative is faster. A did-not-finish is shown and not scored; a prediction made after the fact is never scored. Grade D [blythe-2016-individual-prediction].
  - Coverage and pattern: coverage is counted per race on the final prediction, and a result on the edge of the range is inside. The weekly predictions of one race depend on each other, so a pattern across races (how often the result was inside, the median signed and absolute error) uses one point per race and appears only after 3 scored races (G3). Grade D [hecksteden-2015-individual-response, mann-2014-high-low-responders].
  - No recalibration without acceptance: the record never changes a prediction, the model or the minimum band (L6) by itself; any recalibration from results is a separate rule that the runner accepts first.

## (n) Red flags and disclaimer

The app must surface each of these. It states facts and points to care; it does not diagnose or prescribe treatment.

1. **Focal bone pain or point tenderness.** Stop progression and recommend medical assessment.
   - Design intent, not implemented: high-risk BSI sites (femoral neck, navicular, anterior tibia, fifth metatarsal) require a date entered by a clinician before any running is generated. Today the engine holds progression and advises an assessment, and a recent bone stress injury takes the injury-return ladder (G-3).
   - Design intent, not implemented: after that, the return is walk-run, distance before intensity. Grade A/C/D [hoenig-2023-bsi-return-to-sport, george-2024-tibial-bsi-return-to-run, barrack-2014-bone-stress-triad].
2. **Pain that changes gait, or any injury report.** Hold progression and apply the recent-injury profile. Grade D.
3. **Illness symptoms.** Suspend intensity sessions. Return with 1 symptom-free day, then 3 easy days before any intensity. Never infer illness from HRV or resting HR alone. Grade D [snyders-2022-respiratory-illness-rts, schwellnus-2016-load-illness-ioc].
4. **REDs signs.** Unintended weight loss, menstrual dysfunction, recurrent BSI or illness, persistent fatigue, GI symptoms, disordered eating.
   - Screen all users at onboarding and every 12 weeks, including men on ultra plans. Offer the LEAF-Q to women.
   - Two or more flags hold progression and show a clinician prompt.
   - Grade A/C/D [mountjoy-2023-reds-consensus, melin-2014-leaf-q, hoeg-2022-ultramarathon-triad].
5. **Possible iron deficiency.** Fatigue or underperformance for 2 weeks at stable load: suggest blood testing through a clinician. Give no supplement advice. Grade D [sim-2019-iron-athlete, burden-2015-iron-treatment-meta].
6. **Persistent suppression.** Per J8. No overtraining diagnosis.
7. **EAH.** Nausea, headache, confusion, swelling or weight gain means seek medical care and do not drink more. Show a stronger alert for events over 17 h or in heat. Grade A [hewbutler-2015-eah-consensus, hewbutler-2017-eah-update].
8. **Heat.** WBGT above 25 C, high solar load, or the first hot days of a season (I9).
9. **Postpartum symptoms.** Pelvic heaviness, leaking or pain pause progression and refer to a pelvic health professional. Postpartum progression is gated by symptoms, not dates. Grade C/D [deering-2024-postpartum-return-to-running, moore-2021-postpartum-return-to-running].
10. **Cardiac symptoms.** Chest pain, fainting or palpitations during exercise mean stop and seek care. This is a safety convention; no study in the corpus covers it. Grade D.

**Disclaimer** (shown at onboarding, on every plan and on every race plan):

> This plan is a sketch built from published research on groups of runners and from conservative defaults where research is missing. It cannot guarantee safety or performance, and it is not medical, nutrition or injury advice. Stop and see a qualified clinician for pain, illness or the warning signs listed. Many numbers are defaults that should change with your own experience, and the app shows which ones.

## (o) Delivery to devices

The plan reaches a watch, a workout file or a calendar as steps with targets. The targets come from the same resolver as every other surface, so this section adds only what is particular to a device. It states conventions and one measurement-quality argument; no trial tests how a runner should be cued on a watch. Grade D unless noted.

- **O1 Targets on the device.**
  - An easy, recovery, long, back-to-back or hike step carries one heart-rate ceiling, the top of the current zone 1. It is written as a band from 40 bpm up to the ceiling, so the watch alerts only above the ceiling and never for a low reading. The floor is `o1.hrFloorBpm`. Grade D.
  - Quality work (threshold, intervals, race pace) carries a pace band, one per rep. Recoveries, strides and hill repeats carry no target and are run by effort. Grade D.
  - A step shorter than 180 s carries no heart-rate target (`o1.hrTargetMinStepSec`). Wrist optical heart rate is accurate on average in steady running but errs more when activity changes, so a short step would alert on a reading that lags the effort. The 180 s length is a default; the direction is grade C [bent-2020-ppg-inaccuracy, gillinov-2017-wearable-hr-aerobic].
  - On a day steered by effort (heat, I9), no step carries a heart-rate or pace target; the heart rate stays in the text as a guide only. Grade D.
  - A title sent to a watch or a calendar names the kind of session and its length and nothing else: no injury, illness, pain, return, post-race, eased or run-walk wording, and no reason for a change. Grade D.

## (p) Methods (presets)

A method preset is a named choice, made when a plan is generated, among four structural values: the run-day cap (F5), the long-run share of the week (design-macro), the length of the build phase (E2) and the table of quality kinds by phase (F2). It overrides no guard, no ceiling and no option. The limits of section (g), the class ceilings (`peakWeeklyMin.*`, `peakLongRunMin.*`), the load setting (G-8), the E6 start paths, the holds of section (n), the taper (section k) and the checklist are never overridable, and a preset sets none of `recoveryCycle`, `tidModel`, `strengthSessions` and `loadAppetite`. A preset applies to the goal classes it names; a goal outside them, or a plan held by a persistent condition of the load setting (a recent injury, a bone stress injury, BSI factors, a RED-S hold, bone pain), reads the default and says why (methods proposal, sections 4.3 to 4.5).

**Every preset is grade D: a choice, not a better plan.** No study compares the plans these methods describe, and none shows that matching a method to a characteristic of the runner gives better races (`research/topics/training-methods.md`). The one trial that reports such a match, Qin 2025, was read in full on 2026-10-31 and is not admitted (README method step 16); no rule cites it. Each rule below says what it rests on, at what level, and what is extrapolated. The numeric values are `Param`s of `shared/engine/params/p-methods.ts` (task M2: `p2.runDays` under P2 and `p4.buildWeeks` under P4), each grade D with a default note; this section writes the rules and their evidence, and the numeric table has their rows.

- **P1 Default method.** With the method absent or `default`, no override applies: the plan is the one that sections (d) to (k) produce, as today, and the history oracle pins it. The default carries the best-supported shape of this document: the traditional order of E1 (grade B: a systematic review of reverse periodization found it no more effective than traditional or block periodization, in 11 studies of low to moderate quality), a base that comes first, at most two quality sessions a week with zones 2 and 3 at about 20% of time (F2), run days that follow volume and availability up to 6 (F5) and a long run of about 0.35 of the week (`macroDesign.longRunShareOfWeek`; D7 for 5 km and 10 km goals). Grade D as a choice: no study shows the default plan to be better than P2 or P4, and the components keep the grades of their own rules [casado-2022-elite-runners-review, gonzalezrave-2022-reverse-periodization].
- **P2 Three run days** (goals of 5 km, 10 km and the half marathon only).
  - **Value.** The run-day cap of F5 is 3 (the default cap, `f5.mastersMaxRunDays`, is 6; a preset may lower it to 3 and not below, and never raises it). The quality sessions, the long run and every other value are the default's, so a week holds the long run, the quality sessions and, where the minutes allow, an easy run. Weekly minutes are the plan's, and with fewer days each run is longer where G-1 allows; a run that G-1 would exceed is shorter and its minutes are not made up (G-6). A runner on an E6 start path keeps the novice days (`f5.noviceRunDays`).
  - **Classes.** 5 km, 10 km and half-marathon goals only (decided 2026-10-31, decision 6 of the methods proposal), whose class ceilings three runs of 60 to 90 minutes can carry. A marathon or longer goal reads P1. Whether P2 reaches marathon goals, with a weekly-minutes cap or a cross-training session kind, is undecided.
  - **Rests on.** Published three-day structures, as practice, grade D: the FIRST programme page (three quality runs and two cross-training sessions a week) [furman-2023-first-three-quality-runs] and the Lydiard Foundation's pages (a starter plan of three run days for a beginner, and a pyramid "easily adapted to the number of days you wish to train") [lydiardfoundation-2026-lydiard-principles]. Direction from studies, grade C: three days a week had fewer injuries than five in young men jogging hard (12% against 39%) [pollock-1977-frequency-duration-injury]; three against two days in novices was not significant [kluitenberg-2016-nlstart2run-training-factors]; at equal monthly volume the frequency of training was unrelated to marathon time in men in one race [yamaguchi-2023-volume-frequency-run-distance]; one 8-week trial of 38 recreational runners found a three-session moderate-intensity plan gave gains similar to a four-session polarized plan on matched training impulse with 17% less time, a contrast of intensity and frequency together [festa-2019-polarized-vs-focused-recreational]. Against: across marathon cohorts, more weekly runs coincided with faster finishes in univariate models [doherty-2020-marathon-training-meta]. No study holds volume equal and varies the run days in recreational runners over 5 km to the half marathon, so the cap is extrapolated from direction and practice.
  - **Not FIRST.** FIRST's three days are three hard runs with two cross-training sessions. The engine keeps one or two quality sessions and has no cross-training kind, so a screen may name the shape and never FIRST's results.
  - Grade D: a choice, not a better plan. It changes no guard and no ceiling, and no week plans more minutes than the default's (see the constraint below), so it does not move toward more load (G6).
- **P3 Not offered.** A preset with a shorter long run (the long-run share of the week lowered from 0.35 to 0.30) is not offered (decided 2026-10-31, decision 6 of the methods proposal).
  - It moves the long run further below the floors of D3, which are grade B: a longest run above 21 km for the half marathon and at least one of 25 km for the marathon [fokkema-2020-half-marathon-volume-long-run]. On the twelve matrix athletes the marathon plan's long run falls from 115 to 99 minutes, about 19 km (methods proposal, section 4.6).
  - It shortens the weeks that `longRunFloor` counts toward the longer run, so the feasibility label reads a time goal as nearer while the long run falls short of the floor.
  - The long-run evidence is mixed: a longest run under 25 km went with slower marathons (self-reported categories) [fokkema-2020-half-marathon-volume-long-run]; at equal monthly volume it was unrelated to marathon time (men, one race) [yamaguchi-2023-volume-frequency-run-distance]; across marathon cohorts longer longest runs coincided with faster finishes in univariate models [doherty-2020-marathon-training-meta]. No source read for this section prescribes a share of 0.30; the Hansons page the proposal names for a 25 to 30% share was not read.
  - The id stays unused so that P4 keeps its number; no `Param` carries it.
- **P4 Longer base** (every goal class with a plan long enough to have a base).
  - **Value.** `macroDesign.buildWeeks` is 4 (the default is 6; a preset may shorten it to 4 and not below, and never lengthens it): the build phase lasts up to 4 weeks and base takes the remainder (E2), up to 2 weeks longer. The specific phase (6 to 8 weeks) and the taper are the default's. In the code table of quality kinds by phase (`QUALITY_BY_PHASE`, `micro.ts`), base holds strides only (the default's base holds strides and hills) and the build holds hills and threshold (the default's build holds threshold and intervals); the specific, taper and race rows are the default's. The shares of E4 (zone 1 at least 80%, zone 2 at most 12% in base) are unchanged, and no week plans more minutes than the default's (see the constraint below), so the preset moves intensity later and no load. A plan with no base to lengthen reads P1.
  - **Rests on.** Lydiard's order as structure, practice, grade D: aerobic conditioning, then hill strength, then anaerobic development, with the schedule written backwards from the goal and the remaining time given to base, as the Lydiard Foundation's principles page states it, with no length for any phase [lydiardfoundation-2026-lydiard-principles]; a review's secondary account of the 1997 book (a 10 to 12 week base, 6 to 8 weeks of hills, 10 to 12 weeks of competition; the book was not read) [casado-2023-lactate-guided-threshold]. The order of E1 is grade B [casado-2022-elite-runners-review, gonzalezrave-2022-reverse-periodization], and one 16-week trial in well-trained men at constant load found pyramidal then polarized better than the other orders, a result about the distribution and not about hills or strides [filipas-2022-pyr-pol-periodization].
  - **Against.** Nothing read shows that a longer base is better: reverse periodization is no more effective than traditional, so order is not shown to matter [gonzalezrave-2022-reverse-periodization]. Competitive athletes' descriptions have high-intensity work incorporated through the cycle [seiler-2010-best-practice-tid], and elite runners use threshold-speed and zone 3 sessions each at least weekly [casado-2022-elite-runners-review], so a strides-only base departs from elite practice (descriptive, another population). A narrative review's advice for marathons runs the other way (reverse periodization, from the authors' experience) [boullosa-2020-recreational-runners-review].
  - Grade D: a choice, not a better plan. It changes no guard and no ceiling, and no week plans more minutes than the default's (see the constraint below), so it does not move toward more load (G6).
- **A preset adds no load.** The engine plans the default plan of the same input beside a preset's and holds the preset, week by week, to the default's target and to the minutes the default places in the week (the long run keeps its length and the other runs share what is left). No week target, no week's planned minutes and no peak week of a preset's plan is above the default's. The check matters because a session of the default can place short of its share (a threshold session in the build), where a base of strides alone would place the whole of it, and the ramp of G-2 reads the minutes placed: without the hold the weeks after it rise (G6). The hold is a limit on a preset and moves no default plan.
- **Not offered as presets.** Polarized (the `tidModel` option already is one, for 5 km and 10 km goals) [rosenblat-2025-tid-ipd-nma, oliveira-2024-pol-meta]. The Norwegian double-threshold model: no controlled study, described in 1500 m and 5000 m runners at 150 to 180 km a week and with double days [casado-2023-lactate-guided-threshold], and two training sessions a day went with injury in trail runners, a link by analogy only [viljoen-2022-trail-risk-factors]. A heart-rate ceiling of 180 minus age: no study of the formula was read, fixed heart-rate and lactate rules for a low-intensity zone varied between individual cyclists [meixner-2025-zone2-variability-cyclists], and C1 and C2 rank thresholds above fixed heart-rate rules. The volumes of Pfitzinger and Lydiard: the ceilings are not a preset's to raise (G6). Hudson's assess-then-build: J5 already adapts weekly, and heart-rate-variability-guided adaptation improved submaximal markers and not performance [duking-2021-hrv-guided-meta]. Daniels's paces: paces stay on the anchors of C2, and the accuracy of the VDOT calculator was tested in 20 runners only [scudamore-2018-vdot-paces-evaluation].

## Top evidence gaps

1. There are no training trials (TID, volume, long run, back-to-backs, taper) in 50 km to 100-mile runners. The whole ultra section is extrapolated or convention.
2. Terrain evidence is thin: no dose data for vert progression or downhill conditioning, no trail-technicality penalty, no race-average vert/h, and the downhill pace-credit cap is unquantified.
3. The session-spike rule rests on one observational cohort. No RCT exists, and duration, gain and heat spikes are untested.
4. Nothing compares 3:1 with 2:1 recovery cycles or tests cutback size. No trial tests a return-to-running protocol after a 50 km or longer race; the H12 ultra holds rest on time-course cohorts (most neuromuscular recovery by day 9, all by day 16 after 166 km) and coach convention.
5. The Banister TRIMP sex constants and formula are unverified, and hrTSS and wearable load have no ultra-duration validation.
6. No model predicts 50 km to 100-mile trail times with a stated error, and no prediction interval has been calibrated.
7. No study shows that within-athlete changes in free-living drift or decoupling track changes in fitness.
8. The heat ability multiplier, the acclimation pace discount, ultra-specific heat slopes and the cold-side slope are missing.
9. Women and masters runners are under-represented across load, durability, strength and readiness evidence. Nothing covers runners over 50 or perimenopausal runners.
10. No trial shows that adaptive or AI-adjusted running plans (HRV- or wellness-driven) beat fixed plans for performance or injury over a season.
11. No running study reports the injury rate when a runner returns to an earlier weekly level or long run after two to eight weeks of reduced running, and none gives a retraining rate for recreational or masters runners returning to a prior peak. The J7 tiers rest on fitness studies and convention.
12. No time course of tendon or bone capacity exists for runners after a partial reduction in running. The only timings are 23 days of limb suspension and 1 month of detraining in young non-runners [deboer-2007-ulls-tendon-time-course, kubo-2012-achilles-training-detraining] and a bone review's summaries of other sources [warden-2021-bsi-optimal-workload].
13. **S1 Strength progression scheme.** No paper compares linear, double, percentage-based or autoregulated progression in runners or novices. One synthesis in resistance-trained lifters found effort-based and percentage-based prescription similar for 1RM strength [hickmott-2022-autoregulation-meta], and a consensus stand for healthy adults gives the 2-10% step [acsm-2009-progression-models-resistance]. Default (D): double progression inside the repetition range (F8).
14. **S2 Repetitions in reserve.** No paper tests a reps-in-reserve target in runners. The count is imprecise, by about one repetition [halperin-2021-rir-prediction-accuracy], and strength gain did not depend on it over a wide range [robinson-2024-proximity-failure-meta]. Default (D): `f7.heavyRir` in the block, `f7.easyRir` or more in a ramp-in, a taper and a return, and an unrecorded count holds the load.
15. **S3 Minimal effective dose.** No trial of 1 against 2 sessions a week, or of the number of sets, in endurance runners through a racing phase. The evidence is a narrative review of non-athletes [spiering-2021-minimal-dose], a trial in untrained adults [bickel-2011-maintenance-dosing-retention] and a meta-analysis of trained men gaining strength [androulakis-2019-minimum-effective-dose]. Default (D): `e5.strengthMaintenancePerWeek` session a week, `f7.maintenanceSets` sets and `f7.maintenancePlyoContacts` contacts.
16. **S4 Masters runners.** Two small trials, one of master marathon runners aged about 43-45 [piacentini-2013-masters-runners] and one of middle-aged recreational runners [eihara-2024-middle-aged-pt-rt], and 9 master endurance athletes with a mean age of 51.5 years in a 3-week programme measured on a bicycle [louis-2011-master-athletes-strength-cycling]; no runner over about 50 and no woman in the running trials. Default (D): the general prescription, the long end of the ramp-in range from `f5.mastersMinAge`, no age-based cut.
17. **S5 Calf and plantar-flexor dosing.** One correlation (r = -0.477, 20 runners) supports heavy calf raises [eihara-2024-middle-aged-pt-rt]; no paper gives sets, repetitions, load, tempo, knee angle or Achilles tolerance in runners. One 12-week trial in 14 untrained adults compared standing and seated raises for muscle volume [kinoshita-2023-standing-seated-calf-raise], and one acute study in 14 recreational runners found no change in modelled tibial stress after an exhausting calf protocol [rice-2020-calf-raise-tibial-stress]. Default (D): the heavy range and effort of the other lifts, standing and bent-knee raises alternating (a choice the one trial does not back), slow lowering.
18. **S6 Plyometric dose and progression.** 200 contacts is the highest dose observed and not a tested limit [balsalobre-2016-running-economy-highly-trained, llanoslagos-2024-economy-speeds]. A pooled analysis shows that jump training helps endurance runners and gives no dose [ramirezcampillo-2021-jump-training-runners]; landing force was higher for depth drops from 60 and 90 cm and depth jumps from 90 cm than for a vertical jump, and not for a depth jump from 30 cm, in collegiate and national-level power athletes [wallace-2010-plyometric-grf]. No starting dose, progression, surface or bone-stress data. Default (D): `f7.plyoStartContacts` contacts, `f7.plyoStepContacts` more every `f7.plyoStepEveryWeeks` weeks after a completed running week, four low-amplitude movements, `f7.reducedPlyoShare` of the ladder for a pace above `flib.plyoEmphasisPaceKmh` km/h, a bone stress history, a recent injury or a lighter week.
19. **S7 Eccentric dose.** No trial of eccentric-biased or downhill-specific strength work in trail runners (H5). Default (D): lowering over `f7.lowerSeconds` seconds and no loaded eccentric above the concentric.
20. **S8 Strength in the taper.** No trial of resistance training in a taper; the taper meta-analysis concerns endurance training [bosquet-2007-taper-meta-analysis]. Stopping evidence is small and mixed: 8 runners kept an economy gain over 4 weeks without explosive strength work [berryman-2020-strength-cessation-runners], and 14 elite cyclists lost ground against a control group over 8 weeks [ronnestad-2016-strength-cessation-cyclists]. Default (D): one light session a week until the race week, none in the race week.
21. **S9 Women.** Two small randomised trials in women with mixed results [vikmoen-2016-female-heavy-strength-running, vikmoen-2017-female-duathletes-strength], 8 women in a trial of 17 runners [storen-2008-maximal-strength-economy], and no sex-stratified analysis of dose. Default (D): sex never changes the dose (A5, G-4).
22. **S10 100-mile and trail runners.** One 25-person male ultra study [giovanelli-2017-ultra-sep] and a cross-section of 16 finishers [pastor-2022-trail-determinants-distances]; no training trial in 100-mile runners. Default (D): the general prescription, labelled extrapolated.
23. **S11 Repetitions to a one-rep maximum.** No paper validates a repetitions-to-maximum equation in runners or recreational lifters. The repetitions possible at a percentage vary between people and exercises [nuzzo-2023-reps-at-percent-1rm], and a linear equation was advised only to 10 repetitions [reynolds-2006-1rm-prediction]. Default (D): the linear form with the divisor `f8.e1rmDivisor`, labelled an estimate, and no estimate from a set above `f8.e1rmMaxReps` repetitions to the limit (amended 2026-10-25).
24. **S12 Barbell lifting by recreational runners.** No injury or technique data for recreational runners starting barbell lifts; the reviews of injury rates are of competitive lifters [aasa-2016-weightlifter-powerlifter-injuries, keogh-2016-weight-training-injuries]. Default (D): the barbell from the end of the ramp-in for any runner with the equipment (owner decision of 2026-10-24), with the safety line, no maximal attempt and repetitions in reserve.

## What would change these rules

- **An RCT of session-spike capping.** It would turn G-1 into a causal rule or demote it, and it would set the values for H2 and H3.
- **Ultra TID or volume trials, or a validated position stand (for example from Scheer or Millet).** They would replace the grade D ultra values for Z1, time on feet, back-to-backs and taper.
- **Verified Banister source text, or a runner study of sex-specific TRIMP.** It would confirm or remove the only sex term in load.
- **Running studies of downhill and eccentric dose.** They would replace H4 and H5.
- **A cycle-length trial (3:1 vs 2:1), or masters recovery data in runners.** It would replace E3 and J6.
- **A calibrated ultra or trail prediction model, or an ITRA/UTMB index validation.** It would replace L4, L5 and the 50% widening.
- **Ultra 90-120 g/h fuelling RCTs.** They would move the H7 tiers and possibly the default.
- **Season-long trials of wearable-guided adaptation.** They would set the HRV windows and thresholds and could promote readiness beyond downgrade-only.
- **Published per-ability heat slopes and acclimation pace effects.** They would replace I4 and the I8 discount.
- **Running evidence on retraining speed and restart fractions.** It would replace the J7 tiers.
- **A running study of tendon or bone capacity after a partial reduction in running, or of the injury rate when a runner returns to an earlier weekly level.** It would replace the grade D caution that rests on complete-unloading studies in young non-runners (G-2, J7) and could ground a rebuild rate.
- **A study of return to running after illness or a niggle in recreational runners, or of window lengths.** It would replace the J9 counts and the grade D parts of n-3.
- **A randomised return-to-running trial after an ultra (for example, easy running from day 3 versus a 7-10 day hold after 100 km or 100 miles), or day-by-day function data between day 5 and day 16.** It would replace the grade D H12 holds and fractions, and could promote the check-in gate beyond delay-only.
- **A trial of run-walk against short continuous starts in novices, or injury outcomes by starting dose.** It would replace the E6 ladder, the floor sizes and the BMI hold.
- **A study that links long-run length to 5-10 km performance or injury.** It would replace the D7 tiers and the 16 km cap.
- **A validation of heart-rate alerts on a wrist watch during short intervals, or a watch that alerts on a ceiling alone.** It would replace the O1 floor and the 180 s step length.
- **A trial that compares ways of progressing a load, or a reps-in-reserve prescription, in runners.** It would replace the rules and the effort targets of F8.
- **A trial of maintenance and taper doses (sessions and sets) in runners through a racing phase, or of resistance training in a taper.** It would replace the maintenance, lighter-week and taper doses of F7 and F9.
- **A dose-response trial of ground contacts in distance runners, or a cohort of bone stress injury after added plyometrics.** It would replace the start, step, rung length and cap of the jump ladder in F7.
- **A dose-response trial of calf raises in runners with Achilles and calf outcomes, or of eccentric-biased strength work in trail runners.** It would replace the calf dose of F7 and the eccentric emphasis of H5.
- **Strength trials in women, in runners over 50 and in 100-mile runners.** They would replace the extrapolations of F7 and H10.
- **A validation of a repetitions-to-maximum equation in runners or recreational lifters, or a cohort of recreational runners starting barbell lifts.** They would replace the estimated maximum of F8 and the barbell rule of F7.
- **A trial of weekly run days at equal volume in recreational runners over 5 km to the half marathon (three days against four or five), with injury and performance outcomes.** It would replace the grade D cap of P2.
- **A trial of the length of the base phase, or of strides-only base work against hills and threshold in the base, in recreational runners; or Lydiard's 1997 book read for its phase lengths (task S3).** It would replace the build length and the quality-kind table of P4.
- **A registered, randomised trial that compares named methods by a characteristic of the runner (experience, injury history, days free), with the training verified and a race as the outcome; or the data and a correction of Qin 2025.** It would let a preset be called a better plan for some runners, which no study does today (P1 to P4).
